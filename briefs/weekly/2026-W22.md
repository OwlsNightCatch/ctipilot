### CVE-2026-45585 (YellowKey) — BitLocker bypass via WinRE transactional file delete; TPM+PIN is the only hardening until June Patch Tuesday

**If you did nothing this week:** any Windows 11 24H2/25H2/26H1 or Server 2025 endpoint using TPM-only BitLocker (no PIN) is unlockable by physical access. Three public PoC implementations are available.

CVE-2026-45585 is a BitLocker security-feature bypass (CVSS 6.8; physical-access prerequisite) discovered by researcher Chaotic Eclipse. The attack path: from WinRE, using Transactional NTFS (TxF), an attacker deletes `winpeshl.ini`; the WinRE recovery environment then falls back to launching an unrestricted shell (`cmd.exe`) rather than the standard recovery interface, bypassing the TPM-only BitLocker unseal that WinRE performs automatically. This gives full local SYSTEM access to the encrypted volume's decrypted contents without knowing the BitLocker recovery key. Microsoft has published mitigation guidance rather than a patch: remove the `autofstx.exe` entry from `HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\Session Manager\BootExecute` in the WinRE image and re-seal the image; this is an administrative procedure, not an automated rollout. The permanent fix is expected at June 2026 Patch Tuesday (~2026-06-10) ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/05/20/yellowkey-bitlocker-mitigation-cve-2026-45585/); [Microsoft MSRC](https://msrc.microsoft.com/update-guide/en-US/vulnerability/CVE-2026-45585); [daily 2026-05-20](briefs/2026-05-20.md)).

Hardening priority order: (1) Enable TPM+PIN mode (`BitLocker Drive Encryption > Change how drive is unlocked at startup` → set a PIN, or GPO `Interactive logon: Machine account lockout threshold` → enforce startup PIN via MDM/GPO `RequireStartupPIN`). This closes the attack path regardless of patch status. (2) Apply the WinRE BootExecute mitigation on managed estates. (3) Evaluate blocking physical access to WinRE via UEFI boot-order locking.

### Kali365 OAuth device-code PhaaS — FBI PSA260521 confirms active M365 MFA bypass without credential capture

**If you did nothing this week:** Kali365 operators are selling successful M365 sessions to downstream threat actors; the OAuth device-code flow is the entry point; your CAP policies almost certainly allow it unless you have explicitly created Conditional Access policies blocking device-code authentication.

FBI PSA260521 (2026-05-23) describes Kali365, a phishing-as-a-service platform that exploits the OAuth 2.0 device-authorization grant flow (RFC 8628) to capture valid M365 access tokens without ever capturing user credentials or requiring the attacker to intercept the authentication exchange ([FBI PSA260521](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260521); [daily 2026-05-23](briefs/2026-05-23.md)). The attack flow: (1) attacker initiates a device-code request against Microsoft's `devicecode` endpoint and receives a `user_code` valid for 15 minutes; (2) the user_code is delivered to the target via a lure email, Teams message, or LinkedIn InMail; (3) the target authenticates at `microsoft.com/devicelogin` — completing MFA normally because the MFA challenge is genuine; (4) the attacker polls the token endpoint and receives a full `access_token`/`refresh_token` pair with the scopes the app requested. The attacker obtains a persistent delegated token without any credential theft, AiTM proxy, or evilginx infrastructure — MFA passes and the user sees nothing unusual. Kali365 is specifically noted for selling resultant sessions into downstream abuse pipelines targeting M365 mail access, Teams channels, and SharePoint document libraries.

Detection: Microsoft Entra sign-in logs will show `DeviceCode` as the authentication method; alert on DeviceCode authentications from new or unfamiliar device platforms, or from users who have never previously used the device-code flow. Hardening: create a Conditional Access policy that blocks the `DeviceCode` authentication flow for all users except explicit device-enrollment scenarios (e.g., screen-limited kiosk devices); require MFA method: FIDO2 or Certificate-based auth for sensitive roles (Azure AD Global Admin, Exchange Admin, etc.).

### Developer toolchain and software supply chain — commoditisation inflection point

The W22 window marks the point at which the Shai-Hulud supply-chain technique crossed from targeted threat-actor operation to commodity. The combination of open-source Megalodon release, the SLSA Build Level 3 bypass confirmation, and npm's 2FA-gated publishing countermeasure collectively define a new baseline posture for software supply-chain risk. Three observations for defenders:

(1) **Registry-monoculture risk**: npm's 2FA gate is the first meaningful countermeasure, but it addresses only the npm registry. The five major package registries (npm, PyPI, Maven, Cargo, RubyGems) have materially different trust architectures and not all have equivalent controls. Megalodon's registry-agnostic design means wave-6 Cargo or Maven campaigns remain plausible without a new technical adaptation.

(2) **SLSA attestation limitations**: SLSA Build Level 3 verifies that the build system was the attested one, but if the attested runner was itself compromised by a prior OIDC-reuse attack, the resulting attestation is "legitimately compromised" — the attestation is valid but meaningless. The fix is not a higher SLSA level; it is proactive rotation of all OIDC-trusted identities after any workflow compromise ([Unit 42 / StepSecurity](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/monitoring-npm-supply-chain-attacks/)).

(3) **GitHub internal breach scope**: GitHub committed on 2026-05-20 to publishing a fuller post-incident report on the ~3,800 internal-repository exfiltration. That report was still outstanding as of 2026-05-25 — the operational open question is whether any exfiltrated repos contained signing keys, build-system credentials, or customer-impacting material.

### CERT-EU Cyber Brief 26-05 — Finland MDM zero-day, France ANTS 19M identity records, Hungary ministry credential exposure

CERT-EU's April 2026 monthly threat brief (Cyber Brief 26-05, published 4 May 2026) documented three EU government incidents with direct relevance to Swiss public-sector defenders. (1) Finland's Valtori national MDM service was compromised via a zero-day; approximately 50,000 government employees' managed devices were affected and cyberespionage is suspected — the incident is a canonical single-point-of-failure example for centralised government MDM infrastructure. (2) France's ANTS (national identity documents and driving licences agency) disclosed a breach affecting approximately 19 million users, with a minor arrested; the breach exposed names, emails, and birthdates for the majority of France's adult population with a government digital identity. (3) A Bellingcat analysis documented approximately 795 email/password combinations from 12 of 13 Hungarian government ministries circulating in breach databases, with 97 compromised machines identified via infostealer telemetry. ### CERT-EU Threat Landscape Report 2025 — 174 threat actors vs Union entities; internet-facing exploitation dominant for second year; autonomous AI intrusion first documented

CERT-EU's annual Threat Landscape Report 2025 (published 2026-04-08, TLP:CLEAR) covers cyber threats against EU institutions, bodies, offices, and agencies across 2025. ## 8. Policy & regulatory horizon

### EU Russia 20th-package MSS prohibition enters force 25 May 2026; Swiss parallel effective since 22 May

**What changed:** Regulation 833/2014 (as amended by Council Regulation 2026/506) creates a new Article-3p prohibition on providing managed security services to the Russian government, Russian-incorporated entities, and entities whose business is >50% Russia-state-directed. The prohibition entered EU legal force at midnight on 25 May 2026 — today. Switzerland adopted a parallel package under the Embargo Act (EmbA) on 22 May 2026 effective 23:00 local time.

**MSS definition (Regulation 833/2014, Article 3p):** "a service provided to a third party consisting of carrying out, or providing assistance for, activities relating to cybersecurity risk management, such as incident handling, penetration testing, security audits and consulting, including expert advice, related to technical support." The definition is broader than SIEM-as-a-service and explicitly captures IR retainer services, red-team engagements, and security consulting delivered to Russian-domiciled entities.

**Compliance actions for EU and Swiss security-services firms:** All Russia-touching MSS contracts must have been terminated or a national-competent-authority licence obtained before today (EU) or 22 May (CH). The only licence exception under Regulation 833/2014 Art. 3p is services strictly necessary for the functioning of Russian consular/diplomatic representations in EU member states. Any EU-incorporated firm with a Russian subsidiary continuing to provide managed SOC, MDR, IR retainer, or pentesting services to that subsidiary without an NCA licence is in breach as of today.

**Open compliance question:** SECO has not published an FAQ specifically addressing the cybersecurity-services definition as it applies to the Swiss Embargo Act adoption; Swiss firms must currently rely on European Commission guidance and law-firm analysis. No SECO FAQ update was found as of 2026-05-25 ([Greenberg Traurig analysis](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/eus-20th-russia-sanctions-package-key-changes-and-compliance-implications); [EC DG FISMA announcement](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-adopts-20th-package-sanctions-against-russia-2026-04-23_en)).
