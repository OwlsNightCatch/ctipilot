Storm-2949 is not described as financially motivated: Microsoft's stated objective for the campaign is mass data exfiltration ([Microsoft, 2026-05-18](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/05/18/storm-2949-turned-compromised-identity-into-cloud-wide-breach/)), Storm is its temporary designation for unclassified activity, and it observed no extortion and sees no link to ShinyHunters ([BleepingComputer, 2026-05-19](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/microsoft-self-service-password-reset-abused-in-azure-data-theft-attacks/)). The intrusion did not stay free of binaries: on VMs the actor installed ScreenConnect, weakened Defender Antivirus and cleared event logs. SSPR abuse took over four privileged accounts, not one, and Microsoft hedges the social engineering as consistent with SSPR abuse and likely a phishing scheme, giving an IT-support call only as an example ([Microsoft, 2026-05-18](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/05/18/storm-2949-turned-compromised-identity-into-cloud-wide-breach/)).

At Key Vault the actor changed the vault's access configuration and read dozens of secrets, connection strings and identity credentials among them ([Microsoft, 2026-05-18](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/05/18/storm-2949-turned-compromised-identity-into-cloud-wide-breach/)); the Key Vault operation names given earlier for hunting are not in Microsoft's write-up, and Entra ID records SSPR activity in the Self-Service Password Management audit category ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/authentication/howto-sspr-reporting)). Microsoft names no victim sector or country.
