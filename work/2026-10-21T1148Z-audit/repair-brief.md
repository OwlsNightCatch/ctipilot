## Per entry

1. **Read the entry in full and check the current text against the findings.** A defect the text no longer has is declined as "already fixed".
2. **Re-read the ground-truth or cited source for every sentence you write** (the saved body, or a fresh `python3 tools/fetch_source.py extract <URL>` saved under your `src/`). For cisa.gov pages and other walled hosts use `WebFetch` with the outbound-links template from `.claude/agents/cti-research.md`, asking for verbatim quotes. Quote only verbatim: `grep -F` every new `evidence[]` quote against the saved body. Never construct a URL. Cite the specific page, never a homepage, listing, NVD, MITRE, cve.org or CVE Services API page (`check_run.py` FAILs those). A score or workaround that only the CVE record carries cannot be cited. ENISA EUVD per-vulnerability pages (`euvd.enisa.europa.eu/vulnerability/EUVD-...`) are citable and carry the numbering authority's score.
3. **Bind each citation to the clause its page carries.** A clause cited to a page that holds only part of it, or a paraphrase looser than the source, was the most frequent verifier finding in wave 02. When one sentence draws on two pages, split it or cite each half where it stands.
4. **Fix a wrong or imprecise statement where it stands, and sweep it everywhere.** A corrected claim often stands in several places: title, headline, summary, main analysis, labelled lines, an earlier `## <Type> — <at>` section, `actions[]`, `immediate_action`, `cves[]`, `sources[]` dates, `evidence[]`. Fix every occurrence. An edited earlier section must not report events after its own timestamp. Earlier `updates[]` records (their summaries included) are append-only: never edit them. When most of the analysis is unsupported, rewrite it from its sources (`prompts/cti-run.md` Phase 4 § The actionability contract gives the shape: a 3 to 6 sentence narrative with inline citations at the point of claim, then the labelled lines).
5. **Legacy duties, every entry in your batch, clean ones included:**
   - `classification: {reliability, credibility}` (NATO Admiralty) on every kind, `vulnerability` included (this deployment configures no triage scheme, so `org_triage` stays null). Start from the truth pass's `rating` proposal and check its basis against `prompts/cti-run.md` § Intel classification: a vendor advisory plus CERT restatements of it is one assessor (credibility 2).
   - `techniques[]`: every technique the sources support, active ids only. Check each id mechanically: `python3 -c "import json,sys;d=json.load(open('attack/enterprise-attack.json'))['techniques'];[print(t, d.get(t,{}).get('name'), 'REVOKED' if d.get(t,{}).get('revoked') else '', 'DEPRECATED' if d.get(t,{}).get('deprecated') else '', 'UNKNOWN' if t not in d else '') for t in sys.argv[1:]]" T1190`. Never empty on `threat`, `incident` or `vulnerability`; never an access vector the sources do not state. Every mapped behavior is described in plain language in the body, without T-numbers in prose.
   - The labelled lines, each its own paragraph after the narrative, in this order, one to three sentences each, inline-cited: `**Exposure:**`, `**Detection:**` (telemetry class first, plus the post-patch compromise check for an exploited vulnerability), `**Triage:**` only with an honest source-backed benign-lookalike discriminator (omit it otherwise, never invent one), `**Defender takeaway:**` (the decision that changes and its scope condition). The labels replace prose: fold an existing "Detection concepts:" or "Hardening:" sentence into the matching line instead of keeping both, and drop any uncited guidance you cannot source.
   - `affected_products[]` with the vendor's official product names where the entry is product-specific, `entities` keyed for what the entry is about (check names and aliases in `entities/registry.yaml`; a passing mention stays unkeyed).
   - `sourcing_note`, `verification` and `confidence` consistent with the sourcing actually present.
6. **Pipeline self-reference** in reader text goes: latency tags such as "~96 h" in a title or headline, "first coverage in this brief series", "tracked here", "(migrated)". Keep the entry id and path as they are.
7. **Em dashes.** On every entry that receives a record from you, replace the em dashes in reader text (title, headline, summary, sourcing_note, actions, `cves[]` text fields, body) with a comma, colon, semicolon or parentheses. Never touch a `## <Type> — <at>` heading, a verbatim evidence quote or an earlier record summary. No em dash in anything you write.
8. **English.** A non-English quotation in reader text is given in English translation marked "(translated from <language>)"; an `evidence[]` row keeps the verbatim original in `original:` beside an English `quote:`.
9. **Versions.** Put "version" or "release" before a four-part version number (version 5.3.2.0), or the IOC scan reads it as an IPv4 address.
10. **One record per entry for this run**, written with the record tool, after your edits:
    - `python3 tools/entry_record.py diff <entry-id> --run-id 2026-10-21T1148Z-audit` lists the fields you changed; `fields` must name every one, `body` included.
    - `python3 tools/entry_record.py add <entry-id> <correction|improvement> --run-id 2026-10-21T1148Z-audit --fields a,b --summary-file F (--section-file F | --internal)`; `amend` to change your own record afterwards.
