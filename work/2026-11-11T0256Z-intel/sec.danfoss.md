BACS published a public warning on 2026-11-10, its first on the refrigeration campaign outside the Cyber Security Hub advisory this entry already cites; it says the attacks of recent weeks show that Switzerland is affected too and that it is repeating its warning because the systems are widespread among small and medium-sized businesses (translated from German) ([BACS, 2026-11-10](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/warnung-kuehlanlagen-de)). Per BACS, attackers reach management interfaces that are directly reachable from the internet and then change temperatures, defrost cycles, alarm thresholds or notification settings remotely, which can spoil food, medicines and other temperature-sensitive goods; it names the end-of-life AK-SM 800 and AK-SC 355, the current AK-SM 800A and 8xxA family when it runs outdated firmware, and Copeland and Dixell XWEB.

What is new is the patch statement and the wider population: BACS says security updates fixing the known flaws are available for the AK-SM 800A and 8xxA family and that newer firmware activates additional protections and enforces encrypted HTTPS communication (translated from German), and it lists butchers and food producers, restaurants and hotels, pharmacies, doctors' practices and laboratories, logistics and warehouses, industry and farms among the users, many of which do not know their cooling plants are reachable from the internet or run by an external provider ([BACS, 2026-11-10](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/warnung-kuehlanlagen-de)). It recommends identifying the controllers, those run by contractors included, installing vendor updates, restricting unsupported systems to trusted networks or a VPN, reaching them remotely only through one centrally managed access point with default passwords replaced, and replacing end-of-life products; it names no attacker, no exploited CVE and no count of Swiss victims.
