**Microsoft says its recent Microsoft 365 outage was not caused by a cyberattack, but warns that attackers used the disruption to send phishing emails disguised as service notifications.**

The [November 12 outage](https://www.securityweek.com/microsoft-365-outage-disrupts-outlook-exchange-online-and-teams/) disrupted access to Exchange Online mailboxes and Outlook. The tech giant also identified possible effects on services such as Teams, SharePoint Online and Microsoft 365 Copilot. Organizations and government agencies in several countries reported interruptions to email and other operations.

“This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI / agents,” Microsoft said in a [statement](https://x.com/Microsoft/status/2121282013600907325) on Friday.

The company traced the failure to an earlier network capacity upgrade, with a subsequent network change exposing the problem. Microsoft said it fixed the underlying issue and was bringing services back in stages to ensure they remained stable. The Exchange Online incident, tracked as EX1550543, was marked resolved on November 13.

Microsoft Threat Intelligence said it observed a [phishing campaign](https://x.com/MsftSecIntel/status/2121491564161581148) exploiting the disruption. The fake emails posed as Microsoft 365 service health notices and referred to EX1550543, the actual incident identifier.

In some messages detected on November 13, recipients were falsely told that emails held during the outage would be deleted within 24 hours unless they clicked a link to restore them.

The phishing emails’ subject lines began with “Outage -” followed by a six-character code. Sender display names included organization-specific wording followed by “Outage Notification” or “Service Health.”

Microsoft identified three domains used to send the emails: moments-notice\[.\]com, planetmaison\[.\]com and newbooker\[.\]com. Recipient-specific SendGrid tracking links redirected to reallifeaccounting\[.\]com. Similar messages targeted organizations across several US sectors.

“The activity is consistent with credential phishing, although credential collection and malware delivery have not been confirmed,” Microsoft said.

The company subsequently reported that its security products were [blocking the campaign](https://x.com/MsftSecIntel/status/2121728397093032307).

Microsoft recommended that security teams examine suspicious emails and links, investigate user clicks, and review their Safe Links policies. It also advised organizations to verify service incidents directly through the Microsoft 365 admin center.
