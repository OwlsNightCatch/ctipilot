# CIA, ODNI unclassified intelligence system hacked

## A person familiar with the matter confirmed the breach, while a second said the disruption was resolved and did not affect classified systems.

The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence discovered a breach of an unclassified system used to analyze public and commercially available information, according to a person familiar with the matter.

A second person familiar said it disrupted an unclassified network, but did not affect classified systems and that the matter has been resolved.

Both spoke on the condition of anonymity to communicate their knowledge of the hack.

The New York Times [first reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/24/us/politics/spy-agencies-computer-network-breaches.html?smid=nytcore-ios-share) the intrusion Tuesday. It’s unclear who carried it out or if any information was pilfered.

The CIA declined to comment. ODNI did not return a request for comment.

The affected system supports open-source intelligence, or OSINT, which analysts use to understand foreign governments, military activity and other developments through “open source” material such as news reports and social media.

Although that material is unclassified, an intrusion could reveal how spy agencies use it and which subjects are of interest to their analysts, said Ben Bernstein, a manager at cybersecurity firm Huntress.

“If you can see what analysts are searching for and what topics they’re tracking, you get a solid look at U.S. intelligence priorities and blind spots without ever touching a classified file,” he said.

“Depending on what was exposed, an adversary could learn not only what our intelligence agencies know, but also where their investigative priorities lie and where gaps in their intelligence may exist,” added Aleksandr Yampolskiy, co-founder and CEO at SecurityScorecard.

ARTEX — an AI tool developed by a Chinese cybersecurity engineer known as “Autumn-27” that automatically finds and breaks into computer systems and has recently become a favorite tool of criminal hackers — is believed to have been used in the breach.

Hackers recently used ARTEX to assist in an intrusion into the Defense Department’s Cyber Defense Command, or DCDC, two people familiar with the matter say, though one of them added that the breach was low-impact. It’s unclear if the intrusion into the command coincided directly with the hacks involving the CIA and ODNI.

The Times also reported the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency was concurrently affected with CIA and ODNI.

Spokespeople for NGA and DCDC did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The disclosure also follows several recent intrusions into federal systems. President Donald Trump and other officials [recently acknowledged a hack](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/trump-acknowledges-fbi-crime-database-hack-scope-remains-unclear/417416/) of the FBI’s National Crime Information Center. At the Agriculture Department, officials recently took multiple applications offline [following a security incident](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/usda-grapples-security-breach-it-staff-take-multiple-systems-offline/417350/), disrupting financial tools used to support payments to farmers, *Nextgov/FCW* and *Government Executive* first reported last week.

Government agencies and businesses have also faced a [series of technology outages](https://www.nextgov.com/modernization/2026/11/recent-tech-outages-raise-concerns-over-how-agencies-businesses-maintain-critical-systems/417424/) that administration officials attributed to maintenance and technical problems. The National Security Agency [resumed normal operations Friday](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/outage-nsas-classified-communications-network-appears-fixed-people-familiar-say/417373/) after a three-day disruption to its classified communications network, which Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton attributed to software patching.

“Calling this ‘unclassified’ doesn’t make it harmless, though,” Bernstein said of the CIA and ODNI incident.
