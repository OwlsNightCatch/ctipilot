ReversingLabs correlated ESET's published loader samples and says the same files carry different vendor names, CerealLoader at ESET, BlankPage in Microsoft Defender and CerealMood in other engines, so a hunt on a single family name can miss matches; it says the work extends the artifacts defenders can search for and establishes no additional victims or initial-access method ([ReversingLabs, 2026-11-19](https://www.reversinglabs.com/blog/inside-turlas-cereal-12-exchange-loaders-older-traces-and-new-ways-to-hunt)). Its hunting guidance adds three checks to the Detection line: list live HTTP.sys listeners with `netsh http show servicestate` and read reverse-proxy, load-balancer and WAF logs for the reported CerealNeuron paths, because IIS site logs alone may not show requests served by an independent HTTP.sys listener; search the Exchange and Windows service directories for unexpected managed DLLs named version.dll, WTSAPI32.dll, SensApi.dll or dbghelp.dll; and review module loads in WmiPrvSE.exe as well as EdgeTransport.exe ([ReversingLabs, 2026-11-19](https://www.reversinglabs.com/blog/inside-turlas-cereal-12-exchange-loaders-older-traces-and-new-ways-to-hunt)).
