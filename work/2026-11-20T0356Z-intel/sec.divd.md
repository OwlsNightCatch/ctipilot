DIVD closed its forensic investigation on 2026-11-18. It says that after obtaining root the attacker used AI agents to continue the attack, which it infers from the volume of payloads and the comments in the attack scripts, and that the attacker was mainly interested in credentials, all of which have been rotated ([DIVD, 2026-11-18](https://csirt.divd.nl/cases/DIVD-2026-00014/)). The credential material it touched was a GitLab personal access token that could not reach the internal GitLab, a Slack API key without read permission and the OAuth tokens of two shared mailboxes, used to send test emails with no mailbox-content compromise observed; only the host carrying Zammad was compromised at root level, and because direct outbound traffic from it was impossible the extraction was selective, about 41,000 ticket titles and a limited set of older conversations, 207 volunteer email addresses, two phone numbers and seven profile pictures ([DIVD, 2026-11-18](https://csirt.divd.nl/cases/DIVD-2026-00014/overview_data_investigation/)). DIVD's own lesson is response time: its monitoring alert arrived quickly, but it took 19 hours to initiate the first response ([DIVD, 2026-11-18](https://csirt.divd.nl/cases/DIVD-2026-00014/)).
