### DRIVING THE DAY

**EXCLUSIVE — HUGE CYBERATTACK HITS THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION:** The incident has potentially compromised swaths of personal data, left staff unable to input salary and leave information and prompted the most senior officials to discuss the crisis at their weekly meeting yesterday.

**“The Commission believes it has contained most of the attack**, but the full extent of any damage remains unclear,” an EU official, granted anonymity because of the sensitivity of the issue, told Playbook. “The systems may be coming back online. The answers are taking a little longer.”

**Weapon of choice:** The EU institutions’ cybersecurity watchdog, CERT-EU, detected earlier this month that international hackers used ARTEX, a publicly available AI-powered tool designed to identify vulnerabilities in IT systems. “Some actors misuse such tools and have targeted the Commission,” the EU official added. As a precaution, several applications were temporarily taken offline. Access to most has now been restored.

**“The European Commission has taken the necessary steps** in response to a detected cyber campaign,” Commission spokesperson **Thomas Regnier** told Playbook. “Individual systems were targeted and, in line with our standard practice, we have informed those concerned.”

**Investigators are still trying to establish** whether staff data was compromised, according to the first EU official. The official said the uncertainty explains the limited communication to staff — because the Commission doesn’t yet know whether, or to what extent, personal information was hacked.

**Disruption has lasted over 10 days**. Sysper, the Commission’s HR platform for payslips, leave requests and timesheets, and MyPMO, its medical reimbursement system, were restored earlier this week — though Sysper briefly crashed again yesterday. Translation services were also hit on Tuesday, prompting one particularly inconvenienced official to admit to “swearing a lot.”

**All the way to the top:** The Commission convened its highest-level internal crisis-management body, the Corporate Management Board, for an urgent meeting over these “ongoing cyber incidents” earlier this month, according to a leaked internal mail seen by Playbook. The digital services department ordered stepped-up security checks. And tech commissioner **Henna Virkkunen** briefed counterparts at yesterday’s College of Commissioners meeting on the immediate response, two officials said.

**This is the latest in a series of high-profile global cyberattacks** or outages in recent days, including disruptions affecting Microsoft, large U.S. banks and America’s National Security Agency, as well as a hack that targeted popular websites such as those of Air France, the BBC and Zoom.

**But did you get paid?** Playbook asked around, and the answer was largely yes. While salaries landed as usual, some officials couldn’t submit timesheets or request leave. Three officials also complained about the lack of communication. In a reply to an issue with Sysper seen by Playbook, IT cited a general issue affecting the system’s functionality — without mentioning a cyberattack. ### AFTER THE WHO ON UKRAINE COMES THE WHAT

**EUROPE SEEKS SPECIFICS:** We have an answer to the question “Who represents Europe in talks on Ukraine?” We don’t yet have answers to the questions “What will that look like?” and “Will it be effective?”

**Double events:** After the three-way talks between **Donald Trum**p, **Vladimir Putin** and **Xi Jinping** — and as the Coalition of the Willing meets in Berlin today — Europe is trying to decide what its negotiators can and cannot offer if they get into a room with Russia.

**The who:** EU leaders last week settled on a team made up of officials from five major countries plus top officials from NATO, the European Commission and Council.

**The what:** But agreeing on which line the European team should take is proving divisive. Some countries, led by Austria, are advocating a looser approach involving the likes of limited or partial ceasefires to pave the way for a wider truce, or offering to set up an international forum to monitor any peace agreement, [Nick and Zoya report](https://www.politico.eu/article/europe-argued-over-who-would-talk-to-russia-agreeing-what-to-say-is-even-tougher/).

**Others disagree with that approach:** “We have to agree on the ceasefire and, when the guns go silent, then we negotiate on the long-lasting peace,” Lithuanian Foreign Minister **Kęstutis Budrys** said.

**Unwilling?** Things are no more settled in the Coalition of the Willing, the alliance of more than 35 nations supporting Kyiv. As **Tim Ross** and **Esther Webber** write, Ukrainian President **Volodymyr Zelenskyy** will [find himself among a motley assembly of countries](https://www.politico.eu/article/inside-ukraines-coalition-of-the-wobbling/), convened by two weak European leaders (**Emmanuel Macron** and **Friedrich Merz**) and a rookie without a mandate (Andy Burnham).

**Budget protection:** Merz, with an eye on the future, is [pushing to make the EU’s long-term budget “Le Pen-proof.”](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-presses-to-make-ukraine-aid-marine-le-pen-proof/) Germany wants to lock into the MFF a durable funding scheme for Ukraine that will be difficult for potential opponents to veto, **James Angelos** and **Gregorio Sorgi** write.

### LAWMAKERS RAISE CHINA CONCERNS

**BRUSSELS FEELS A CHINA CHILL:** European Parliament President **Roberta Metsola** contacted Chinese authorities after MEPs reported pressure from Beijing, [her spokesperson **Jüri Laas** said](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-lawmakers-accuse-chinese-officials-of-intimidation-over-taiwan/).

**Piling up:** Seven MEPs told Max Griera and **Carlo Martuscelli** about what they described as intimidation inside Parliament offices as well as detention at the Chinese border and threatening letters, mostly concerning engagement with Taiwan.

**“I have never encountered such behavior from foreign diplomats,”** said **Engin Eroglu**, chair of Parliament’s delegation to China. The Chinese mission to the EU did not respond to a request for comment.

**No grace period:** The incidents come just over a year after Parliament and Beijing [ended a four-year freeze in relations](https://www.politico.eu/article/first-chinese-delegation-7-years-visit-european-parliament/) over China’s sanctions on lawmakers who criticized China’s human rights record.

**Surprise visit:** Patriots for Europe Vice Chair **António Tânger Corrêa** said a senior Chinese diplomat visited his office unannounced before a trip to Taiwan, warning that they “could not ensure” his safety. And lawmakers who attended a Taipei event received letters, [seen by POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/09/Chinas-EU-Mission-Letter-to-Ondrej-Krutilek.pdf), urging them to “refrain from any official exchange” with Taiwan.

**Detained:** Czech MEP **Markéta Gregorová** said she was held for 1.5 hours while entering China, had her passport confiscated and was forbidden from using her phone, in what she said could be a “deliberate intimidation tactic.” Gregorová demanded an explanation from Beijing and asked Metsola to bar Chinese representatives from Parliament if she doesn’t get one.

### BRUSSELS CORNER

**WEATHER**: Light showers. High of 11C.

**A MODEL DELIVERY DELAY:** Remember the tactile miniature of the Europa building I was so excited about in [yesterday’s Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/brussels-new-line-to-the-middle-east/)? Well, the unveiling was canceled just an hour beforehand, “for reasons beyond the control of the Council of the European Union and the ONCE Social Group,” a note from the organizers read. The problem? The model itself never arrived.

**Costa’s Christmas spirit:** European Council President **António Costa** [still met](https://x.com/eucopresident/status/2123035657899561395) the ONCE Foundation’s **Miguel Carballeda**, joking in an Instagram reel that in Spain presents arrive on Three Kings Day on Jan. 6, so there’s still time for his gift.

**Mystery solved:** “The delivery company did not deliver on time. Which I am sure is relatable to many of us in Brussels,” a person familiar with the arrangements told Playbook, granted anonymity to avoid trouble with future parcels. The model is stuck at a logistics center but should eventually reach the Council for visitors to enjoy. Sooner or later.

**CONVERSION THERAPY CONTROVERSY:** Looking for a heated debate? Head to the European Parliament at **11:30 a.m.**, where an event on freedom of speech and conscience is raising eyebrows over its focus on so-called conversion practices aimed at changing or suppressing someone’s sexual orientation or gender identity.

**Freedom for whom?** Conservative MEP **Paolo Inselvini** defended the event to Playbook, arguing that while violence and coercion must be condemned, “we cannot equate abuse with the right of parents to bring up their children, of a priest to offer spiritual guidance.” Renew MEP **Raquel García Hermida-van der Walle** disagreed: “Blergh. Forcing people to give up their sexuality, often under severe pressure, is quite the opposite of freedom.” She added: “Love is not a disease.”

**Brussels draws a line:** A spokesperson for Equality Commissioner **Hadja Lahbib** declined to comment on the event itself but pointed to her previous call: “Ban conversion practices, now.” Parliament, meanwhile, stressed that the gathering is organized by an MEP, not the institution itself.

**DON’T WASTE THIS REMINDER:** The blue bag is for packaging, not your old sweater. Brussels residents tossed nearly 4,000 tonnes of the wrong stuff into blue bags in 2025, costing the capital almost €4 million. The usual suspects? Toys, clothes and e-cigarettes. Bruxelles-Propreté and Fost Plus are [launching a campaign](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/milieu/verkeerd-gesorteerde-pmd-uit-brussel-kost-de-overheid-jaarlijks-bijna-4-miljoen-euro-2026-11-18) to sort things out. Literally.
