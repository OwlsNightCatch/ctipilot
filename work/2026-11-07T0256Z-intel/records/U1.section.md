NCSC Switzerland edited its advisory on 2026-11-06 with the reason "added Swiss incidents, Copeland/Dixell XWEB and healthcare attacks" and states that "The public information reviewed does not establish a common attack method across these incidents or confirm exploitation of the specific Danfoss CVEs listed above"; it lists CVE-2025-41451, CVE-2025-41450 and CVE-2025-41452 as "alleged as potentially related" ([NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13](https://security-hub.ncsc.admin.ch/#/posts/13054)). Its checklist asks operators to review refrigeration management systems across all operations, "including equipment managed by external service providers"; to remove direct internet access to management interfaces and allow remote access only through a secure VPN or protected network; to check temperature settings, defrost schedules, alarm thresholds, notification recipients and user accounts, verify actual temperatures and test alarm delivery; and to apply current firmware and plan to replace equipment that no longer gets security updates ([NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13](https://security-hub.ncsc.admin.ch/#/posts/13054)). For a suspected compromise it says "A factory reset alone is insufficient if the system remains vulnerable and directly accessible from the internet" ([NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13](https://security-hub.ncsc.admin.ch/#/posts/13054)).

Ransom-ISAC published the event log of a Copeland XWEB Pro controller, reportedly on firmware 1.9.0 and reachable through a public address, compromised on 2 November: two Administrator sessions from different external addresses read parameters across 31 devices, then made 588 writes across 26 devices in about six minutes and 155 further writes across five cold rooms, which Ransom-ISAC reads as scripted or bulk programming rather than manual edits, moving setpoints to 110 and 150 degrees C and recording keypad-lock, alarm-mute and defrost commands ([Ransom-ISAC, 2026-11-06](https://ransom-isac.org/blog/the-copeland-incident/)). The post says the controller still used its default Administrator credentials, publishes no evidence for that statement, says the log cannot show how the attackers got in or what temperatures were reached, and says it has no evidence linking the case to NoName057(16), Z-Pentest Alliance or the French attacks ([Ransom-ISAC, 2026-11-06](https://ransom-isac.org/blog/the-copeland-incident/)). Its suggested alerts are more than about 20 parameter-write events a minute from one account, writes to more than three devices within five minutes, and keypad-lock, alarm-mute or defrost commands that do not come from the scheduler ([Ransom-ISAC, 2026-11-06](https://ransom-isac.org/blog/the-copeland-incident/)).

Unit 42 counts over 5,000 internet-exposed devices from the manufacturers CERT-FR names on 5 and 6 November, with 1,853 in the first half of October, 558 in the second half and 316 in the first six days of November, and says the claims of NoName057(16) and Z-Pentest Alliance remain unverified ([Unit 42, 2026-11-06](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/europes-refrigeration-systems-attacks/)). The counts do not show which versions are vulnerable.
