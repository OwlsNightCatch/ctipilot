Previdian's page for CVE-2026-123158 says its sensors observed 14 exploitation attempts from 9 addresses in 8 countries, first and last observed on 2026-11-06 with the first at 00:05 UTC, and that these observations do not show that a target was vulnerable or that an attempt succeeded ([Previdian, 2026-11-06](https://previdian.com/CVE-2026-123158)). The Hacker News, quoting a statement from Previdian's chief executive, reports that Previdian confirmed successful root access on a honeypot running Alpine Linux and OpenSSH, where the attacker used oversized passwords to bypass authentication and ran the reconnaissance commands id, uname and hostname in non-interactive SSH sessions, and that the first attempt came less than nine hours after the CVE was published ([The Hacker News, 2026-11-06](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploited-openssh-sshole-flaw.html)); the honeypot detail is not on Previdian's page. Previdian's page lists the flaw as not in CISA's catalog ([Previdian, 2026-11-06](https://previdian.com/CVE-2026-123158)).

Alpine's advisory adds that updating the package does not restart a running sshd, so sshd must be restarted, and that on exposed machines the administrator should review doas use and recently created users, files and authorized keys, treating the machine as compromised if a login cannot be ruled out ([Alpine Linux, 2026-11-05](https://alpinelinux.org/posts/2026-11-05-openssh-password-authentication-bypass/)). No vendor or authority source reports attacks on production systems.
