# Ransomware group Killsec busted: teenager named as main suspect

Hamburg law enforcement led “Operation Killswitch”, resulting in three arrests and eight searches.

International investigators, led by the Public Prosecutor’s Office and the Hamburg State Criminal Police Office, have taken action against a cybercrime group operating under the alias “Killsec” that has carried out around 1,000 attacks worldwide. At least seventy of these were in Germany. In the raid against the group, investigators saved 110 terabytes of data and searched eight locations in four countries: Greece, Romania, Spain, and the United Kingdom. Three suspects were temporarily arrested. Among the suspects are a sixteen-year-old and an eighteen-year-old.

Killsec has been active since around 2024 and operated a typical “Ransomware as a Service” business. The criminals infiltrated corporate networks through security vulnerabilities, often in cloud storage, copied data, and extorted their victims by threatening publication (“Hack and leak”). In some cases, according to the investigators in their [press release](https://www.europol.europa.eu/media-press/newsroom/news/teenager-suspected-of-leading-killsec-ransomware-group-law-enforcement-seizes-servers-and-leak-site), they extorted “significant ransom payments”.

### Servers seized, leak site defaced

In addition to the eight house searches, investigators seized various items (including two [Flipper Zeros](https://heise.de/news/c-t-3003-Hacking-Geraet-Flipper-Zero-im-Hands-on-7187241.html?from-en=1)) as well as five servers for managing the ransomware program and storing the stolen data. This is typical for cybercrime raids: on the Onion address of the darknet leak site (DLS) taken over by the investigators, there is now a warning notice. In addition, on a landing page set up specifically for “[Operation Killswitch](https://www.operation-killswitch.com/)”, a cliché-ridden AI video flickers across the screen.
