### A.4 Contradictions and nuances against findings.S2.yaml item 1

- No factual contradiction with the CCB text. Every CCB-derived statement in the S2 summary (no victim count, sector, actor, chain or date range; the seven steps in order; the 'Misconfiguration' typing; the warning against blind switch-off) matches the page. Absence checks run on the saved body (grep -i): the words victim, sector, 'how many' and 'number of' do not occur, 'actor' occurs only inside 'factor', and OTP, intercept and SIM occur only in the generic sentence quoted above.
- UI label: the S2 summary and evidence use 'Use for sign-in' only. That is the CCB's wording and the Message Center's (MC1465763). The Microsoft Learn SMS sign-in how-to, page dated 2026-10-30, calls the same checkbox 'Enable first factor sign-in'. Both labels exist in Microsoft's own documentation; an entry that tells readers where to click should name both.
- The CCB sentence about the retirement ('Microsoft will stop delivering SMS and voice codes in Entra ID') omits what Microsoft's page states: it is Microsoft-provided delivery that retires, and a customer-configured telephony provider (Soprano, Telesign in private preview, selectable from 2026-10-30) keeps SMS and voice working. The S2 summary already carries the provider fact from Microsoft; the CCB's text alone would read as a full removal.
- Additions the S2 summary did not carry, all from Microsoft: (1) Microsoft is auto-clearing the first-factor control in tenants with no successful first-factor SMS sign-in in the previous 30 days, rollout mid-September to end of November 2026 (MC1465763, revised 2026-10-28), so a tenant that still has the control enabled is, from Microsoft's criteria, one that either uses it or has not yet been evaluated (an inference from the criteria, stated by no source as an explanation of the Belgian attacks); (2) a temporary tenant opt-out from the passkey auto-enablement exists until 2027-02-01 and does not delay enforcement; (3) Microsoft's how-to says to enable SMS sign-in only for frontline workers; (4) Free-tenant first-factor SMS was retired on 2026-08-11 (MC1448374).
- Date: the S2 `lookback: true` is right; the CCB page carries a date but no time, and 2026-11-10 is outside the 26 h window from 2026-11-12T03:50Z.

### C.3 Contradictions and nuances against findings.S3.yaml item 4

- ATT&CK mapping: Microsoft's own technique list for the post contains T1190, T1059.004, T1053.003, T1543.002, T1036, T1564.001, T1222.002, T1070.006, T1070.004, T1105 and T1090.002. T1068 and T1070.003 are mechanical mappings from stated behaviour, so they are defensible, but they are the composer's mappings, not Microsoft's.
- Staging location: S3 says the client is staged 'in a hidden directory under /dev/shm (or /tmp)'. Microsoft's chain step names writable or memory-backed paths such as /dev/shm; the hidden staging directory is under /dev/shm in the technique detail, and a hidden file under /tmp appears among the detections. Whether /tmp is a staging location versus a detection location is not clear from the prose.
- Dirty Frag: S3 correctly says 'attempts' and 'in a few incidents'. The post gives no patched versions, affected distributions or success statement for CVE-2026-43284 and points to a separate FORGE Lab post for kernel context; an entry should not state a fix status for it from this source.
- Actors: S3 says 'multiple unrelated actors'. Microsoft's wording is 'multiple unrelated threat actors have adopted it' (an explanatory sentence) and 'threat actors' for the incidents; no cluster designation, no named actor and no per-incident actor split exists in the post. Absence check (grep -i) on the saved body for Storm-, UNC, APT and 'attribut' found only the word 'attributable' in the statistics sentence.
- Corroboration: S3 notes 'no independent corroboration found'. The three references on the post (SANS ISC, Sansec, Elastic) independently document GSocket persistence behaviour (see C.2) but none corroborates the 2026 telemetry, so the entry remains single-source for Microsoft's figures.

- S3 summary matches the page on: 356.2% rise (non-monotonic), 87.1% web applications and hosting, the 63.9% and 24.3% sub-shares, 30.7% education and research, 22.9% technology and telecommunications, Microsoft's no-deliberate-targeting caveat, the shared-secret relay model, and the delivery channels for the secret.

### C.4 Benign-lookalike discriminator (only where the source gives the basis)
