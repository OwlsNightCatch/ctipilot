- Both advisories are **V1.0 of 2026-11-10** with CSAF tracking status **interim**; CVSS 3.1 9.8 (vector AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:H/I:H/A:H) and CVSS 4.0 9.2; CWE-787. Siemens describes the flaw as an out-of-bounds write in the Brotli decoder (c/dec/decode.c, Brotli 1.0.3 through 1.2.0) reachable by a remote unauthenticated attacker with a crafted Brotli stream, via the Brotli decoder bundled with Node.js (Node.js update of 2026-10-21).
- **SSA-242944 (industrial products):** 15 affected, 1 not affected (gDAP, component not present). Fixes exist for **SIMATIC WinCC OA V3.20 below V3.20 P17.1 (update to V3.20 P17.1)** and **SIMATIC BRAUMAT / SISTAR >= V8.2 Update 1 < V8.2 Update 5 (update to V8.2 Update 5)**; **no fix planned** for the SIMATIC Profiling Tool; **no fix available** for WinCC V7/V8, WinCC Unified PC Runtime / for Industrial Edge / SIQENCE, WinCC Runtime Professional, WinCC Companion, WinCC OA V3.21, TIA Administrator, User Management Component (UMC), COMOS Walkinside and Project Server Web Administration Tool. Interim control for all but WinCC OA V3.20: disable Brotli compression and decompression at the application or proxy layer; UMC and TIA Administrator additionally get an IIS request-filtering rule (Content-Encoding header, deny string `br`); for TIA Administrator it applies "If UMC authentication is enabled".
- **SSA-678870 (software products):** 28 affected, all versions, **"Currently no fix is available" for all 28**: Teamcenter V2412/V2506/V2512/V2606 (manual mitigation in KB PL8869204), the Opcenter family and Process Preparation X (IIS reverse-proxy mitigation in KB PL8869211; 13 products), HyperLynx, PADS Pro Essentials / Student Edition / Professional and Xpedition Designer / Enterprise (disable and firewall-block Brotli), and Innexis Architecture Native Acceleration 2026, Insights Hub, PAVE360 Automotive 2026, Simcenter Executable Digital Twin and Simcenter Studio (no mitigation text). **Not affected (8):** Polarion (vulnerable code not present), Solid Edge, Calibre, Calibre ICE, Capital, Designcenter X Essentials, Pathway to Learning Engineering, Teamcenter Share (Node.js not present).
- **Exploitation:** neither advisory nor CSAF says anything about exploitation (silence). The only exploitation source is CISA KEV (CVE-2026-111497 added 2026-10-27, due 2026-11-10, ransomware use Unknown).

### B.4 Contradictions between the primaries and the earlier S1 finding
1. **Polarion is not affected (S1 title, affected_products, summary).** S1 listed Siemens Polarion as an affected product and its item title says the two advisories extend the flaw to Polarion. SSA-678870 lists Polarion under 'Known Not Affected Products' (vulnerable code not present: 'Polarion uses node.js 10.15.0 - Brotli was introduced to node.js in 10.16.0'). Do not name Polarion as affected.
2. **S1 summary names Solid Edge, Calibre ICE and Capital among the 28 affected software products.** SSA-678870 lists all three as not affected (Node.js not present), together with Calibre, Designcenter X Essentials, Pathway to Learning Engineering and Teamcenter Share. The 28 affected are exactly: HyperLynx, Innexis Architecture Native Acceleration 2026, Insights Hub, Opcenter Connect Electronics, Opcenter Connect MOM, Opcenter Execution Core, Discrete, Foundation, Pharma and Process, Opcenter Intelligence, Opcenter Intra Plant Logistics, Opcenter Quality, Opcenter RDnL, Opcenter X, PADS Pro Essentials, PADS Pro Student Edition, PADS Professional, PAVE360 Automotive 2026, Process Preparation X, Simcenter Executable Digital Twin, Simcenter Studio, Teamcenter V2412, V2506, V2512 and V2606, Xpedition Designer and Xpedition Enterprise (table in the derived section).

- **Publica (own page, section "Stand: 10.11.2026", headed "Richtigstellung zu Medienberichten über Lösegeldforderungen"):** the federal bodies know of no concrete ransom demands; "der betroffene Softwarelieferant erhielt nach aktuellem Kenntnisstand unmittelbar nach dem Angriff allgemeine Erpresserschreiben", never made concrete with an amount or payment deadline; Publica is in no contact with the attacker group and received no demand; it does not know whether organisations or individuals are being extorted (matter for the Federal Prosecutor's Office); no known case of persons demonstrably contacted or harmed; no sign of darkweb publication. A newer section "Stand: 11.11.2026" says only that the analyses continue. The page does not name Akira; Publica's own newsletter of 2026-10-22 (PDF) does say "Die Hackergruppe Akira wird mit dem Angriff in Verbindung gebracht" and its ransom-demand FAQ paragraph is the same text that still sits on the web page under the correction.
- **PK Softech (own page, 2026-11-10):** "keine konkreten Lösegeldforderungen eingegangen"; never contacted the attackers. It does not mention extortion letters, Akira or a chat.
- **watson.ch (Daniel Schurter, published 2026-11-11 16:48 +01:00, modified 18:42):** reports that on enquiry PK Softech confirms it ignores the prompt Akira left in its network to contact it via an anonymous chat and does not want to negotiate; reads the Akira pattern (no sum in the first note, a code and a darknet negotiation-chat link) from a 2025 FBI/CISA bulletin it cites (not checked here); infers that the firm and the Confederation knew early that Akira was behind it and that crisis communication left out the general extortion note; reads the denials as "nobody clicked the darknet link". The ISG/EDÖB paragraph is watson's legal framing, not verified.
- **SRF (published 2026-11-10 05:58 +01:00, no correction note on the page):** "Die Täter forderten nach ihren Angriffen Lösegeld" and "Sie drohten, die Daten zu veröffentlichen oder weiterzuverkaufen", VBS knows, "mehrere Quellen"; PK Softech and Publica deny receipt, a Dreamlab consultant infers the demand went to the Confederation. cash.ch/AWP relays this with Sepos' statement (no concrete demand to the federal bodies, nothing paid).
- Net: a *general* extortion letter to the supplier is now first-hand confirmed by Publica (current knowledge); a *concrete* demand, the threat to publish/resell and the Akira sender remain press-sourced (SRF, Tamedia, watson); PK Softech's own page is narrower than Publica's.

## F. Certighost (CVE-2026-54121) and the Citrus ransomware report — update to `2026-07-25/certighost-cve-2026-54121-ad-cs-dc-impersonation-poc` (S3 item 3)

### F.1 Bottom line as read on 2026-11-12 (about 04:08Z)
- **Kaspersky (Securelist, authors Larinsky, Vlasov and Trefilov, 2026-11-11):** Citrus, a previously undocumented group (Sn00py RaaS ransomware, Go, RSA-2048 + ChaCha20, `.citrus`), gets in through credentials stolen from employees of compromised contractors, used on corporate VPNs or internet-facing terminal servers over RDP. "In every case we know of" the contractor domain account had very limited privileges, so the attackers "attempted to exploit" Certighost: the attempts created machine accounts named GHOST plus eight random letters (script from a public GitHub exploit) and Machine-template certificate requests whose `Attributes` carry `cdc:` (the system the CA must contact) and `rmd:` and whose SAN names a domain controller. **Kaspersky never says an attempt succeeded**; its text is conditional ("If exploitation succeeds, the certification authority issues a certificate with the specified parameters, based on the specified template.").
- **Rest of the chain as Kaspersky states it:** LLM-written commands/scripts; Impacket atexec scheduled tasks; mimikatz, Snaffler, fscan, ADRecon, SharpHound; CVE-2020-1337 (Metasploit-repo exploit); insecure 1C:Enterprise servers; Telegram `tdata` archiving from a domain controller; reverse VLESS tunnels through Xray on port 443 (REALITY); AnyDesk; log clearing and AV disabling incl. via the central security console; encryption deployed by a malicious GPO ("Kaspersky GPO", created with Import-GPO/New-GPLink from a script on SYSVOL, drops the archive, creates a scheduled task, enables WinRM, disables Volume Shadow Copy and Defender), domain controllers on the ransomware's host blacklist; ransom note with a 72-hour deadline.
- **Victims' geography:** Kaspersky says only that Citrus and Toy Ghouls are Russian-speaking and "attack Russian companies only"; no victim count, sector or date range is given; same infrastructure compromised by both groups in several incidents; no direct infrastructure overlap.
- **Microsoft (Defender blog, dated 2026-07-31):** update of 2026-07-14 fixes it; exposure needs all of: Enterprise CA integrated with AD, machine template, default MAQ or an attacker-controlled machine account, CA-to-attacker SMB/LDAP reachability, a valid domain account; the chase fallback occurs only for templates that require a DNS name and only on CAs with the EDITF_ENABLECHASECLIENTDC flag enabled (Microsoft's wording); PoC public 2026-07-24; "Microsoft has not observed confirmed threat-actor exploitation, and the activity seen so far appears consistent with security testing"; AD CS auditing is off by default (Certification Services subcategory + CA AuditFilter 127; events 4886/4887); Defender for Identity alert "Potential Certighost (CVE-2026-54121) AD CS abuse". The MSRC record (API, read today) is unrevised since 2026-07-14: exploited No, publicly disclosed No, Exploitation Less Likely, CVSS 3.1 8.8.
- **CISA:** CVE-2026-54121 is not in the KEV catalogue read today (released 2026-11-10T19:03Z, 1779 entries). **No source says the flaw was exploited successfully.** Kaspersky's report is the single source for the attempts.

### F.3 Contradictions between the primaries and the earlier S3 finding
1. **S3: 'Kaspersky does not say whether any attempt succeeded'.** Confirmed. The only wording is 'attempted to exploit' and the conditional 'If exploitation succeeds, the certification authority issues a certificate with the specified parameters, based on the specified template.' The artefacts Kaspersky names are the attempts' leftovers (GHOST-prefixed machine accounts, Machine-template requests with a cdc attribute); it names no issued certificate, no PKINIT logon and no DCSync. Do not write that Certighost was 'used', 'succeeded' or 'exploited successfully'; write 'attempted'.
2. **S3: 'Certighost attempted in real intrusions'.** Supported by Kaspersky's own telemetry, single source. No second source (Microsoft, CISA, another lab) states it; the one open-web search I ran found none. Microsoft's Defender post (2026-07-31) and the MSRC record ('Exploited: No', 'Exploitation Less Likely', last revised 2026-07-14) have not been revised and say nothing about it. CISA's KEV has no entry.
3. **S3: 'Kaspersky's own scope is Russian victims only'.** Precisely: Kaspersky writes that both groups (Citrus and Toy Ghouls) are Russian-speaking and 'attack Russian companies only'. The report names no victim count, sector, date range or country; the geography statement is the only one (assessment, not stated by Kaspersky: a telemetry vendor's view is bounded by its own customer base).
4. **S3 attributes the report to 'Kaspersky Securelist (GReAT)'; the page names three authors (Stanislav Larinsky, Vladislav Vlasov, Boris Trefilov) and no team.** Write 'Kaspersky researchers' unless GReAT is confirmed elsewhere.
5. **S3: 'initial access through contractor VPN and RDP credentials'.** Confirmed ('credentials stolen from these contractors' employees' used against corporate VPNs or internet-facing terminal servers over RDP).
6. **S3 tooling list.** All items confirmed against the saved body except two points of wording: the 'Telegram Desktop tdata archives uploaded to an external web service' are archives created by steal_tgs.ps1 on a domain controller and uploaded through Chrome (not stated to be used for the leak threat); the 72-hour note (S3 did not carry it) is in the ransom text, which also threatens dumps of services, employee personal data, accounting, contracts and source code.

Microsoft's 'affects Windows Server 2012 through Windows Server 2025' is a product range, not an exposure statement; exposure needs all conditions.
