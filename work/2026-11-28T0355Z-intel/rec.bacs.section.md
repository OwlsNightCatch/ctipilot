BACS director Florian Schütz told SRF on 2026-11-26 that BACS currently has five confirmed cases of attacks with AI agents, all reported in recent days (translated from German) ([SRF, 2026-11-26](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/fuenf-bestaetigte-faelle-erstmals-cyberangriffe-mit-ki-agenten-in-der-schweiz)). SRF reports that three victims are operators of critical infrastructure and that SMEs that reported voluntarily were also hacked with the help of AI agents, that systems were successfully hacked in four of the five cases (indirect speech, with no named speaker for that sentence), that there had been no larger outages, that it is still unclear who is behind the attacks and that BACS will not name the victims, who have the right to communicate themselves, in Schütz's words ([SRF, 2026-11-26](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/fuenf-bestaetigte-faelle-erstmals-cyberangriffe-mit-ki-agenten-in-der-schweiz)). RTS, relaying the interview, reads three attacks on critical infrastructure of which two succeeded, without saying whether that count covers all five cases (translated from French) ([RTS, 2026-11-26](https://www.rts.ch/info/suisse/2026/article/cybersecurite-5-cyberattaques-menees-par-l-ia-en-suisse-29428554.html)), while BACS's own page of 2026-11-24, unchanged on 2026-11-28, still says that every case known to it ended in a compromise ([BACS, 2026-11-24](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/en/26-ki-agenten-1-en)). The three readings differ, so no single success ratio is a BACS finding. The electricity, water and telecom examples in SRF and RTS define what critical infrastructure is and are not the victims' sectors, and neither outlet names a framework or a public administration. SRF also reports that BACS recommends installing critical updates at the latest 24 hours after their release ([SRF, 2026-11-26](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/fuenf-bestaetigte-faelle-erstmals-cyberangriffe-mit-ki-agenten-in-der-schweiz)).
