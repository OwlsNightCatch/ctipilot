# SAP hit by 'Project Granite' cyberattack as hackers reportedly steal GitHub tokens and alter source code

**What happened?** [SAP](https://dealroom.co/companies/sap/), Europe's largest software company, is dealing with an IT security incident that targets its software development. Der Spiegel first reported a hacking attack on Thursday evening, 26 November. An insider told the magazine that attackers stole authentication tokens belonging to SAP employees and used them to copy and alter SAP source code on GitHub. Internally, SAP is handling it as an "active security incident" under the code name "Project Granite", and Spiegel reports the attackers may have used AI agents.

**What SAP says:** In a statement to dpa, SAP said it had identified suspicious activity and "immediately took the necessary measures to protect its customers and ensure the security of its services". Based on the current state of its investigation, it has found no evidence of unauthorised access to customers' business data. SAP has not said how the attackers got in, who they are, or which code or repositories were affected, and has not confirmed the token theft or the AI angle.

**The response:** According to Handelsblatt, citing people inside the company, SAP restricted access to GitHub, paused automated build and delivery pipelines and rotated its code-signing keys, steps meant to stop attackers using stolen credentials and to keep tampered code out of shipped software. Many development teams are affected, and staff were asked to check and change their credentials.

**Why it matters:** SAP's software runs the finance, supply chain and HR operations of many of the world's largest companies and public bodies, so any sign of tampering in its development pipeline is a supply-chain question for its customers. The key open question is whether any altered code was published or reached customer builds. It is SAP's second brush with a code supply-chain attack this year, after official SAP npm packages were compromised in April. The incident lands the same day Bloomberg reported that SAP is weighing at least 10,000 job cuts in an AI overhaul.
