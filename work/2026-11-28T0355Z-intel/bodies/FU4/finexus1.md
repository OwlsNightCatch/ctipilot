SAP SE, the global leader in enterprise resource planning software, confirmed on November 27, 2026, that it is investigating a cybersecurity incident involving its software development infrastructure. The breach, which the company is tracking internally under the code name Project Granite, involved the unauthorized access of SAP repositories on the GitHub platform. According to internal communications and reports from German media outlets including Der Spiegel and Handelsblatt, threat actors successfully utilized stolen employee authentication tokens to copy and, in some instances, alter source code.

The incident was first detected after SAP identified suspicious activity within its GitHub environment. While the company has not publicly detailed the specific repositories affected, insiders suggest that the breach allowed attackers to bypass standard security protocols by leveraging valid developer credentials. Reports indicate that the intrusion may have been facilitated by agentic artificial intelligence tools, a development that has prompted warnings from Germany’s Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) regarding the rising sophistication of supply-chain attacks.

In response to the discovery, SAP implemented a series of emergency security protocols to contain the threat and prevent the distribution of tampered code. These measures included the immediate rotation of code-signing keys and the restriction of access to its GitHub environment. Furthermore, the company reportedly suspended automated development and delivery operations, affecting numerous software development teams across its global organization. Employees were instructed to assess their login credentials and implement additional security precautions as part of the remediation effort.

SAP has emphasized that its investigation has so far found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data. In a statement provided to media outlets, a company representative noted that SAP promptly implemented necessary measures to protect its customers and preserve the security of its services. The company, which serves more than 300 million subscribers and operates in 157 countries, maintains that its highest priority remains the protection of customer data in a dynamic threat environment.

The Project Granite incident marks the second significant code-related security challenge for SAP in 2026, following a compromise of official npm packages earlier in the year. Although there is currently no evidence that tampered code reached customer production environments, the breach highlights ongoing vulnerabilities in developer-centric infrastructure. SAP has not yet disclosed the total number of affected tokens or the specific identity of the threat actors involved. The company continues to conduct a comprehensive audit of its source code repositories to ensure the integrity of its software supply chain.
