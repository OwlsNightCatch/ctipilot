**Enterprise software giant SAP has confirmed it is investigating a cybersecurity incident following reports that threat actors targeted its development infrastructure.**

German publication [Der Spiegel](https://www.spiegel.de/netzwelt/sap-wird-opfer-von-hackerangriff-a-6648b5ce-5d74-48dc-86ea-9469705bb91d) \[paywalled\] broke the news on Thursday, citing SAP insiders and an internal notification sent to employees.

SAP told *SecurityWeek* that it had detected suspicious activity and implemented measures to protect customers and secure its services. The German company did not disclose details of the attack.

“Based on our investigation to date, we have found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data,” SAP told *SecurityWeek*.

German news agency [DPA](https://www.sueddeutsche.de/wirtschaft/softwarekonzern-sap-haben-verdaechtige-aktivitaeten-identifiziert-dpa.urn-newsml-dpa-com-20090101-261126-930-57534) reviewed internal SAP communications that revealed the suspicious activity had been identified within GitHub.

According to an insider cited by Der Spiegel, the hackers obtained SAP employees’ authentication tokens, enabling them to copy and alter source code stored on GitHub.

SAP publishes developer tools, sample applications, and open source projects on GitHub. It’s unclear whether the threat actor accessed any proprietary code.

Separately, [Handelsblatt](https://www.handelsblatt.com/technik/ki/sap-it-sicherheitsvorfall-haben-verdaechtige-aktivitaeten-identifiziert/100273138.html) reported that SAP’s emergency security measures have affected numerous teams. The software giant has reportedly replaced signing keys, limited access to GitHub, and suspended automated development operations.

None of the reports indicate that tampered code was incorporated into software delivered to SAP customers.

Sources cited by Der Spiegel suggested that the attack may have been assisted by AI. While AI involvement has not been confirmed, several major organizations, including the [European Commission](https://www.securityweek.com/european-commission-uncovers-data-breach-in-cyberattack-investigation/), have recently disclosed [AI-assisted cyberattacks](https://www.securityweek.com/barracuda-hacked-amid-surge-in-artex-powered-cyberattacks/).

In late October, the Gentlemen ransomware group listed SAP on its leak website, pointing to the company’s Latin American operations. File listings reviewed by *SecurityWeek* suggest the hackers obtained SAP-related software and source code, but it is unclear whether SAP itself or a third party was compromised. The two incidents do not appear to be connected.
