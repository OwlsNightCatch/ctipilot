SAP says it detected suspicious activity and took steps to secure its services after a reported intrusion into the GitHub environment it uses to develop and deliver software.

The company says its investigation has so far found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data.

German news magazine [DER SPIEGEL first reported](https://www.spiegel.de/netzwelt/sap-wird-opfer-von-hackerangriff-a-6648b5ce-5d74-48dc-86ea-9469705bb91d) the incident on November 26, citing an internal memo to SAP employees. The German Press Agency (dpa), which separately reviewed part of the memo, reported that SAP had detected the activity in its GitHub environment and asked employees to check whether they needed to change their credentials.

SAP, headquartered in Walldorf, Germany, makes enterprise resource planning software that companies use to run operations such as finance, human resources, and supply chain management.

### Reported token theft and source code changes

DER SPIEGEL cited an insider who said the attackers stole authentication tokens belonging to SAP employees and used them to copy and alter source code on GitHub. Authentication tokens act as digital credentials, giving whoever holds them access within the permissions granted to the token.

Handelsblatt, citing sources within SAP, reported that the company restricted access to GitHub, paused automated development workflows, and replaced signing keys as part of a response dubbed “Project Granite.” The measures affected numerous development teams, [the newspaper reported](https://www.handelsblatt.com/technik/ki/sap-it-sicherheitsvorfall-haben-verdaechtige-aktivitaeten-identifiziert/100273138.html).

“SAP has identified suspicious activity and promptly implemented the necessary measures to protect its customers and preserve the security of its services,” SAP said in a statement shared with the media. “Based on our investigation to date, we have found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data.”

SAP’s statement did not specify when it detected the activity or how it began. The company has yet to say whether source code was copied or altered, or whether any software releases delivered to customers were affected.

DER SPIEGEL also reported that the attackers may have used AI agents, a possibility SAP’s statement did not address. In a separate advisory issued the same day, Germany’s Federal Office for Information Security [(BSI) warned](https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Service-Navi/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/Presse2026/261126_agentische-KI.html) that AI agents can automate much of a cyberattack using widely available models, lowering the cost and potentially making such attacks more common. The agency urged companies still building out their defenses to prioritize incident response and close off the easiest ways into their systems.
