# Europe’s SAP security incident: No evidence of customer data breach found

The incident of security breach was first reported by Der Spiegel on Thursday

Europe’s largest software company has recently been hit by an alleged cyberattack that targeted its software development.

The incident of security breach was first reported by Der Spiegel on Thursday as one insider told German magazine that hackers stole authentication tokens which belonged to SAP employees and used them to copy and change SAP source code on GitHub.

SAP has internally classified the breach as an "active security incident" codenamed "Project Granite." According to Der Spiegel, the attackers may have utilized AI agents in the assault.

On Friday, Reuters reported that although SAP admitted the occurrence of suspicious activity in its IT systems but company found no evidence of “unauthorized access to its custom business data.”

In a statement, SAP said it acted quickly to protect customers and secure its services after detecting the activity.

"Based on our investigation to date, we have found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data," it said.

Moreover, SAP has not disclosed the attackers’ identities, how they hacked the systems, which specific code or repositories were affected or confirmed reports regarding stolen tokens or the use of AI in the security incident.

SAP also restricted access to GitHub and paused automated build and delivery pipelines. The company rotated its code-signing keys to prevent tampered code from reaching shipped software and instructed affected development teams to verify and change their credentials.

This incident raises privacy concerns as SAP runs core finance, HR, and supply-chain systems for major global corporations and public institutions. This also marks SAP’s second code supply-chain security incident this year.
