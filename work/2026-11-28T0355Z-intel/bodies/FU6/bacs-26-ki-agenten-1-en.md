# Cyberattacks involving AI agents: First confirmed cases in Switzerland

**In recent days, the NCSC has received its first reports of cyberattacks against organisations in Switzerland in which the attack was carried out with the assistance of an AI agent. Various organisations and operators of critical infrastructure across at least two sectors have been affected by these cyberattacks. In all cyberattacks known to the NCSC, the use of AI agents led to the successful compromise of the victim’s IT systems. These cases represent a shift in the cyber threat landscape. The NCSC anticipates an increase in the number of rapid and successful cyberattacks. It therefore strongly advises companies and operators of critical infrastructure to take necessary measures to enhance their cyber security and resilience.**

For the first time, the NCSC has confirmed cases in which AI agents have been used in cyberattacks against companies and operators of critical infrastructure in Switzerland. In these cases, the attackers used so-called “Agentic Offensive Frameworks”. These are AI agents which, for example, independently search for vulnerabilities, select suitable attack tools, evaluate the results, and plan and execute the next steps for the successful execution of the cyberattack. Such AI agents can be used both by IT security specialists to identify vulnerabilities (penetration testing) and by attackers to breach computer networks (dual-use).

In doing so, such AI agents exploit, for example, standard or easily guessed passwords, as well as known vulnerabilities in software or in systems connected to the internet. AI agents often require only a fraction of the time a human would need to locate such vulnerable systems and exploit existing vulnerabilities. Furthermore, they can react flexibly to defence mechanisms by switching to other known attack methods. This leaves organisations with significantly less time to detect an attack and respond appropriately.

According to assessments by the NCSC, a clear shift in the cyber threat landscape is emerging, with AI-enabled cyberattacks greatly increasing the speed of common attack methods. Furthermore, the number of AI agents accessible via the internet, such as “ARTEX” or “CyberStrikeAI”, has almost doubled since October 2026. This points to an increasing prevalence of these publicly available tools. Their growing use is leading to more frequent, faster and automated cyberattacks with a correspondingly higher success rate, which can target all types of organisations.

In view of the changing cyber threat landscape, the free availability of such AI agents and their growing popularity, the NCSC anticipates an increase in successful cyberattacks and urgently advises companies and operators of critical infrastructure to take the necessary measures to enhance cyber security and resilience.

The NCSC monitors the cyber threat landscape closely and is in constant communication with national and international partners. The NCSC also shares technical indicators for detecting attacks by such AI tools with operators of critical infrastructure.

## Recommendations

The NCSC recommends that companies in Switzerland implement the following measures without delay.

- **Limiting exposed systems:**
Systems accessible via the internet are a popular target for AI-assisted cyberattacks. Minimising the number of systems exposed to the internet also reduces the attack surface. Where possible, restrict access to such systems, e.g. by means of geo-blocking or the use of an “allow list” for specific IP addresses or network segments.
- **Patch management:**
Critical security updates for systems accessible via the internet should be installed as soon as possible, but no later than 24 hours after release.
- **Use of MFA or passkeys:**
All internal resources accessible via the internet, such as webmail, VPN, etc., should be consistently and without exception secured using multi-factor authentication (MFA) or passkeys.
- **Principle of least privilege:**
Adhere to the principle of least privilege by granting access rights with the lowest possible privileges wherever possible.
- **Detection of changes in behaviour and data volumes**:
Ensure that any deviations from established behaviour patterns in identities, applications, workloads and infrastructure are detected.
- **Regular penetration tests:**
Test your own IT infrastructure regularly, either yourself or through an external IT specialist, for potential vulnerabilities.
