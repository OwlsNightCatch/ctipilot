*Frontier AI is changing security. X-Force puts it to work across the enterprise, with experts in control, autonomous testing where it fits and everything on one platform.*

Published 19 October 2026

Updated 06 November 2026

Enterprises are now shifting their focus to how to apply frontier AI models within their own organizations without losing control. Simply adding another AI tool to your kit and hoping for the best is not the answer.

Today IBM is launching X-Force Advanced Proactive Security. It applies frontier AI, via OpenAI’s Daybreak program and other best-of-breed solutions, to your security testing in a controlled way. X-Force experts direct the work, autonomous testing runs where it makes sense and everything sits on one platform. The result gives exposure management programs continuous, validated evidence of an attacker’s capabilities, something these programs often lack.

X-Force Advanced Proactive Security is part of IBM’s broader strategy for [autonomous security.](https://www.ibm.com/services/autonomous-security) It acts alongside [IBM’s Autonomous Threat Operations Machine (ATOM)](https://www.ibm.com/services/autonomous-threat-operations) agents, which already support SOC analysis, threat intelligence and threat hunting.

AI-driven tools can produce findings faster than any team can validate them. Without control, that adds to a security team’s workload instead of lightening it. It also leaves a deeper problem unsolved. Penetration testing is still scoped and scheduled around a snapshot in time. Between tests, most of the estate goes unchecked, and attackers already using AI are ready to exploit that gap.

Responsible use of AI starts with business risk, not the model. We look at what each application holds and what it can reach, how exposed and critical it is and what regulators expect. From there, we recommend how deep testing should go, how often it should run and how much human oversight it needs. The client makes the final call, and we update our recommendation as their environment changes.

While AI is rapidly changing the speed of security, for most organizations the issue is how to deploy this technology responsibly. Letting agents operate without strong controls and visibility introduces unnecessary risk to the environment. To address this, X-Force keeps an expert in the loop. Frontier models assist with reconnaissance, attack-surface mapping, exploit development, ranking and report drafting, but the expert chooses the best model for each task based on measured performance. As the AI landscape changes, new models and technologies are integrated into our testing, keeping your organization ahead of emerging threats.

The expert can identify the business-logic flaws automation often misses. They understand the business context behind a system, weigh its level of criticality and direct every step that matters. The expert also validates AI output wherever the stakes call for it.

External network penetration tests and low-risk web application tests deliver continuous assurance through autonomous, AI-driven testing on a schedule the client sets. Clients can add human validation if they choose and request retests on demand. Frontier AI, via OpenAI’s Daybreak program and other best-of-breed solutions, powers the tests, while X-Force testers set the policy and review what is escalated before it reaches a client.

The result is continuous assurance of external exposure and application risk, feeding the client’s exposure management program with an ongoing answer instead of an annual snapshot.

Autonomous or expert-led, every test runs on the same platform. This means that there is one client portal to set up and request tests, one deduplicated view of findings per application and one way to retest every fix against the original finding. Findings also flow into the ticketing, vulnerability and exposure management tools teams already use across their software development lifecycle, making this method future proof. As new models arrive and more testing can responsibly run on its own, clients get the benefit without changing how they work, what they sign or how they buy.

The offering is designed around flexibility, not procurement. Organizations commit to one testing budget that gives them access to all X-Force offensive security services. The services range from autonomous testing for continuous assurance to expert-led, in-depth engagements, and organizations draw on the budget as they need it. Every test in the published catalog has a known cost, so more depth is within reach without a new contract or scoping exercise. As more testing becomes autonomous, the same budget goes further.

The offering is delivered by [IBM X-Force’s offensive security](https://www.ibm.com/services/offensive-security) testers, who test applications, networks, cloud, mainframe, hardware, OT and people every day. Each test follows the methodology and is backed by IBM Consulting’s global delivery and X-Force threat intelligence.

Attackers are already putting AI to work, and organizations change faster than point-in-time testing can keep up with. The answer is neither to wait nor to hand control to a model. It is to apply the best frontier AI under expert control, automate where it is safe, and keep one experience that stays the same as technology evolves. IBM works on both sides of this. It applies AI to complex operational problems, for example with [Lightwell, which remediates open-source vulnerabilities](https://www.ibm.com/products/lightwell). And it keeps frontier AI within guardrails: the [recently announced AI Cyber Resilience program](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/rethinking-readiness-how-enterprise-security-needs-to-plan-for-frontier-ai-models) helps clients assess their risk and plan remediation. As part of IBM’s autonomous security strategy, what testing uncovers and what threat operations see will increasingly inform each other.
