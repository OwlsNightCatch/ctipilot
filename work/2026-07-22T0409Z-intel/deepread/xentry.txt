												<div class="c-wysiwyg">
													<p>Recently, our teams in Latin America investigated a series of incidents involving misconfiguration, the deployment of BitLocker, and the exploitation of corporate printers. Attackers used the devices to notify organizations that their infrastructure had been compromised and they had to pay a ransom to recover their data.</p>
<p>This article analyzes two incidents that occurred in June in Colombia and in May in Mexico. We highlight the similarities in the attackers&#8217; communications and outline emerging trends in ransom amounts.</p>
<h2 id="initial-sign-of-an-attack">Initial sign of an attack</h2>
<p>In both cases, the affected users initially noticed a padlock icon next to their drives in Windows Explorer. This indicated that the drive was encrypted with BitLocker, blocking access to its contents.</p>

<p>This is not the first time we have seen such threats; a few years ago, our team discovered <a href="https://securelist.com/ransomware-abuses-bitlocker/112643/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">a threat known as ShrinkLocker</a>, which utilized BitLocker to achieve its goals.</p>
<h2 id="first-case-abusing-rdp-to-encrypt-data">First case: abusing RDP to encrypt data</h2>
<p>One of the incidents occurred in Colombia in June. The attackers exploited an internet-exposed RDP service on a machine connected to an 8 TB storage device containing mission-critical data. After taking control of the system and manipulating user credentials, the attackers enabled BitLocker exclusively on the drive that primarily stored financial data. Once the encryption was complete, they locked the drive and used the company&#8217;s printers to produce ransom notes.</p>
<div id="attachment_120733" style="width: 909px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171237/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker3.png" class="magnificImage"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-120733" class="size-full wp-image-120733" src="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171237/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker3.png" alt="Ransomware note" width="899" height="355" srcset="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171237/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker3.png 899w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171237/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker3-300x118.png 300w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171237/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker3-768x303.png 768w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171237/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker3-886x350.png 886w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171237/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker3-740x292.png 740w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171237/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker3-709x280.png 709w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171237/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker3-800x316.png 800w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 899px) 100vw, 899px" /></a><p id="caption-attachment-120733" class="wp-caption-text">Ransomware note</p></div>
<p>Unfortunately, it was not possible to obtain evidence in the case due to the company&#8217;s rush to restore the encrypted disk. The communication with the attackers revealed a demand for just $3,000, and the company considered paying the ransom. After that, the system was restored before the forensic team could take any action, eliminating the evidence needed to assess the incident.</p>
<div id="attachment_120734" style="width: 784px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171317/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker4.png" class="magnificImage"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-120734" class="size-full wp-image-120734" src="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171317/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker4.png" alt="Attacker's reply to the victim's email sent to the address in the printed ransom note" width="774" height="818" srcset="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171317/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker4.png 774w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171317/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker4-284x300.png 284w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171317/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker4-768x812.png 768w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171317/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker4-331x350.png 331w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171317/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker4-740x782.png 740w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171317/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker4-265x280.png 265w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 774px) 100vw, 774px" /></a><p id="caption-attachment-120734" class="wp-caption-text">Attacker&#8217;s reply to the victim&#8217;s email sent to the address in the printed ransom note</p></div>
<p>This attack was made possible by an internet-facing remote desktop service (RDP) with additional open ports, which employees used to access corporate information. By exploiting this network exposure and misconfiguration, attackers breached the system, identified an additional drive, and leveraged BitLocker to encrypt the data and demand a ransom payment. Leaving RDP ports open without proper security controls jeopardizes the security of systems and information, as highlighted in the our &#8220;<a href="https://www.kaspersky.com/enterprise-security/resources/reports/mdr-ir-analyst-reports" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Global Report: Anatomy of a Cyber World</a>&#8220;.</p>
<div id="attachment_120735" style="width: 374px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171348/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker5.png" class="magnificImage"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-120735" class="size-full wp-image-120735" src="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171348/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker5.png" alt="Exposed ports identified in the system in recent months" width="364" height="143" srcset="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171348/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker5.png 364w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171348/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker5-300x118.png 300w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 364px) 100vw, 364px" /></a><p id="caption-attachment-120735" class="wp-caption-text">Exposed ports identified in the system in recent months</p></div>
<p>The company confirmed that, due to compatibility issues with applications required for operation, EPP (Endpoint Protection Platform) protection was disabled on the system, making it easier for attackers to validate, enumerate, and execute applications without revealing malicious activity to central monitoring systems.</p>
<h2 id="second-case-meet-the-xentry-team">Second case: meet the XEntry Team</h2>
<p>In another incident, which occurred in Mexico in May, our team identified how the threat actor gained initial access to the infrastructure. They exploited a misconfigured MSSQL service. This allowed them to execute commands on the system after obtaining the database login credentials from code insecurely published on GitHub.</p>
<div id="attachment_120736" style="width: 970px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171447/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker6.jpeg" class="magnificImage"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-120736" class="size-full wp-image-120736" src="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171447/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker6.jpeg" alt="XEntry team attack" width="960" height="1280" srcset="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171447/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker6.jpeg 960w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171447/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker6-225x300.jpeg 225w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171447/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker6-768x1024.jpeg 768w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171447/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker6-263x350.jpeg 263w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171447/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker6-740x987.jpeg 740w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171447/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker6-210x280.jpeg 210w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19171447/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker6-675x900.jpeg 675w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" /></a><p id="caption-attachment-120736" class="wp-caption-text">XEntry team attack</p></div>
<p>In this incident, the attack began three months prior to detection, with the intruder discovering and verifying their access to the environment. After confirming their access and privilege level within the MSSQL server settings, which extended beyond the DBMS to the underlying operating system, the attackers initially focused on manipulating certain aspects of the web server configuration on the same system. They lowered the server&#8217;s security settings and created web shell files in the publicly accessible folders. Many of these attempts to manipulate the service or create malicious files were contained by existing EPP security controls, but despite the alerts, the necessary investigation to address the activity was not conducted.</p>

<p>The attackers subsequently confirmed their ability to execute commands locally and set up their attack infrastructure to transmit data via a communications bridge. By exploiting the MSSQL service, they gained access to each of the organization&#8217;s internal systems.</p>
<p>The database engine used by the company was Microsoft SQL Server 2019.0150.2160.04, misconfigured to allow operating system сommand execution via the <code>xp_cmdshell</code> extended stored procedure.</p>
<p>Due to this misconfiguration of an internet-exposed service, the attackers established a channel capable of executing any type of command directed at the server and the local infrastructure within its scope.</p>

<p>In early May, the attackers focused on running additional scans and deploying ManageEngine&#8217;s Endpoint Central RMM (Remote Monitoring and Management) to establish persistence and begin the final stages of their intrusion.</p>
<div id="attachment_120740" style="width: 1606px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14.png" class="magnificImage"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-120740" class="size-full wp-image-120740" src="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14.png" alt="Scanning and RMM deployment" width="1596" height="665" srcset="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14.png 1596w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14-300x125.png 300w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14-1024x427.png 1024w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14-768x320.png 768w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14-1536x640.png 1536w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14-840x350.png 840w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14-740x308.png 740w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14-672x280.png 672w, https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172023/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker14-800x333.png 800w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1596px) 100vw, 1596px" /></a><p id="caption-attachment-120740" class="wp-caption-text">Scanning and RMM deployment</p></div>
<p>Further RMM-type applications, such as Mesh Agent and Tactical RMM, were installed in the days that followed. These were used to deploy scheduled tasks responsible for enabling the BitLocker service and individually encrypting the infrastructure&#8217;s disks, generating a key for each encrypted system.</p>
<div id="attachment_120741" style="width: 1674px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2026/07/19172058/new-extortion-scheme-printers-bitlocker15.png" class="magnificImage"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" <p>Finally, in mid-May, the attackers managed to execute a Group Policy Object (GPO) used to deploy activation and encryption tasks, as well as other policies responsible for continued deployment of RMM applications via scheduled tasks. The activity initially targeted critical systems but later spread to every system synchronized with the domain controller. Users became aware of the attack when their machines displayed a blue screen with the message &#8220;Hacked by XEntry Team&#8221;, and their credentials stopped working to access their systems.</p>
<p>A few hours later, ransom notes began emerging from office printers.</p>

<p>These cases confirm that adversary&#8217;s objective is to gain access to infrastructure while avoiding investment in or partnership with ransomware groups. Instead, they leverage built-in Microsoft tools to facilitate data encryption and ransom payments. Monitoring and centralizing logs on protected resources, as well as promptly managing alerts, are critical to countering this type of intrusion.</p>
<h2 id="conclusions">Conclusions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Although the systems under review had security measures in place, there was a lack of proper alert management or inadequate decisions regarding application incompatibilities.</li>
<li>We strongly recommend configuring the Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) in strict accordance with cybersecurity best practices to prevent unauthorized access. This is especially critical: according to our <a href="https://www.kaspersky.com/enterprise-security/resources/reports/mdr-ir-analyst-reports" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Global Report: Anatomy of a Cyber World</a>, more than 13% of incidents are related to policy violations and configuration errors, confirming that misconfigurations continue to pose a significant risk.</li>
<li>Organizations should prioritize strict application control policies and active monitoring of network traffic for command-and-control (C2) communications. This is especially critical: <a href="https://www.kaspersky.com/enterprise-security/resources/reports/mdr-ir-analyst-reports" target="_blank" rel="noopener">according to the same report</a>, more than 20% of incidents involved the abuse of RMM (Remote Monitoring and Management) tools for execution and C2 strategies. The fact that attackers used more than three distinct tools to gain control during a single incident further underscores the urgent need for these measures.</li>
<li>Some questions remain unanswered due to a lack of evidence and a hasty system restoration effort that bypassed critical stages of the incident response process. It is important to ensure an adequate incident response procedure, preserving evidence to confirm all related activities, and adjusting or proposing controls to prevent future incidents involving similar TTPs.</li>
<li>Although the ransom notes do not reveal a clear connection between the actors, certain words used in the messages, as well as the method of delivery and communication, may confirm a link:</li>
</ul>
<p><em>&#8220;As a guarantee, we have no negative online reviews about non-fulfillment of our obligations…&#8221; (Ransom note from the first case)</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;Our reputation is the guarantee that all content will be fulfilled…&#8221; (Ransom note from the second case)</em></p>
<p>Our teams continue to monitor these threats.</p>
<h2 id="detection-signatures">Detection signatures</h2>
<ul>
<li>Trojan.Multi.Agent.gen</li>
<li>Trojan.Win32.GenAutorunMsSqlServerCommandRun.a</li>
<li>Trojan.Win32.Generic</li>
<li>Exploit.Win32.SCShell.a</li>
</ul>
