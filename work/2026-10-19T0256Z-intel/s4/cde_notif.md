## Notice of Data Breach

Educational Testing Service (ETS), at the direction of the California Department of Education (CDE), is notifying California students and families about a data security incident involving unauthorized access to and copying of student data from an ETS secure file transfer protocol (SFTP) site, the steps being taken to protect their personal information, and available resources and support. Appropriate law enforcement agencies have been notified, but their investigations have not delayed this notice.

ETS is a nonprofit assessment organization under contract with the State of California to provide state and federally required testing to all students enrolled in California public schools. ETS supports California’s statewide assessment programs, including the California Assessment of Student Performance and Progress (CAASPP) and the English Language Proficiency Assessments for California (ELPAC).

We are notifying you because you or your minor dependent’s personal information was accessed and copied during this data security incident.

**To help protect affected current and former students, ETS is providing complimentary identity-protection services through Experian IdentityWorks for 2 years.**

## What Happened?

On September 25, 2026, ETS identified unauthorized activity involving an SFTP site used to exchange California statewide assessment information with CDE.


ETS disabled the account involved, took the affected SFTP site offline, and initiated an investigation with the assistance of external cybersecurity experts. The investigation determined that an unauthorized party used a compromised login credential at various times during the period from May 22, 2026 to September 18, 2026 to access the SFTP site to copy files containing California student information.

## What Information Was Involved?

Information about California students, including but not limited to all students enrolled in the three previous school years, was accessed and copied. The information that was accessed and copied contained student demographic, program participation, and assessment data that ETS maintains in connection with California's statewide assessment programs.


The student information accessed and copied varies by individual, but may include:

- Student name, date of birth, residential address, school, district, and grade level;
- Statewide Student Identifier (SSID) and local student identifier;
- Gender, race or ethnicity, primary language, country of origin, and English learner status;
- Disability and Section 504 status, testing accommodations, and primary disability type;
- Eligibility status for the following programs: Title I, Part A; Title I, Part C; McKinney-Vento; and Military Connected Student; and/or
- English language proficiency information and statewide assessment registration, participation, and results, where applicable.

**The data accessed and copied did not include Social Security numbers, driver’s license numbers, passport numbers, financial account information, credit or debit card information, or account passwords. The incident did not involve parent or guardian personal information.**

## What We Are Doing

### ETS Response

Following discovery of the incident, ETS has taken numerous measures to enhance the security of the SFTP site, including taking the SFTP site offline and building an entirely new SFTP site with enhanced security controls. ETS continues to evaluate additional security measures that may be appropriate.


After working with law enforcement as well as with cybersecurity experts, ETS has taken steps to reduce the potential risk of the data being made publicly accessible. Furthermore, ETS, following regular web monitoring, has, to date, not uncovered any evidence that the information has been made public.

### State Response

CDE and the State Board of Education (SBE) are working with state cybersecurity experts to assess the effect on California students and families and so that affected individuals receive appropriate information and support.


The California Cybersecurity Integration Center (Cal-CSIC) is assisting with digital forensics and incident response support. The California Department of Technology (CDT) and California Governor’s Office of Emergency Services (Cal OES) are also supporting the state’s technical, legal, and operational response.


CDE and SBE are also reviewing the incident and ETS’s compliance with applicable data security and contractual requirements.

## Complimentary Identity-Protection Services Through Experian

To help protect affected current and former students, ETS is providing identity-protection services through Experian IdentityWorks for 2 years at no cost to you.


Two identity-protection services packages are available, one for minors and one for current and former students who are now adults.


**Minor Plus - For affected students under age 18:** A parent or legal guardian can enroll the student in the available minor identity-protection services on or before the enrollment deadline on March 19, 2027. Minors who turn 18 between now and March 19, 2029 may enroll in the adult product upon their 18th birthday by using the adult product activation code. **For information on how to sign minors up for this product, see the Attachment to this Notice.**

**Adult - For affected individuals age 18 or older:** Affected adults can enroll directly in the available Experian IdentityWorks services before the enrollment deadline on or before March 19, 2027. **For information on how to sign up for this product as an adult, see the Attachment to this Notice.**

**There is no cost to you to enroll, and no credit card information will be requested.**

## What You Can Do

We encourage you to consider enrolling affected students in the identity protection services and remain alert to possible misuse of student personal information. Be cautious of fake identity protection sites by double checking URLs before providing information.

It is always best practice to be cautious of unexpected emails, text messages, or phone calls that reference a student's school, statewide assessments, or this incident. Do not share personal information, passwords, or financial information in response to unsolicited requests.

Affected students and their families may also consider reviewing additional information about identity theft and protecting personal information available from the California Department of Justice’s Privacy Enforcement and Protection Unit at [www.oag.ca.gov/privacy](https://www.oag.ca.gov/privacy).

##### Security Freeze

Security freezes, also known as credit freezes, restrict access to your credit file, making it harder for identity thieves to open new accounts in your name. You can freeze and unfreeze your credit file for free. You also can get a free freeze for your children who are under 16. And if you are someone’s guardian, conservator or have a valid power of attorney, you can get a free freeze for that person, too.

How will these freezes work? Contact all three of the nationwide credit reporting agencies – Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. If you request a freeze online or by phone, the agency must place the freeze within one business day. If you request a lift of the freeze, the agency must lift it within one hour. If you make your request by mail, the agency must place or lift the freeze within three business days after it gets your request. You also can lift the freeze temporarily without a fee.

The following information must be included when requesting a security freeze (note that if you are requesting a credit report for your spouse, this information must be provided for him/her as well): (1) full name, with middle initial and any suffixes; (2) Social Security number; (3) date of birth; (4) current address and any previous addresses for the past five years; and (5) any applicable incident report or complaint with a law enforcement agency or the Registry of Motor Vehicles. The request must also include a copy of a government-issued identification card and a copy of a recent utility bill or bank or insurance statement.

It is essential that each copy be legible, display your name and current mailing address, and the date of issue.

**For Colorado and Illinois  residents:** You  may obtain information from the credit reporting agencies and the FTC about  security freezes.

**For New Mexico residents:** You may obtain a security freeze on your  credit report to protect your privacy and ensure that credit is not granted in  your name without your knowledge. You may submit a declaration of removal to  remove information placed in your credit report as a result of being a victim  of identity theft. You have a right to place a security freeze on your credit  report or submit a declaration of removal pursuant to the Fair Credit Reporting  and Identity Security Act. For more information, including information about additional  rights, go to [www.consumerfinance.gov/learnmore](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/learnmore) or  write to: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, 1700 G Street N.W., Washington,  DC 20552.

##### Fraud Alerts

A fraud alert tells businesses that check your credit that they should check with you before opening a new account. An initial fraud alert stays on your credit report for one year. You may have an extended alert placed on your credit report if you have already been a victim of identity theft with the appropriate documentary proof. An extended fraud alert stays on your credit report for seven years. To place a fraud alert on your credit reports, contact one of the nationwide credit bureaus. A fraud alert is free. The credit bureau you contact must tell the other two, and all three will place an alert on their versions of your report.

**For Colorado  and Illinois residents:** You may obtain additional information from the credit reporting  agencies and the FTC about fraud alerts.

##### Federal Trade Commission and State Attorneys General Offices

If  you believe you are the victim of identity theft or have reason to believe your  personal information has been misused, you should immediately contact the  Federal Trade Commission and/or the Attorney General’s office in your home  state. You may also contact these agencies for information on how to prevent or  avoid identity theft. You may contact the Federal Trade Commission, Consumer  Response Center, 600 Pennsylvania Avenue, NW, Washington, DC 20580, [www.ftc.gov/bcp/edu/microsites/idtheft/](http://www.ftc.gov/bcp/edu/microsites/idtheft/), 1-877-IDTHEFT  (438-4338).

##### Reporting of identity theft and obtaining a police report

You have the right to obtain any police report filed in the United States in regard to this incident. If you are the victim of fraud or identity theft, you also have the right to file a police report.

**For Iowa Residents:** You are advised to report any suspected  identity theft to law enforcement or to the Iowa Attorney General.

**For Massachusetts Residents:** You have the right to obtain a police  report if you are a victim of identity theft. You also have a right to file a  police report and obtain a copy of it.

**For Oregon Residents:** You are advised to report any suspected  identity theft to law enforcement, the Federal Trade Commission, and the Oregon  Attorney General.

**For Rhode  Island Residents:** You have the right to file or obtain a police report regarding this  incident.
