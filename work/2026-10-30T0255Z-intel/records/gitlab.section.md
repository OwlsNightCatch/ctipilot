CISA added CVE-2026-97229 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 2026-10-29, "based on evidence of active exploitation" ([CISA, 2026-10-29](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/29/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)), and NCSC Switzerland edited its advisory the same day to add reports of exploitation attempts, moving the exploitation status to active exploitation ([NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-29](https://security-hub.ncsc.admin.ch/#/posts/13051)). The report behind it is Alpine Linux's: on 2026-10-20 and 21 a newly registered account sent crafted CI configurations to its GitLab instance, which still ran 19.3.4, crashed the worker processes eight times and got partway through the exploit in four of them; the attacker returned on 2026-10-25 and 26 and failed, Alpine found no evidence that code ran but could not rule it out, and it rebuilt the instance ([Alpine Linux, 2026-10-28](https://alpinelinux.org/posts/GitLab-security-incident-CVE-2026-97229.html)). Alpine's upgrade to GitLab 19.3.6 on 2026-10-22 did not close the flaw, because the fix is a set of patches to the Ruby that GitLab applies only in its official packages and images, so an installation that runs its own Ruby needs Ruby 3.3.13, 3.4.12 or 4.0.9 or later as well ([Alpine Linux, 2026-10-28](https://alpinelinux.org/posts/GitLab-security-incident-CVE-2026-97229.html)). GitLab's release page now says its security team has released six threat detections for self-managed instances ([GitLab, 2026-10-12](https://docs.gitlab.com/releases/patches/patch-release-gitlab-19-4-2-released/)). Rapid7's statement of 2026-10-13 that it saw no confirmed exploitation predates all of this.
