The French blood service EFS confirmed on 2026-11-03 that on 2 November a refrigeration-equipment supplier suffered a cybersecurity incident that affected the supervision and control installations of cold rooms at three EFS sites, with temperature and defrost thresholds raised to very high values (translated from French) ([EFS, 2026-11-03](https://presse.efs.sante.fr/actualites/l-efs-confirme-l-incident-de-cybersecurite-impactant-un-de-ces-prestataires-d-equipement-frigorifique-2c07c-08a0a.html)). EFS says its own monitoring and alarm systems identified the anomalies quickly, the affected equipment was brought under control without impact on the products or neutralised, the products stored in those cold rooms were transferred preventively to other installations, and no health impact or loss of blood products is to be reported; it names no supplier and gives no temperature figure (translated from French) ([EFS, 2026-11-03](https://presse.efs.sante.fr/actualites/l-efs-confirme-l-incident-de-cybersecurite-impactant-un-de-ces-prestataires-d-equipement-frigorifique-2c07c-08a0a.html)).

BleepingComputer, heise and SRF identify the supplier as Coolworld, and SRF reports that the attackers reportedly entered through its remote maintenance ([BleepingComputer, 2026-11-04](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cyberattack-disrupts-blood-storage-refrigeration-systems-in-france/); [heise, 2026-11-04](https://www.heise.de/news/Nach-Gefriertruhen-Attacken-Jetzt-Blutbank-Kuehlung-im-Visier-11511444.html); [SRF, 2026-11-04](https://www.srf.ch/news/international/hintergruende-unklar-hackerangriff-auf-kuehlsysteme-blutbanken-in-frankreich-betroffen)). Coolworld's statement of 2026-11-04 says the incident involved remote monitoring software, that the vulnerability reported in the media relates to a component from a third-party supplier outside its control and that a limited number of its units were affected; it does not say that attackers exploited that component ([Coolworld, 2026-11-04](https://www.coolworld-rentals.com/news/cybersecurity-update)). No source names Danfoss, Copeland or Dixell equipment for this case.

ENISA, the European Commission (DG CNECT), Europol's EC3, the EU CSIRTs Network and EU-CyCLONe issued a joint statement on 2026-11-04: since 11 October 2026 several commercial refrigeration systems and similar devices have been hit in a number of EU Member States, a new wave occurred on 1 to 2 November, affected Member States are investigating and issuing guidance, and organisations are advised to take immediate action to mitigate exposure in the affected systems; it names no product, actor or count and does not mention Switzerland ([ENISA, 2026-11-04](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/joint-statement-on-cyber-incidents-in-refrigeration-systems-across-the-eu)). CSIRT Italia's bulletin of 2026-11-04 extends its call to every operator of cold-chain technology, whatever the sector, to apply its earlier measures urgently, removing direct internet exposure of administrative and control interfaces and verifying that operating parameters and alarms are intact first ([CSIRT Italia, 2026-11-04](https://www.acn.gov.it/portale/w/sicurezza-dei-sistemi-a-supporto-della-catena-del-freddo-e-interfacce-di-management-di-sistemi-connessi-richiamo-urgente-alle-misure-di-verifica-e-mitigazione)). Its earlier bulletin asks operators to limit supplier access to authorised resources and periods, to require multi-factor authentication on remote access, applied on the mediating system when the device cannot do it, and to check actual temperatures and alarms with instruments independent of the supervision platform ([CSIRT Italia, 2026-11-04](https://www.acn.gov.it/portale/w/misure-urgenti-di-verifica-e-mitigazione-per-operatori-della-distribuzione-alimentare-e-della-filiera-del-freddo)). The BSI had urged operators of networked freezer controllers to disconnect them from the internet or at least secure and patch them ([heise, 2026-11-04](https://www.heise.de/news/Nach-Gefriertruhen-Attacken-Jetzt-Blutbank-Kuehlung-im-Visier-11511444.html)).
