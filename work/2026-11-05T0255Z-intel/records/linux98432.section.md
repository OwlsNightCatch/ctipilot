Citrix's bulletin CTX697256 of 2026-11-03, rated Critical, says an unauthenticated attacker on the management network may compromise a XenServer host and any running Workload Balancing or Conversion Manager appliance; it affects XenServer 8.4 and 9, Workload Balancing before 2026.1.1 and Conversion Manager before 2026.2.0, and Citrix has pushed updates to the Early Access and Normal channels of each XenServer line without naming a build and released Workload Balancing 2026.1.1 or later and Conversion Manager 2026.2.0 ([Citrix, 2026-11-03](https://support.citrix.com/external/article/CTX697256/xenserver-security-update-for-multiple-i.html)). The bulletin states no exploitation.

Vates's advisory VSA-2026-056, published on 2026-11-03 and updated on 2026-11-04, rates the flaw critical for XCP-ng 8.3 and the Xen Orchestra Appliance, lists the fixed packages kernel-4.19.19-8.0.46.14.xcpng8.3 and, for the appliance, linux 6.1.190-1, requires a reboot, and gives an interim mitigation that drops inbound IP options, because without IP options the vulnerable code path cannot be reached ([Vates, 2026-11-04](https://docs.vates.tech/security-advisories/advisories/2026/vates-sa-2026-056)). XCP-ng's post of 2026-11-04 says anyone who can send TCP traffic to the host, for example from its management network, VMs on it included, can attempt the attack, and tells hosts that cannot reboot at once to apply the iptables-based mitigation and not yet the update ([XCP-ng, 2026-11-04](https://xcp-ng.org/blog/2026/11/04/november-2026-security-updates-2-for-xcp-ng-8-3-lts/)). Both say a public exploit exists that relies on MPTCP, which the XCP-ng 8.3 kernel does not have, so it does not work against XCP-ng, while the underlying bug is present and other ways to exploit it cannot be ruled out ([XCP-ng, 2026-11-04](https://xcp-ng.org/blog/2026/11/04/november-2026-security-updates-2-for-xcp-ng-8-3-lts/)).

WatchGuard's advisory WGSA-2026-0033 of 2026-11-03 lists Fireware OS, AP and Dimension as potentially affected with no fix, says Fireware OS and AP do not enable MPTCP and are not affected by the exploit chain circulating online, and says it will update the advisory when Ubuntu ships a fix for Dimension ([WatchGuard, 2026-11-03](https://psirt.watchguard.com/WGSA-2026-0033/)). MikroTik's RouterOS 7.25.1 of 2026-11-02 and the long-term release 7.24.6 of 2026-10-30 list the CVE under "improve stability" ([MikroTik, 2026-11-02](https://upgrade.mikrotik.com/routeros/7.25.1/CHANGELOG); [MikroTik, 2026-10-30](https://upgrade.mikrotik.com/routeros/7.24.6/CHANGELOG)). NCSC Switzerland's advisory 13120 of 2026-10-29 rates exploitation unknown with vendor patches available and names MPTCP listeners as the target of the known exploits ([NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-29](https://security-hub.ncsc.admin.ch/#/posts/13120)). Fortinet's advisory is unchanged: still investigating, with no fixed build, and Known Exploited: No ([Fortinet, 2026-11-02](https://fortiguard.fortinet.com/psirt/FG-IR-26-188)).
