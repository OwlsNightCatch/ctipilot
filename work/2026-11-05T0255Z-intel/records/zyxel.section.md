Binarly reports that the Python exploit GreyNoise described, which it recovered from Red Heron infrastructure, targets a flaw in the request-parameter parser of the switches' CGI (dispatcher.cgi), not the stack overflow of CVE-2026-7273, and that it works without change against the "…(x).2C0" builds Zyxel advertised as the June fix; Binarly confirmed this against all ten builds on 2026-09-28 ([Binarly, 2026-11-03](https://www.binarly.io/blog/zyxel-gs1900-rce-cve-2026-107441)). Zyxel treats the report as a separate vulnerability, CVE-2026-107441, fixed on 2026-10-22 in the "…(x).3C0" builds of GS1900-8, -8HP, -10HP, -16, -24, -24E, -24EP, -24HPv2, -48 and -48HPv2 and disclosed in an advisory of 2026-11-03 that credits Binarly and shows no score or exploitation statement ([Zyxel PSIRT, 2026-11-03](https://www.zyxel.com/global/en/support/security-advisories/zyxel-security-advisory-for-out-of-bounds-write-vulnerability-in-gs1900-series-switches-11-03-2026)). Binarly labels the flaw an in-the-wild zero-day on the strength of the exploit match, scores it 9.8 with a network vector against Zyxel's LAN-based wording, and says it will publish a minimised proof of concept; CISA's catalog lists only CVE-2026-7273 ([Binarly, 2026-11-03](https://www.binarly.io/advisories/brly-2026-057); [BleepingComputer, 2026-11-04](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/zyxel-fixes-zero-day-exploited-to-hack-nearly-1-000-network-switches/)). Switches on the June build are therefore still exposed to the exploit until the "…(x).3C0" build is installed.
