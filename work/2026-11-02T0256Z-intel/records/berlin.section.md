rbb24 Recherche reported on 2026-10-21, from the two affected Senate departments' own written answers, that the attack-detection system introduced in 2026 had licences for only five percent of all devices, that extending it to every device was not possible "for financial reasons", and that neither the endpoint identified as the criminals' entry point nor the servers from which data later flowed were covered by it (translated from German) ([rbb24, 2026-10-21](https://www.rbb24.de/politik/beitrag/2026/10/berlin-hackerangriff-verwaltung-darknet-rhysida-daten-senatorin-bonde.html)). The departments also said that in 2024 the funds needed for IT security were available only in part to the extent required, although the state's information-security guideline has obliged agencies since 2017 to provide enough staff and money (translated from German) ([rbb24, 2026-10-21](https://www.rbb24.de/politik/beitrag/2026/10/berlin-hackerangriff-verwaltung-darknet-rhysida-daten-senatorin-bonde.html)). The stolen data includes copies of identity cards and a digital signature of the transport senator and of employees; the mobility department confirmed to rbb that the data is mostly unstructured files from shared and personal directories (translated from German) ([rbb24, 2026-10-21](https://www.rbb24.de/politik/beitrag/2026/10/berlin-hackerangriff-verwaltung-darknet-rhysida-daten-senatorin-bonde.html)).

BSI told rbb that it had warned repeatedly since at least March 2025 of fake security prompts that make users run a malicious command themselves, publicly and in daily situation reports that also went to representatives of the federal states, and that it described the TerminalFix variant in September 2026; it does not confirm to rbb that the Berlin attack followed that pattern, saying only that it is in close contact with Berlin and supporting the response (translated from German) ([rbb24, 2026-10-21](https://www.rbb24.de/politik/beitrag/2026/10/berlin-hackerangriff-verwaltung-darknet-rhysida-daten-senatorin-bonde.html)). BSI's advisory of 2026-09-04 never names Berlin, so the link between TerminalFix and this incident rests on heise's reading of BSI's Mastodon post (the update of 2026-09-08), not on a BSI statement about Berlin. rbb describes the chain for such warnings: the Berlin-CERT run by the IT service centre ITDZ passes relevant reports to each agency's information-security officers, and each agency is itself responsible for acting on them; the ITDZ and the Senate Chancellery did not say whether BSI's warnings reached the two departments or what followed (translated from German) ([rbb24, 2026-10-21](https://www.rbb24.de/politik/beitrag/2026/10/berlin-hackerangriff-verwaltung-darknet-rhysida-daten-senatorin-bonde.html)).

**Defender takeaway:** the transferable gap is licence coverage, not the absence of a tool. Compare the endpoints and servers that actually run the detection agent, file servers holding shared and personal directories first, with the estate, and settle who in a federated administration is accountable for acting on a national-CERT warning, because in Berlin's chain the departments' answers do not show whether the warnings reached them.
