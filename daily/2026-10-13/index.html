    01Active threats, incidents & disclosures1 item

    HIGHexploitedupdatedNATOA2

    Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC, Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb refrigeration controllers compromised, with forced defrost reported at hundreds of French supermarkets; CERT-FR and NCSC Switzerland say to remove internet exposure

    Coopérative U, Intermarché and E. Leclerc confirmed to AFP a cyberattack on their refrigeration systems overnight on 11 to 12 October 2026; AFP reports from the retailers' statements at least 300 Intermarché or Netto shops and more than 100 Coopérative U outlets, and the Paris prosecutor opened an investigation (AFP, 2026-10-12). BFMTV puts the Intermarché and Netto count at 300, which it says is twice the first estimates of 150 points of sale, and Coopérative U below 100 stores, while Infosecurity quotes 150 Intermarché and 30 Netto stores (BFMTV, 2026-10-12; Infosecurity Magazine, 2026-10-12). BFMTV, writing in the conditional, reports that the attackers are said to have imposed a forced, continuous defrost with the setpoint at 50 degrees, on software older than version 4, heating the equipment for hours (translated from French) (BFMTV, 2026-10-12). CERT-FR's alert, in its revision of 2026-10-13, says it knows of numerous compromises of the remote refrigeration controllers Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC series as well as Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb, and that exposing a management interface to the internet is contrary to good practice (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13). Danfoss told AFP that most affected units are legacy products that no longer receive software or security updates, naming the AK-SM800 and AK-SC355 regulators, and a person close to the Prime Minister said ANSSI had identified a vulnerability in software from a Danish company and that other countries may be affected (AFP, 2026-10-12). Intermarché told BleepingComputer that the attack reportedly affected a service provider that manages refrigeration systems in stores (BleepingComputer, 2026-10-12), and E. Leclerc told AFP that a cyberattack affected one of its refrigeration equipment suppliers (AFP, 2026-10-12). Infosecurity says the affected stores include those running vulnerable consoles with remote control and no adequately secured communication channel (Infosecurity Magazine, 2026-10-12). ANSSI's director general told the French Senate on 2026-10-21 that elementary flaws were exploited, above all equipment directly on the internet and administered without particular protection, plus weak passwords, and dismissed analyses that blamed vulnerabilities researchers had published over the previous two years (Public Sénat, 2026-10-21); Le Monde Informatique adds that the systems were reachable without a firewall or network segmentation and that the management interface was protected only by weak passwords (Le Monde Informatique, 2026-10-22). No source names an actor, and none confirms a tie to the 2025 AK-SM 8xxA vulnerabilities that Claroty found: BleepingComputer says no information is available on how the controllers were compromised (BleepingComputer, 2026-10-12) and NCSC Switzerland lists three of them only as alleged to be potentially related (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13); Censys names the same three as flaws that Danfoss patched more than a year earlier and says Danfoss has not confirmed the initial access vector (Censys, 2026-10-14).

    Exposure
    A Danfoss AK-SM or AK-SC refrigeration controller, or a Copeland or Dixell xweb unit, whose management interface is reachable from the internet, above all legacy units; Danfoss says the current AK-SM800A units that were affected ran outdated firmware (Cooling Post, 2026-10-12), and CERT-FR names no models, flaw or entry vector for the Copeland and Dixell units (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13). Copeland first declined to confirm CERT-FR's finding and has since acknowledged a vulnerability, which, Cybersecurity Dive reports, a Copeland spokesperson confirmed was found in discontinued end-of-life controllers, and says it is working with impacted customers to mitigate the risk; neither article names the flaw (SecurityWeek, 2026-10-15; Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-10-15). Infosecurity reports, without naming them, that supermarkets in Belgium and the Netherlands were also reportedly affected (Infosecurity Magazine, 2026-10-12); Blick reports that Migros confirms using, in a limited number of its Swiss stores, the same Danfoss refrigeration systems that were targeted and says nothing indicates an attack on them, while Aldi and Lidl say they are not concerned and Coop says it was not affected and declines to say whether it uses Danfoss (Blick, 2026-10-13); no source reports a Swiss victim, so ask whoever manages your refrigeration and building services whether a controller sits in your estate. For an AK-SM 800A, Censys maps the firmware to the published flaws: a build below 4.2 carries all three, 4.2.4 fixes only the date-based login and so still carries the two later ones, and 4.3.1 or later fixes all three, with 5.3.2 the current build it lists (Censys, 2026-10-14). For the Copeland and Dixell units, CERT-FR links a Dixell France guide (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16) that carries Copeland's own bulletin of 14 October, in which Copeland acknowledges the vulnerability on a generation of XWEB EVO controllers that is no longer marketed and has reached end of life, says to remove every XWEB EVO from the public internet immediately and says XWEB EVO no longer receives security patches, and that adds Dixell France's own recommendations, among them that XWEB PRO and EVO ship with the same factory Admin account (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). The bulletin names the affected generation but describes no flaw (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). Forescout counts 12,976 internet-exposed service observations on 6,017 public addresses in 99 countries, 7,075 of them Copeland and Dixell XWEB and 4,697 Danfoss StoreView, and finds that 3,762 exposed Danfoss StoreView observations on AK-SM 800A units, 80 percent of the 4,697 StoreView observations, run firmware older than 4.3.1 (Forescout, 2026-10-22).
    Detection
    On the controller, defrost, setpoint and account changes outside a maintenance window; in firewall, provider VPN or ISP logs, inbound connections to the management interface from addresses other than the refrigeration contractor's; CERT-FR accepts filtering to the contractors' source addresses only as an emergency measure and wants access restored through a secure VPN or protected network (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13). For a controller that may have been taken over, CERT-FR's current text is to remove the internet exposure, restart the Danfoss unit or otherwise restore it with a factory reset, restore access only through a secure VPN or protected network, reconfigure it and apply the maker's practices, which for Danfoss means updating the firmware, setting the security level to Strict and confirming the default passwords were changed (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16); for a unit not known to be compromised it asks to check the whole configuration, notably the defrost ranges (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). Le Monde reports that besides raising the defrost setpoint to 50 degrees the attackers lengthened the defrost operation from one hour to eight so that alarms trigger late (translated from French), which makes the defrost duration a tamper signal beside the setpoint (Le Monde, 2026-10-12; SecurityWeek, 2026-10-15). Forescout advises correlating remote-access sessions with changes to defrost schedules, alarms, setpoints, users and network configuration, because the abused functions are legitimate and malicious changes resemble routine activity (Forescout, 2026-10-22).

    Triage: a defrost cycle is normal; BFMTV reports, citing sector professionals, that installations can be programmed for two hours of defrost per 24 hours, whereas the reported attack is said to be a continuous forced defrost at a 50-degree setpoint held for hours (translated from French) (BFMTV, 2026-10-12).

    Defender takeaway
    Take any Danfoss AK-SM or AK-SC, Copeland xweb or Dixell xweb management interface off the internet now and restore access only through a secure VPN or protected network, as CERT-FR advises (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13), and ask the contractor whether its own remote access was affected; for the AK-SM800 and AK-SC355, replacement is the lever, since Danfoss says it had long advised supermarkets to move off those models (AFP, 2026-10-12), no fix exists for them according to NCSC Switzerland (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13), and CERT-FR says a factory reset on an unpatched end-of-life unit removes the compromised instance's persistence but leaves the device vulnerable and almost certainly compromised again (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13). Because ANSSI puts the cause at exposure and weak passwords, a firmware update alone does not close the hole: the public address and the weak or default passwords have to go too, and ANSSI notes that its guidance on industrial systems has put not exposing them directly on the internet first since 2014 (Public Sénat, 2026-10-21).

    HIGHCVE-2026-97229exploitedupdatedNATOA2

    CVE-2026-97229, GitLab CE/EE: an integer overflow in the CI/CD regular-expression compiler allows code execution on self-managed servers, has a public proof of concept and is listed by CISA as exploited; fixed in 19.4.2, 19.3.5 and 19.2.8, and 19.4.1 does not close it (CVSS 10.0)

    GitLab's critical patch release of 2026-10-12 (19.4.2, 19.3.5 and 19.2.8) fixes CVE-2026-97229, an integer overflow in the CI/CD regular-expression compiler that, "under certain conditions", could allow an unauthenticated user to execute arbitrary code on the GitLab server through a specially crafted regular expression in a CI/CD configuration; GitLab scores it CVSS 3.1 10.0 with no privileges required (GitLab, 2026-10-12). Affected are self-managed CE and EE from 19.2 before 19.2.8, 19.3 before 19.3.5 and 19.4 before 19.4.2; CERT-EU and BleepingComputer name 19.3.4 as the 19.3 fix, which GitLab's page does not (CERT-EU, 2026-10-12; BleepingComputer, 2026-10-12). The 19.4.1 release of 2026-09-23 fixed two related flaws in the same component (GitLab, 2026-09-23) but is still affected (GitLab, 2026-10-12). NCSC Switzerland and CERT-EU both published advisories on 2026-10-12 (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-12; CERT-EU, 2026-10-12). Rapid7 reproduced the code execution on 19.4.1 and published a proof of concept on 2026-10-13 (Rapid7, 2026-10-13); CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 2026-10-29 on evidence of active exploitation (CISA, 2026-10-29), and NCSC Switzerland updated its advisory the same day to active exploitation, citing Alpine Linux (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-29). Alpine says a newly registered account sent crafted CI configurations to its GitLab 19.3.4 on 2026-10-20 and 21, crashing worker processes eight times, four of them partway through the exploit; it found no evidence that code ran, could not rule it out and rebuilt the instance (Alpine Linux, 2026-10-28).

    Exposure
    Self-managed GitLab CE or EE on 19.2.x below 19.2.8, 19.3.x below 19.3.5 or 19.4.x below 19.4.2; GitLab's table starts at 19.2 and lists no earlier branch (GitLab, 2026-10-12). NCSC Switzerland states the prerequisites as network access to the instance and delivery of a crafted regular expression through CI/CD configuration (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-12); Rapid7 found the conditions: an attacker must first commit a malicious CI/CD configuration file and a repository path containing UTF-8 characters to a branch in a public project, which an ordinary account can do by creating a public project where the instance allows it (Rapid7, 2026-10-13), and on instances where new users can self-register and create projects that constraint is removed (Rapid7, 2026-10-13). An instance with open sign-up, or with public projects that untrusted users can write to, is the case to prioritise.
    Detection
    GitLab's release page lists six detections for exploitation attempts on self-managed instances (GitLab, 2026-10-12). Alpine saw the attack as crashes of GitLab worker processes after crafted CI configurations from a newly registered account, with 502 errors and failed CI jobs, and notes that a successful exploit would leave no crash and no trace in the web logs (Alpine Linux, 2026-10-28); in process-creation telemetry, shells, interpreters or network tools started as children of the GitLab application server processes are the sign of success, and a server where that turns up needs its CI/CD variables, runner registration tokens and deploy keys treated as exposed.

    Triage: ordinary CI jobs run shells under the runners; only a shell spawned by the GitLab application server processes themselves matches this flaw, not one started inside a runner job.

    Defender takeaway
    Upgrade to 19.4.2, 19.3.5 or 19.2.8 now (GitLab, 2026-10-12), or better to 19.5.1, 19.4.3, 19.3.6 or 19.2.9, the builds of 2026-10-21 that also fix a second CVSS 10 regular-expression compiler flaw, CVE-2026-110264 (GitLab, 2026-10-21), internet-facing instances first as CERT-EU advises (CERT-EU, 2026-10-12), then instances where new users can self-register and create projects, which removes the push-access constraint Rapid7 found (Rapid7, 2026-10-13); the fix is a set of patches to the Ruby that GitLab bundles in its own packages and images, so an installation with its own Ruby, from source or in a self-built image, needs Ruby 3.3.13, 3.4.12 or 4.0.9 as well, and Alpine's GitLab on 19.3.6 with standard Ruby stayed vulnerable (Alpine Linux, 2026-10-28). CISA lists the flaw as exploited and NCSC Switzerland reports active exploitation, so an instance reachable on an affected version needs a compromise assessment as well as the upgrade; Rapid7's interim controls (restrict anonymous access to public projects and stop untrusted users creating or writing to public projects) reduce exposure only to the path it demonstrated (Rapid7, 2026-10-13).
