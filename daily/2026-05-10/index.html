    JDownloader website compromise: "Download Alternative Installer" and Linux shell installer links pointed to malware on 6 and 7 May 2026

    Attackers exploited an unpatched vulnerability in the website of JDownloader, a free download manager with millions of users across Windows, macOS and Linux (CyberKendra, 2026-05-08), that let them change access control lists and content without authentication (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-09). After a test on a low-traffic page at about 23:55 UTC on 2026-05-05, they repointed the Windows "Download Alternative Installer" links and the Linux shell installer link to unrelated malicious files at about 00:01 UTC on 2026-05-06; the developers were alerted through Reddit on 2026-05-07 and shut the server down that day, and the site returned with verified links on the night of 8 to 9 May (JDownloader). The genuine installer packages were not modified, the changes stayed within the content management system without access to the host file system, and the other installers on the site, the RSA-signed built-in updater and the winget, Flatpak, Snap and Docker channels were not affected (JDownloader). The developers said the malicious Windows installers lacked a valid digital signature (Security Affairs, 2026-05-10), and users reported unfamiliar publisher names such as Zipline LLC, The Water Team and Peace Team (PiunikaWeb, 2026-05-08).

    Researcher Thomas Klemenc found the Windows files to be a loader for a heavily obfuscated Python-based RAT that runs Python code delivered from its C2 servers; the Linux installer downloads an archive disguised as an SVG file, installs one of two extracted binaries as a SUID-root binary under /usr/bin, creates a persistence script under /etc/profile.d and launches a Pyarmor-obfuscated payload masquerading as a system daemon (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-09). An ANY.RUN analysis shows an 8-minute delay before the payload activates (Security Affairs, 2026-05-10).

    Exposure
    Machines on which an installer downloaded through the "Download Alternative Installer" links or the Linux shell installer link of jdownloader.org on 6 or 7 May 2026 (UTC) was run; downloading without running, other installers from the site, in-app updates and store or package channels are not at risk (JDownloader).
    Detection
    Code-signing state in process and file telemetry: a JDownloader installer that is unsigned or signed by anyone other than AppWork GmbH (JDownloader); on Linux hosts that ran the shell installer, a new script under /etc/profile.d and a new SUID-root binary under /usr/bin (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-09).

    Triage: genuine JDownloader installers are signed by AppWork GmbH; the same installer unsigned or carrying another publisher's name is the substitute (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-09).

    Defender takeaway
    On any machine where a substitute installer ran, reinstall the operating system and change passwords for important accounts from another device, as JDownloader advises (JDownloader); machines that only took in-app or store updates need no action.

    Groupe 3R (Réseau Radiologique Romand): Akira ransomware attack on 20 Swiss imaging centres, administrative data stolen and published, medical data encrypted

    Groupe 3R (Réseau Radiologique Romand), which runs 20 medical-imaging centres in seven Swiss cantons (ICTjournal.ch, 2026-05-06), lists its centres in Vaud, Valais, Fribourg, Geneva, Neuchâtel, Berne and Zurich (Groupe 3R). It was hit on Thursday 2026-04-30 by a ransomware attack with data encryption; patient care was affected above all on 1 and 2 May, all centres stayed open, the attack was reported to the Federal Office for Cybersecurity, a criminal complaint was filed and no ransom will be paid (Groupe 3R). Akira listed the operator on its leak site, where ransomware.live recorded it on 2026-05-08 with an estimated attack date of 2026-04-30 (ransomware.live, 2026-05-08), announcing it would upload 48 GB of corporate data including employee identity documents, patient information, payment details and NDAs (DeXpose, 2026-05-09).

    Groupe 3R now states that administrative data, including personal information of current and former employees, was encrypted and stolen, that medical data (exam results) was encrypted and made unusable, and that it is not possible to say with certainty whether patient medical data was also stolen (Groupe 3R). It acknowledged that internal documents stolen in the attack were published on the darknet, and attributes the attack to Akira because the gang's methods match what its specialist cybersecurity partners observed (ICTjournal.ch, 2026-07-06). This is its second attack in about a year: in April 2025 attackers stole medical and administrative data without disrupting operations (SwissCybersecurity.net, 2026-05-07), and Groupe 3R says the two attacks have different perpetrators and methods (Groupe 3R). Since 2026-06-26 all centres run on a rebuilt IT infrastructure with RIS, PACS, telephony and teleradiology restored, although some past examination data was still inaccessible in the operator's update of 2026-09-22 (Groupe 3R). No source states how the attackers got in; CISA's Akira advisory describes initial access mostly through VPN services using known CVEs, and encryption that is full or partial depending on file type and size (CISA AA24-109A).

    Exposure
    Current and former Groupe 3R employees, whose personal information was stolen, and patients of its centres, whose medical data was encrypted and may or may not have been taken (Groupe 3R).
    Defender takeaway
    Groupe 3R expects stolen data to be used for fraud such as fake supplementary invoices by email or post, and asks patients to check any invoice with it; patients insured under the LAMal system normally receive only a copy of the invoice sent to their insurer (Groupe 3R). With no access vector public, the case changes no patching or detection decision for other healthcare operators.

    Braintrust, an AI evaluation and observability platform, discovered on 2026-05-04, after a report of suspicious behaviour, that attackers had accessed one of its AWS accounts (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-08). It emailed customers on Monday 2026-05-04 and disclosed the incident on its website on Tuesday 2026-05-05 (TechCrunch, 2026-05-06); SecurityWeek dates the customer email to 2026-05-05 (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-08).     Exposure
    Organisations that stored org-level AI provider keys in Braintrust; Braintrust informed all org admins with stored AI provider secrets (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-08).
    Detection
    AI provider usage and billing telemetry for the keys that were stored in Braintrust: the signal three customers reported was a suspicious spike in AI provider usage (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-08).
    Defender takeaway
    Braintrust customers delete or revoke the existing AI provider secrets on the org-level settings page, configure new ones and confirm the rotation from their timestamps, as Braintrust recommends (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-08), and review the provider-side usage of the old keys up to their revocation; organisations that stored no keys in Braintrust have no decision to make.

    Microsoft Semantic Kernel CVE-2026-26030 / CVE-2026-25592: prompt injection reaches eval() and the host Startup folder in an AI agent framework, fixed since February

    Microsoft's Defender research team published on 2026-05-07 how two Semantic Kernel flaws turn a prompt injection into code execution on the host running an AI agent (Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-05-07). Both were fixed months earlier: CVE-2026-25592 in February (GitHub Security Advisory GHSA-2ww3-72rp-wpp4, 2026-02-06) and CVE-2026-26030 later the same month (GitHub Security Advisory GHSA-xjw9-4gw8-4rqx, 2026-02-19).

    CVE-2026-26030 (Python semantic-kernel before 1.39.4) sits in the In-Memory Vector Store filter behind the Search Plugin: in the default configuration the filter is a Python lambda executed with eval(), and an AI-controlled value is interpolated into it (Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-05-07). Microsoft's exploit prompt escaped the template string, traversed Python's class hierarchy to BuiltinImporter, loaded os and called system(), bypassing the AST-based blocklist; the fix adds node-type and function-call allowlists and blocks class-hierarchy attributes (Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-05-07). The attack needs a prompt-injection vector and the Search Plugin backed by the In-Memory Vector Store in its default configuration (Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-05-07), and the advisory advises against using the In-Memory Vector Store in production (GitHub Security Advisory GHSA-xjw9-4gw8-4rqx, 2026-02-19).

    CVE-2026-25592 (.NET Microsoft.SemanticKernel.Plugins.Core before 1.71.0) exposed DownloadFileAsync to the model as a kernel function, so a prompt could have the agent write a script created inside the Azure Container Apps sandbox into the host's Windows Startup folder, where it runs at the next sign-in; upload_file in both SDKs similarly accepted any local path, allowing host files to be read out (Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-05-07). The advisory's workaround is a Function Invocation Filter that allow-lists localFilePath for the download and upload functions (GitHub Security Advisory GHSA-2ww3-72rp-wpp4, 2026-02-06). No source reports exploitation.

    Exposure
    Agents built on Python semantic-kernel before 1.39.4 using the In-Memory Vector Store filter, or on the .NET Core plugins before 1.71.0 using the sessions Python plugin (Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-05-07).
    Detection
    In process telemetry, a Semantic Kernel host process (a Python interpreter or dotnet.exe) spawning a shell or download utility is what Microsoft's hunting queries look for, and Microsoft names an agent process dropping scripts into a Startup folder as behaviour endpoint defences should catch (Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-05-07).
    Defender takeaway
    The model is not a security boundary; any agent tool that reaches eval() or the host file system needs the same input validation as an internet-facing endpoint, and these two fixes have been available since February (GitHub, 2026-02-06) (GitHub, 2026-02-19).
    Correction11 Oct 2026 14:44 UTCrun 2026-10-11T1307Z-audittitleheadlinesummarytechniquesaffected_productscvessourcesclassificationbodydeep_divedeep_dive_category

    In CVE-2026-25592, DownloadFileAsync is what writes attacker content to the host, including the Startup folder, and upload_file is what reads host files out (Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-05-07); the earlier text had the two reversed. CVE-2026-26030 passes an interpolated lambda to eval() behind an AST-based blocklist, which Microsoft bypassed through BuiltinImporter (Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-05-07).     cPanel & WHM security update of 2026-05-08: CVE-2026-29202 Perl code injection, CVE-2026-29203 symlink chmod and CVE-2026-29201 arbitrary file read

    cPanel published patches and one advisory per CVE for three cPanel & WHM and WP Squared vulnerabilities on 2026-05-08 (cPanel, CVE-2026-29202). CVE-2026-29202 is a Perl code-injection flaw in the plugin parameter of the create_user API call (cPanel, CVE-2026-29202) that gives arbitrary Perl code execution as the already authenticated account's system user (The Hacker News, 2026-05-09). CVE-2026-29203 is an unsafe symlink handling error that lets a user chmod an arbitrary file, allowing denial of service and possible privilege escalation (cPanel, CVE-2026-29203). CVE-2026-29201 is an arbitrary file read in the feature::LOADFEATUREFILE adminbin call, where a relative path in the feature file name makes an arbitrary file world-readable (cPanel, CVE-2026-29201). The CVE records, as ENISA's EUVD carries them, score CVE-2026-29202 and CVE-2026-29203 at 5.3 under CVSS 4.0 (EUVD-2026-28811; EUVD-2026-28812) and CVE-2026-29201 at 8.6 under CVSS 3.1 with no privileges required (EUVD-2026-28810); The Hacker News reports 8.8, 8.8 and 4.3, and no evidence of exploitation in the wild as of 2026-05-09 (The Hacker News, 2026-05-09).

    The fixed releases are cPanel & WHM version 11.136.0.9, 11.134.0.25, 11.132.0.31, 11.130.0.22, 11.126.0.58, 11.124.0.37, 11.118.0.66, 11.110.0.117, 11.102.0.41, 11.94.0.30 and 11.86.0.43 or higher, WP Squared version 11.136.1.11 or higher, and version 11.110.0.116 as a direct update for CentOS 6 and CloudLinux 6 (cPanel, CVE-2026-29202). The update followed by ten days cPanel's advisory for CVE-2026-41940, an authentication bypass (cPanel, CVE-2026-41940) that hosting provider KnownHost reported exploited in the wild since 2026-02-23 (SecurityWeek, 2026-04-30); Shadowserver counted at least 44,000 IP addresses likely compromised through it scanning its honeypots on 2026-04-30 (The Hacker News, 2026-05-04).

    Exposure
    Every cPanel & WHM and WP Squared server below the fixed release of its branch; CVE-2026-29202 needs an authenticated account (The Hacker News, 2026-05-09) and CVE-2026-29203 a user on the server (cPanel, CVE-2026-29203). The patch ships through the standard automatic update, so servers with automatic updates disabled or version-pinned in the update configuration are the ones left behind (NCSC-CH, 2026-05-08), and the running version is read with /usr/local/cpanel/cpanel -V (cPanel, CVE-2026-29202).
    Detection
    File-permission telemetry on the server: the two file flaws show as system files made world-readable through the adminbin call (cPanel, CVE-2026-29201) or chmod'ed through a symlink by a user account (cPanel, CVE-2026-29203).
    Defender takeaway
    No out-of-band action where automatic updates run; on pinned or manually updated servers run /scripts/upcp --force and confirm the version afterwards, as cPanel instructs (cPanel, CVE-2026-29202).

    Bauman University "Department No. 4": leaked documents show a GRU training pipeline that places graduates in Sandworm and Fancy Bear units

    Department No. 4 of the military training centre at Bauman Moscow State Technical University trains hackers and saboteurs for the GRU, according to a joint investigation by The Insider, The Guardian, Le Monde, Der Spiegel, Frontstory and VSquare whose journalists reviewed more than two thousand internal documents from the department (Meduza, 2026-05-07); the Guardian names Delfi among the six outlets and says the files cover several years of activity up to 2025 (The Guardian, 2026-05-07). The documents indicate that the GRU sends its own officers to conduct exams, approve candidates and oversee placements (The Guardian, 2026-05-07). A 144-hour course over two semesters, "Defence against technical reconnaissance", teaches password attacks, software vulnerabilities and trojans, and students must carry out practical penetration tests and develop a computer virus to pass (The Guardian, 2026-05-07). Heise, citing Der Spiegel, adds DDoS attacks, the hacking of a test server at the end of the course and a one-month training camp that all students attend (heise online, 2026-05-07). Students study the protected networks of the US Department of Defense and are trained to exploit vulnerabilities in them with Metasploit (VSquare, 2026-05-07).

    Between 10 and 15 students from the department are assigned to GRU units each year, some to military unit 74455, which belongs to Sandworm, and others to Fancy Bear (Meduza, 2026-05-07). The Guardian traces one 2024 graduate to Fancy Bear and another to Unit 74455 in Anapa, and names Viktor Netyksho, a former commander of Fancy Bear's Unit 26165, among the teachers (The Guardian, 2026-05-07). The operations attached to the two units are western accusations, and the outlets differ on one of them: the Guardian says Sandworm has been accused of targeting Ukraine's power grid in 2015 and Emmanuel Macron's 2017 presidential campaign (The Guardian, 2026-05-07), while Le Monde says Fancy Bear (APT28) is accused of stealing emails from Macron's team in early 2017 and, more recently, of targeting institutions involved in organising the Paris 2024 Olympic Games (Le Monde, 2026-05-07). Heise reports, citing Der Spiegel, that one graduate apparently joined a unit said to be behind the 2017 NotPetya malware (heise online, 2026-05-07). Bauman is one pipeline among several: the Guardian's source, a former senior Russian defence official, calls it one of a handful of elite universities used to identify gifted students and says another university, Mirea, was even more crucial in training hackers (The Guardian, 2026-05-07).

    Defender takeaway
    No change to detection or patching priorities; the files show Sandworm and Fancy Bear staffed through a supervised university course, and the latest cohort of trainees does not graduate until the end of the 2027 academic year (The Guardian, 2026-05-07).

    macOS infostealer campaign shifts to ClickFix Terminal commands to deliver MacSync, SHub Stealer and AMOS

    Microsoft reported on 2026-05-06 that an infostealer campaign targeting macOS, whose earlier iterations delivered the stealers in disk-image (.dmg) files that users installed by hand, now instructs users to run Terminal commands that retrieve remote content with native utilities and then execute a script-based loader (Microsoft, 2026-05-06). The ClickFix-style instructions sit on standalone sites, on the note-taking platform Craft and in fake troubleshooting posts on Medium that promise to fix common macOS problems such as low disk space, and the pasted Base64 command decodes and runs an AppleScript or Bash loader (Microsoft, 2026-05-06). Scripts launched from Terminal do not undergo the code-signing and notarization evaluation Gatekeeper can apply to application bundles opened through Finder (Microsoft, 2026-05-06).

    Microsoft tracks three execution paths. In the loader campaign, seen since February 2026, curl fetches a loader, a zsh script decodes a Base64 and Gzip payload and runs it with eval, fingerprints the host, stops on a Russian or CIS keyboard layout and runs a remote AppleScript stealer in memory through osascript; the stealer, which calls itself SHub Stealer, asks for the user's password as if installing a helper, validates it with dscl, stages browser credentials, Keychain entries, iCloud and Telegram data, wallets and small documents in a temporary folder, compresses and exfiltrates them and deletes the staging files, then persists as a LaunchAgent disguised as a Google software-update agent that runs Base64 commands returned by its C2 (Microsoft, 2026-05-06). The script campaign, seen in April 2026, streams a remote script straight into the shell from a one-line pipeline and persists an obfuscated AppleScript in a LaunchAgent that probes hard-coded C2 candidates with curl, falls back to a server identifier hidden on a Telegram bot page and pipes the server's response into osascript (Microsoft, 2026-05-06). The helper campaign (AMOS), seen since late January 2026, decodes and gunzips a first stage that downloads a Mach-O executable, strips its extended attributes, checks for virtualisation with system_profiler, copies Keychain databases and Chromium browser passwords and session cookies, exfiltrates a ZIP archive and installs a LaunchDaemon that runs a bash wrapper with root privileges at every boot to keep a backdoor running (Microsoft, 2026-05-06). In the loader and helper campaigns the stealer replaces installed Ledger Wallet and Trezor Suite apps, and in the loader campaign also Exodus, with trojanized versions fetched from its C2 (Microsoft, 2026-05-06); Malwarebytes documented SHub Stealer modifying wallet apps after a Terminal-paste lure on a fake CleanMyMac site in March 2026 (Malwarebytes, 2026-03-06).

    Exposure
    Mac users who can paste commands into Terminal; Apple has updated XProtect signatures, and macOS 26.4 and later blocks the paste of a potentially malicious command into Terminal, so earlier releases lack that mitigation (Microsoft, 2026-05-06).
    Detection
    Process lineage: Terminal or shell sessions spawned by installers or user apps, and unusual sequences of curl, base64, gunzip, osascript and dscl; file telemetry for LaunchAgent and LaunchDaemon plists written by script interpreters; archive creation of collected data followed by an HTTP POST with curl (Microsoft, 2026-05-06).
    Defender takeaway
    Managed Macs on macOS 26.4 or later get Apple's paste blocking against this delivery; on a Mac where the chain ran, treat the Keychain, browser passwords and session cookies as stolen and reinstall wallet apps from their vendors, because the stealer replaces Ledger Wallet, Trezor Suite and Exodus with trojanized copies (Microsoft, 2026-05-06).

    Sophos: "Beagle" backdoor distributed via fake Claude AI site using DonutLoader + DLL sideloading on a signed G DATA AV updater

    Sophos X-Ops published a write-up on 2026-05-07 of a counterfeit Claude AI site distributing a previously undocumented Windows backdoor it named Beagle, and assesses that the site is likely part of an active malvertising campaign (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07). The chain delivers a roughly 505 MB ZIP archive containing a malicious MSI that drops a legitimate, signed G DATA antivirus updater executable, an encrypted data file and an attacker-controlled DLL into the user's startup folder, so the updater sideloads the malicious DLL (DLL side-loading) (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07). The DLL decrypts the data file and executes the resulting Donut (DonutLoader) shellcode, an open-source in-memory loader, which loads Beagle (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07). Beagle communicates with a subdomain of the same lookalike site over TCP/443 and UDP/8080 with AES-encrypted payloads, and its eight commands are uninstall, cmd, upload, download, mkdir, rename, ls and rm (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07). Sophos notes that an almost identical G DATA sideloading chain has previously been associated with PlugX, which several threat groups use, and suggests the Beagle payload may reflect an actor retooling or imitating another actor's chain. It does not attribute the campaign (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07). A possible hosting server was set up in March 2026, and Sophos found related samples reusing the same XOR key from February, March and April 2026, while cautioning that further evidence would be needed to link them to the same actor (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07).

    Sophos places the campaign in a wider pattern of threat actors crafting lures that imitate legitimate AI sites, often through malvertising (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07).

    Contradiction: Malwarebytes, describing the same archive, identifies the payload as PlugX, by analogy with an earlier campaign that used the same G DATA sideloading files (Malwarebytes, 2026-04-10), while Sophos analysed it as a previously undocumented backdoor it named Beagle (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07).

    Exposure
    Windows hosts where a user ran a Claude installer taken from a lookalike site or an ad rather than the official download page. The trojanized installer puts a working copy of Claude under a misspelled lookalike of the Claude directory name inside the Anthropic program folder, which Malwarebytes gives as a check alongside the sideloading files in the user's Startup folder (Malwarebytes, 2026-04-10).
    Detection
    In file-creation and process telemetry, a signed G DATA antivirus updater, an encrypted data file and a DLL written together into a user's Startup folder after the fake installer's MSI runs (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07). Malwarebytes, describing the same archive, adds that the MSI installs a desktop shortcut that launches a VBScript dropper, run through WScript.exe, which copies the three files into the Startup folder, and that the updater made an outbound connection within seconds (22 seconds in its sandbox run). The dropper script deletes itself, so the Startup-folder files and the running updater process are what persist (Malwarebytes, 2026-04-10).

    Triage: the updater is a legitimately signed G DATA binary, so the parent process can look benign to endpoint tools; by inference from Malwarebytes' sandbox and dropper-script analysis, the discriminator is the sequence, in which WScript.exe writes the updater, the DLL it loads from its own directory and an encrypted data file into a user's Startup folder and starts the updater with a hidden window while the freshly installed Claude application opens in the foreground (Malwarebytes, 2026-04-10).

    Defender takeaway
    Treat AI-tool installers as a high-risk download class. Sophos advises downloading Claude only from the legitimate site and being wary of links from ads and sponsored search results (Sophos X-Ops, 2026-05-07), and Malwarebytes names claude.com/download as the official download page (Malwarebytes, 2026-04-10).
