    CERTFR-2026-ACT-016: CERT-FR says autonomous AI assistants such as OpenClaw must not be deployed on workstations until proven secure

    CERT-FR's advisory CERTFR-2026-ACT-016, published on 2026-04-13, addresses autonomous personal assistants such as OpenClaw, Claude Cowork and open-source equivalents, whose adoption has grown strongly since early 2026. Unlike chat assistants, they execute commands, control the browser, read and write files, manage calendars and send mail, triggered by a text message through applications such as Slack, WhatsApp or Discord (CERT-FR, 2026-04-13). Plugins can be loaded dynamically and, depending on the integration model, run as trusted code with the application's privileges, which widens the software supply chain; prompt injection is an intrinsic weakness of the underlying language models, and an agent that orchestrates operating-system-level tools raises the risk of unauthorized command execution and data exfiltration through prompt injection or hijacked messages (CERT-FR, 2026-04-13). The advisory's risk list has five items: compromise of the user's workstation through a vulnerability in these tools, most of them still in beta; leakage of sensitive data by the agents to uncontrolled external resources; excessive access rights over all of the user's office applications (mail, calendar, file manager, business and HR applications); sharing of login credentials with the agents and their potential leakage; and loss of control over the agents' actions on the workstation, including destructive actions that damage the integrity or availability of business applications or data (CERT-FR, 2026-04-13).

    CERT-FR's position is that such assistants must not be deployed on workstations and their use should be prohibited until the product is stabilised and proven from a security standpoint; they must in no case be deployed in production, only in isolated test sandboxes holding no sensitive data, and the same applies to mobile deployments (CERT-FR, 2026-04-13). Where they are used, the main lever is configuration hardening: limit the tools and actions available to the strict minimum, require human validation for any system command or action with side effects, run the agent in a sandbox, and allowlist the channels and interlocutors that may trigger it, for example only on an explicit mention of the agent (CERT-FR, 2026-04-13).

    Exposure
    Any workstation or phone where users have installed an autonomous assistant with access to mail, calendar, files or business applications, typically as unvalidated Shadow IT (CERT-FR, 2026-04-13).
    Defender takeaway
    Treat autonomous desktop AI assistants as unapproved software on production workstations: block installations not validated by IT and security, and allow them only in isolated sandboxes without production data, secrets or personal data (CERT-FR, 2026-04-13).

    Poland's ABW: attackers breached five municipal water treatment plants in 2025 and in some cases altered equipment parameters, and hacktivists exploited weak passwords on exposed management panels at municipal sites

    Poland's Internal Security Agency (ABW) reports in its 2024-2025 activity review that in 2025 "security breaches were reported at water treatment plants in the following towns: Jabłonna Lacka, Szczytno, Małdyty, Tolkmicko and Sierakowo", and that "by gaining access, in some cases, to industrial control systems, the attackers were able to alter the technical parameters of the equipment", a direct risk to the plants' continued operation and to water supply (ABW, 2026-05-25). ABW describes hacktivist groups as particularly active against municipal infrastructure and says they "exploited glaring vulnerabilities in the form of poor password policies and unsecured device management panels, accessible directly via the public internet" (ABW, 2026-05-25). The report names no actor and no access vector for the water-plant breaches and does not tie them to the hacktivist activity. The state-sponsored groups it names, APT28, APT29 and UNC1151, are described as carrying out "sophisticated, long-term espionage and sabotage operations" (ABW, 2026-05-25). ABW's press office told CyberDefence24 that the potentially compromised water and sewage operators had IT resources reachable from the public internet, including HMI panels controlling their processes (CyberDefence24, 2025-10-08). CyberDefence24, which lists the five plants among the sites hit, describes the attacks on the Polish water sector as the work of pro-Russian hacktivists (CyberDefence24, 2025-10-08).

    Exposure
    Municipal infrastructure operators, which ABW says include sewage and water treatment plants and waste incinerators, whose device management panels answer from the internet and accept weak passwords, the weaknesses ABW says hacktivist groups exploited (ABW, 2026-05-25).
    Detection
    Successful logins to HMI, PLC or device management interfaces from internet addresses, and changes to setpoints or equipment parameters that match no operator log.
    Defender takeaway
    ABW's report does not say how the five plants were breached, but its press office points to internet-reachable IT resources and HMI panels at the potentially compromised operators (CyberDefence24, 2025-10-08). The route ABW describes for hacktivists against municipal infrastructure, an internet-reachable management panel with a weak password (ABW, 2026-05-25), is closed by removing direct internet exposure of OT interfaces and enforcing unique strong credentials on them.

    Rapid7 links a Chaos-branded extortion intrusion to MuddyWater with moderate confidence: Teams screen-sharing harvested credentials and MFA, and no files were encrypted

    Rapid7 investigated an early-2026 intrusion that presented as an attack by the Chaos ransomware-as-a-service operation and assesses, "with moderate confidence", that it is linked to MuddyWater (Seedworm), the Iranian state-sponsored group, on the strength of a code-signing certificate and command-and-control infrastructure (Rapid7, 2026-05-06). The actor opened external one-on-one Microsoft Teams chats, moved victims into screen-sharing sessions, had them type credentials into locally created text files and change their MFA configuration to include attacker-controlled devices, then established persistence with the remote management tools DWAgent and AnyDesk (Rapid7, 2026-05-06). It authenticated to internal systems, including a domain controller, with several compromised accounts and deployed a downloader for the Darkcomp backdoor that Rapid7 says is firmly attributed to MuddyWater (Rapid7, 2026-05-06). The actor exfiltrated data and demanded ransom under the Chaos brand by email, but forwent file encryption in favour of data theft and long-term persistence (Rapid7, 2026-05-06). Rapid7 reads the false flag as deniability for espionage and prepositioning, particularly in the US; Chaos itself had claimed 36 victims by late March 2026 (Rapid7, 2026-05-06). The Hacker News reported the research the same day (The Hacker News, 2026-05-06).

    Detection
    In collaboration-platform audit telemetry, an external-tenant Teams one-on-one chat that moves to screen sharing, followed by a new MFA device registration for the same user, is the chain Rapid7 describes (Rapid7, 2026-05-06). On endpoints, DWAgent or AnyDesk installed outside change control and credential text files created during a support session are the follow-on signals (Rapid7, 2026-05-06).

    Triage: legitimate external Teams support sessions exist; the discriminator is an MFA method added during or right after a chat with an external tenant, a step a genuine support session has no reason to include.

    Defender takeaway
    An extortion demand with no encryption is not proof of a criminal actor. Restrict external Teams chat and screen sharing to approved tenants, and require out-of-band verification before any MFA change requested in a chat.

    CVE-2026-6973: Ivanti EPMM admin-authenticated RCE exploited in limited attacks, fixed with four further EPMM flaws including unauthenticated Sentry certificate issuance (CVE-2026-5787)

    Ivanti's May 2026 update for on-premises Endpoint Manager Mobile (EPMM) fixes five vulnerabilities. Ivanti states: "At the time of disclosure, we are aware of very limited exploitation of CVE-2026-6973, which requires admin authentication for successful exploitation", and "We are not aware of any customers being exploited by the other vulnerabilities disclosed today"; Ivanti Neurons for MDM, Ivanti EPM and Ivanti Sentry are not affected (Ivanti, 2026-05-07). CVE-2026-6973 (CVSS 7.2) is improper input validation that lets "a remotely authenticated user with administrative access to achieve remote code execution", fixed in 12.6.1.1, 12.7.0.1 and 12.8.0.1 (The Hacker News, 2026-05-07), and CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day (CISA KEV catalog, 2026-05-07).

    The admin-login precondition is less reassuring than it looks. CERT-FR recalls that its guidance for January's exploited EPMM flaws CVE-2026-1281 and CVE-2026-1340 was to reset every local EPMM account password, and warns that where this was not done, attackers can reuse administrator passwords obtained then to exploit CVE-2026-6973 (CERT-FR, 2026-05-07). Ivanti makes the same link, saying customers who rotated credentials after January face a significantly reduced risk (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-07).

    The four companion flaws are not reported exploited: CVE-2026-5786 (CVSS 8.8) lets an authenticated attacker gain administrative access, CVE-2026-5787 (CVSS 8.9) lets an unauthenticated attacker impersonate registered Sentry hosts and obtain valid CA-signed client certificates, CVE-2026-5788 (CVSS 7.0) lets an unauthenticated attacker invoke arbitrary methods, and CVE-2026-7821 (CVSS 7.4) lets an unauthenticated attacker enroll a device from a restricted set, exposing information about the appliance (The Hacker News, 2026-05-07). Shadowserver counted over 850 exposed EPMM instances, 508 of them in Europe and 182 in North America (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-07). No one has attributed the exploitation; SecurityWeek notes only that Chinese actors are often believed to be behind Ivanti zero-days (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-08).

    Exposure
    On-premises EPMM below 12.6.1.1, 12.7.0.1 or 12.8.0.1, most acutely appliances that were exploited in the January CVE-2026-1281/1340 wave and whose admin passwords were never reset. CVE-2026-7821 affects only deployments that use and have configured Apple Device Enrollment (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-07).
    Detection
    Administrative API sessions from sources outside the management network or at unusual hours, followed by the EPMM service spawning shells or making outbound connections, and for CVE-2026-5787, client certificates issued to Sentry hosts that do not exist in the deployment.
    Defender takeaway
    Patching closes all five flaws, but only a credential reset evicts an attacker who already holds a January-era admin password, so do both on any appliance that was internet-facing during January. Ivanti advises reviewing every account with admin rights and rotating those credentials where necessary (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-07). The May builds are not the end of the patch path: the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security reports that Ivanti's critical EPMM update of 2026-06-09 covers versions 12.9.0, 12.8.0.2, 12.7.0.1 and prior (Canadian Centre for Cyber Security, 2026-06-09).

    Phishing and BEC through hijacked Amazon SES accounts: leaked IAM keys let attackers send mail that passes SPF, DKIM and DMARC

    Kaspersky reports that phishing sent through Amazon Simple Email Service (SES) rose in January 2026 and stayed steady through the first quarter (Kaspersky Securelist, 2026-05-04). Attackers mostly reach SES through leaked AWS IAM access keys that developers leave in public GitHub repositories, ENV files, Docker images, configuration backups or publicly accessible S3 buckets; bots built on the open-source secret scanner TruffleHog find the keys, and the attackers then check each key's permissions and sending limits (Kaspersky Securelist, 2026-05-04). Because the mail is sent from legitimate infrastructure, it passes SPF, DKIM and DMARC, almost always carries .amazonses.com in its Message-ID header, and comes from sender IPs that reputation blocklists cannot block without blocking all SES mail (Kaspersky Securelist, 2026-05-04).

    A common early-2026 lure was a fake electronic-signature notification whose link led to a sign-in form hosted on amazonaws.com (Kaspersky Securelist, 2026-05-04). In a business-email-compromise case Kaspersky investigated, an email posing as an employee went to the company's finance department with a fabricated thread about an outstanding invoice and PDF attachments holding only payment details and supporting documentation, with no malicious link or QR code (Kaspersky Securelist, 2026-05-04). BleepingComputer reported the research the same day (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-04).

    Exposure
    Any AWS account whose IAM access keys have leaked into a public repository, container image, backup or bucket can be turned into a phishing sender; an organization is exposed both as the owner of such keys and as a recipient of SES-sent mail (Kaspersky Securelist, 2026-05-04).
    Detection
    In mail-gateway telemetry, SES-sent messages almost always carry .amazonses.com in the Message-ID header; a message from an unfamiliar SES sender whose link resolves to a sign-in form on amazonaws.com, or an invoice thread to finance with PDFs holding only payment details and supporting documentation, is the pattern Kaspersky describes (Kaspersky Securelist, 2026-05-04). On the AWS side, in analyst judgement, SES sending activity by an IAM key that has no history of sending mail is the signal for a hijacked key.
    Defender takeaway
    Authentication results do not separate this mail from legitimate SES mail, so payment-change and invoice requests need out-of-band verification regardless of a passing DMARC result. For your own AWS accounts, Kaspersky advises least-privilege IAM keys, roles instead of long-lived keys, MFA, IP restrictions and automated key rotation with regular audits (Kaspersky Securelist, 2026-05-04).
