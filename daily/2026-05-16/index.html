    Suspected Dream Market main administrator "Speedstepper" arrested in Germany; US indictment traces reactivated marketplace wallets and gold purchases

    On 2026-05-07, in a coordinated effort by German and US law enforcement, Owe Martin Andresen, 49, was arrested in Germany on German charges, and his residence and two other locations were searched (US DEA, 2026-05-13). A US federal grand jury had indicted him on 2026-01-13 on six counts of international concealment money laundering and six counts of concealment money laundering, each carrying up to 20 years, and Germany has charged him with several counts of concealment money laundering, each punishable by up to five years (US DEA, 2026-05-13). The DOJ indictment, unsealed in the week The Record reported it, accuses him of being the main administrator of Dream Market, a darknet marketplace launched in 2013 that announced its shutdown in 2019 (The Record, 2026-05-13); the release calls him the suspected main administrator, the role whose holder used the moniker "Speedstepper" and was not identified in the earlier prosecutions (US DEA, 2026-05-13). The case is investigated by the IRS-CI Cyber Crimes Unit and the DEA Miami Counternarcotic Cyber Investigations Task Force with substantial assistance from the Bundeskriminalamt Cybercrime Unit and the Zentrale Kriminalinspektion Oldenburg; the FBI, USPIS and HSI were part of the task force that investigated the marketplace between 2016 and 2022, whose prosecutions convicted the administrators "Oxymonster" and "KITT3N" in the US and the mid-level administrator "GOWRON" in the UK (US DEA, 2026-05-13).

    Prosecutors allege that in November and December 2022 Andresen moved funds from the marketplace's dormant commission wallets into new consolidated wallets, activity that could only have been initiated by someone holding Dream Market's original private keys, and that in August 2023 he used an Atlanta-based cryptocurrency service provider to buy gold bars that were shipped to his home address in Germany; in total he is alleged to have laundered over $2 million between August 2023 and April 2025 (US DEA, 2026-05-13). The US Attorney says the commissions came from selling illegal drugs, stolen personally identifiable information, counterfeit identification documents and other items (US DEA, 2026-05-13). US agencies had kept watching the marketplace's wallets after the shutdown and found them untouched until then (The Record, 2026-05-13). The searches located about $1.7 million of gold bars and over $23,000 in cash, along with information identifying bank accounts and cryptocurrency wallets holding about $1.2 million believed to be Dream Market proceeds (US DEA, 2026-05-13).

    Defender takeaway
    No change for enterprise defenders, since the sources describe the laundering of marketplace proceeds and no intrusion technique. For financial-crime and cryptocurrency-tracing teams the case shows that wallets left untouched for three years stayed under watch, and IRS-CI draws the lesson that "illicit actors may hide in the shadows, but their financial footprints remain" (US DEA, 2026-05-13).

    node-ipc npm package backdoored, likely via an expired-domain account takeover: three malicious versions steal developer and CI credentials, flagged about three minutes after publication

    On 2026-05-14, three malicious versions of the node-ipc npm package (9.1.6, 9.2.3 and 12.0.1) were published at the same time by a co-maintainer account with no prior publish history on the package (StepSecurity, 2026-05-14). The account's email domain had expired on 2025-01-10 and was re-registered on 2026-05-07, a week before the attack (The Hacker News, 2026-05-14). Socket assesses that the new domain owner could then trigger a standard npm password reset and gain publish rights without touching the maintainer's own infrastructure (Socket Security, 2026-05-14). CSO counts almost 700K weekly downloads and 424 dependent projects, while StepSecurity reports over 10 million weekly downloads (CSO Online, 2026-05-15 · StepSecurity, 2026-05-14). The 9.x releases are fabricated from the 12.x package structure, so projects on ^9, ~9.1.x, ~9.2.x, ^12 or ~12.0 ranges received a malicious build on their next install or lockfile refresh (StepSecurity, 2026-05-14). When StepSecurity published, npm's latest tag pointed to 12.0.1, so an unpinned npm install node-ipc pulled the compromised tarball (StepSecurity, 2026-05-14). As of 2026-10-21 the npm registry no longer lists 9.1.6, 9.2.3 or 12.0.1, and its latest tag points to 14.0.0 (npm registry, 2026-10-21).

    The payload is an 80 KB obfuscated function appended to the CommonJS bundle node-ipc.cjs. It runs on every require('node-ipc') and uses no npm lifecycle hook, so tools that only scan preinstall and postinstall scripts miss it (StepSecurity, 2026-05-14). ESM-only consumers do not load the malicious file unless another dependency or a direct require loads node-ipc.cjs (Socket Security, 2026-05-14). The payload forks a detached child Node process that collects cloud provider credentials, SSH keys, Kubernetes tokens, GitHub CLI configuration, Terraform state, CI workflow files, .env files, shell history and macOS Keychain databases, gzips them and exfiltrates them (Socket Security, 2026-05-14 · StepSecurity, 2026-05-14). Four stacked obfuscation layers (string-array shuffling, control-flow flattening, dead-code injection and a custom reversed-nibble base-16 encoding) resist static analysis (StepSecurity, 2026-05-14). Socket found no persistence and no second-stage download, and its scanner flagged the publish within roughly three minutes (Socket Security, 2026-05-14).

    Contradiction: the analyses disagree on payload details. StepSecurity and The Hacker News describe 90+ file-path patterns, an HTTPS POST channel alongside DNS TXT exfiltration, and a SHA-256 targeting gate that leaves 12.0.1 inert except on one targeted project (StepSecurity, 2026-05-14 · The Hacker News, 2026-05-14). Socket counts 113 macOS and 127 Linux patterns, says the payload uses DNS TXT queries and not HTTP, HTTPS or TLS for exfiltration, and finds the same malicious file in all three versions, with a hash gate that only changes what the module exports (Socket Security, 2026-05-14). Treat all three versions as active stealers.

    Exposure
    Any project whose lockfile, build cache or local npm cache holds 9.1.6, 9.2.3 or 12.0.1 and loads the package through CommonJS, directly or transitively (Socket Security, 2026-05-14).
    Detection
    The payload first resolves a lookalike Azure hostname through the public resolvers 1.1.1.1 or 8.8.8.8, then sends DNS TXT queries straight to the attacker's server acting as a resolver, so the traffic never reaches corporate resolver logs (Socket Security, 2026-05-14 · StepSecurity, 2026-05-14). Look for DNS traffic from developer machines or CI runners to non-corporate resolvers and for large bursts of TXT queries: Socket estimates a 500 KiB archive produces about 29,400 of them (Socket Security, 2026-05-14). On endpoints, the payload shows as a Node process spawning a detached child Node process that runs node-ipc.cjs directly (Socket Security, 2026-05-14).
    Defender takeaway
    On any workstation or CI runner that loaded one of the three versions, remove it, reinstall a known clean version (9.2.1 or 12.0.0), and rotate every secret present on the host: SSH keys, npm, GitHub and GitLab tokens, cloud keys, and Kubernetes, Docker registry, Terraform and database credentials (Socket Security, 2026-05-14 · The Hacker News, 2026-05-14). Review dependencies by their entrypoints rather than only their install scripts, and prefer short-lived, scoped credentials in development and CI (Socket Security, 2026-05-14).

    GTIG: UNC6671 "BlackFile" vishing → AiTM → rogue-MFA → programmatic SharePoint exfiltration (1M+ files from one victim); DLS shutdown signals possible rebrand

    Google Threat Intelligence Group published on 2026-05-15 an analysis of UNC6671, a financially motivated extortion cluster that adopted the "BlackFile" brand by February 2026 and has targeted dozens of organizations across North America, Australia and the UK (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). Callers hired by the actor phone employees' personal mobile numbers, pose as internal IT or helpdesk staff citing a passkey migration or MFA update, and send the victim to a lookalike single sign-on portal on Tucows-registered domains, lately as organization-named subdomains with "passkey" or "enrollment" themes (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). The operator relays the credentials and the MFA code or push approval to the real SSO provider in real time, then immediately registers a new attacker-controlled MFA device for persistence (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). GTIG stresses that these compromises come from social engineering, not a vendor vulnerability (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15).

    After access, the actor moves into connected SaaS applications (SharePoint, OneDrive, Zendesk, Salesforce), searches for strings such as "confidential" and "SSN", and exfiltrates with Python requests and PowerShell scripts through Microsoft Graph and direct HTTP GET requests against document URLs, reusing session cookies (for example FedAuth) captured during the vishing phase (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). In one case the script downloaded more than a million files from SharePoint and OneDrive (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). The direct-fetch method is often logged as FileAccessed rather than FileDownloaded, so it slips past SOCs that treat FileAccessed as benign (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). In early intrusions the FileDownloaded records carried a spoofed Microsoft Office ClientAppId, which GTIG says served to bypass basic conditional access filters, while the user-agent showed python-requests or WindowsPowerShell (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). In later intrusions the FileAccessed records named python-requests as the client application in AppAccessContext (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). The sessions came from commercial VPN exit nodes and hosting providers (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15).

    The BlackFile leak site went offline in late April 2026, came back on 2026-05-11 with a message that BlackFile "is shutting down… under this name", and was inaccessible at publication (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). GTIG reads this as a possible transition phase rather than a permanent cessation, noting that extortion clusters commonly rebrand or disperse after shutdowns (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). GTIG assesses UNC6671 as independent of ShinyHunters (UNC6240), although UNC6671 co-opted the ShinyHunters brand at least once (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15).

    Detection
    In identity-provider logs, look for new MFA factor registrations (Okta system.multifactor.factor.setup) immediately preceded by failed MFA authentications or abandoned challenges (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). In Microsoft 365 audit logs, weigh FileAccessed as heavily as FileDownloaded when the user-agent is a scripting library or command-line tool, and flag FileAccessed bursts faster than a person can browse or whose AppAccessContext shows a headless client (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15).

    Triage: an Office client identity paired with a scripting-library user-agent is the mismatch GTIG found, and GTIG reads it as scripted access rather than a person using SharePoint (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15). A session source on a commercial VPN or hosting provider that is unusual for the user adds weight (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15).

    Defender takeaway
    Move users who can be reached by a fake helpdesk call from SMS and push MFA to FIDO2 security keys or passkeys, which GTIG says resist this AiTM and vishing chain (Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-05-15).

    OpenClaw "Claw Chain": four chainable sandbox, exec-allowlist and MCP loopback flaws (CVE-2026-44112, CVE-2026-44113, CVE-2026-44115, CVE-2026-44118) fixed in release 2026.4.22

    Cyera disclosed four vulnerabilities in OpenClaw, an open-source platform for autonomous AI agents originally launched as Clawdbot in late 2025, on 2026-05-15 after reporting them to the maintainers in April (Cyera, 2026-05-15). OpenClaw's advisories of 2026-04-23 list the openclaw npm package up to release 2026.4.21 as affected and 2026.4.22 as fixed for all four (OpenClaw GHSA-wppj-c6mr-83jj, 2026-04-23; OpenClaw GHSA-5h3g-6xhh-rg6p, 2026-04-23; OpenClaw GHSA-x3h8-jrgh-p8jx, 2026-04-23; OpenClaw GHSA-r6xh-pqhr-v4xh, 2026-04-23). CVE-2026-44112 and CVE-2026-44113 are time-of-check/time-of-use races in the OpenShell sandbox's filesystem bridge, where a symlink swap redirects a write outside the intended mount root or makes a read return bytes from outside it (OpenClaw GHSA-wppj-c6mr-83jj, 2026-04-23; OpenClaw GHSA-5h3g-6xhh-rg6p, 2026-04-23). CVE-2026-44115 lets an allowlisted command hide shell expansion in an unquoted heredoc body, so the shell evaluates more than the approved command text shows, which Cyera says returns environment variables such as API keys and tokens (OpenClaw GHSA-x3h8-jrgh-p8jx, 2026-04-23; Cyera, 2026-05-15). CVE-2026-44118 sits on the loopback MCP path, which accepted spoofable owner-context metadata from request headers and so let a non-owner loopback client act as owner for owner-gated operations such as gateway configuration, cron scheduling and execution-environment management (OpenClaw GHSA-r6xh-pqhr-v4xh, 2026-04-23; Cyera, 2026-05-15).

    Cyera's chain starts from code execution inside the OpenShell sandbox through a malicious plugin, prompt injection or compromised external input, uses the read escape and the heredoc flaw to expose credentials, secrets and files, the loopback flaw for owner-level control of the agent runtime, and the write escape to plant backdoors or change configuration; it says each step looks like normal agent behavior to traditional controls (Cyera, 2026-05-15). Cyera rates CVE-2026-44112 at CVSS 9.6 and CVE-2026-44115 at 8.8 (Cyera, 2026-05-15), VulnCheck scores them 8.4 and 8.7 in CVSS 4.0 (VulnCheck, CVE-2026-44112; VulnCheck, CVE-2026-44115), and OpenClaw's own advisory for the heredoc allowlist bypass rates it moderate (OpenClaw GHSA-x3h8-jrgh-p8jx, 2026-04-23). Cyera counts about 65,000 (Shodan) and about 180,000 (Zoomeye) publicly accessible OpenClaw instances as of May 2026 (Cyera, 2026-05-15). Neither Cyera nor the vendor advisories report exploitation.

    Exposure
    OpenClaw deployments running the openclaw npm package at release 2026.4.21 or earlier (OpenClaw GHSA-r6xh-pqhr-v4xh, 2026-04-23). The chain needs code execution inside the OpenShell sandbox first, so agents that install plugins or take prompts and other input from untrusted parties are in scope, and Cyera names deployments exposed to the internet without authentication or network controls in front of them as most at risk (Cyera, 2026-05-15).
    Detection
    In filesystem telemetry on the agent host, reads and writes by the sandbox process that resolve outside the sandbox mount root through a symlink swapped in after validation (OpenClaw GHSA-wppj-c6mr-83jj, 2026-04-23; OpenClaw GHSA-5h3g-6xhh-rg6p, 2026-04-23); in the agent's command-execution logs, approved commands carrying unquoted heredoc bodies with shell expansion (OpenClaw GHSA-x3h8-jrgh-p8jx, 2026-04-23); on the MCP loopback, owner-gated operations such as gateway or cron changes requested by a non-owner client (OpenClaw GHSA-r6xh-pqhr-v4xh, 2026-04-23; Cyera, 2026-05-15). Patching does not undo a compromise: Cyera says to assume any environment variable or credential reachable by OpenClaw processes may be compromised (Cyera, 2026-05-15), and configuration or files written outside the mount root survive the upgrade.
    Defender takeaway
    Upgrade every OpenClaw deployment to release 2026.4.22 or later, put internet-facing instances behind authentication or firewall controls, and rotate the API keys, tokens and bearer credentials its processes could reach (Cyera, 2026-05-15). Organizations without OpenClaw have no decision to make.

    AMD-SB-7052 / CVE-2025-54518: AMD Zen 2 op-cache corruption lets local code run instructions at a higher privilege, guest to host included (CVSS 7.3); OS updates on EPYC 7002, OEM BIOS updates on Ryzen

    AMD disclosed on 2026-05-12 that improper isolation of shared resources in the CPU operation cache of Zen 2-based products can cause incorrect instructions to execute at a higher privilege level; it scores CVE-2025-54518 at 7.3 on CVSS 4.0, with local access, high attack complexity and low privileges, and found it internally (AMD Product Security, 2026-05-12). Xen says code of any privilege could escalate, including userspace to kernel and guest to host, that only AMD Fam17h (Zen 2) CPUs are believed vulnerable, that all Xen versions are affected and that there is no mitigation other than its patches (Xen Project, 2026-05-12). Microsoft lists the flaw as not exploited or publicly disclosed and rates it Exploitation Unlikely, and says the latest Windows builds enable the mitigation (MSRC, CVE-2025-54518).

    The fix depends on the product line: for EPYC 7002 and EPYC Embedded 7002 processors AMD's mitigation is an OS update from the OS vendor, while for Ryzen 3000, 4000, 5000, 7020 and 7030 series, Threadripper PRO 3000 WX and Ryzen Embedded V2000 AMD released Platform Initialization firmware to OEMs between October and December 2025, to be installed as an OEM BIOS update, for example ComboAM4v2 PI version 1.2.0.10 for Ryzen 3000, 4000 and 5000 desktop parts (AMD Product Security, 2026-05-12). NCSC-NL lists updates from Debian (Xen), Dell PowerEdge, Fedora, Google Cloud Platform, Lenovo and Microsoft Windows (NCSC-NL, 2026-05-15).

    Exposure
    Any Zen 2-based system, with the most at stake where less-trusted code shares the processor with more-privileged code, as on virtualisation hosts, since guest-to-host escalation is possible (Xen Project, 2026-05-12); the processor model tells which channel fixes it, an OS update on EPYC 7002 and a BIOS at or above AMD's listed PI version on Ryzen, Threadripper PRO and Ryzen Embedded parts (AMD Product Security, 2026-05-12).
    Defender takeaway
    Patch in the regular cycle, Zen 2 hypervisor and multi-tenant hosts first: AMD lists OEM BIOS updates carrying its platform-initialization versions for Ryzen-class parts (AMD Product Security, 2026-05-12), Microsoft says the mitigation for certain AMD processor models requires a Windows update (MSRC, CVE-2025-54518), and Xen hosts need the XSA-490 patch (Xen Project, 2026-05-12).

    SentinelOne "Living Off the Pipeline": attackers subverted TeamCity, GitLab automation, self-hosted runners and developer workstations in 2025

    SentinelOne's post of 2026-05-15 describes how attackers in 2025 went after the infrastructure that builds and ships software, not only its dependencies (SentinelOne, 2026-05-15). In one case attackers exploited a vulnerable self-hosted TeamCity server (the post links JetBrains' advisory for CVE-2023-42793), created a benign-looking build configuration that a trusted build agent ran with SYSTEM privileges, deployed a backdoor into internal environments and stayed undetected for more than a year (SentinelOne, 2026-05-15). JetBrains fixed CVE-2023-42793, an unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaw, in TeamCity On-Premises 2023.05.4 (JetBrains, 2023-09-21) and warned that backdoors planted before the patch persist after it (JetBrains, 2023-10-18). In an intrusion from SentinelOne's annual threat report, a compromised GitLab service-account token was used to create projects with malicious Ansible playbooks that the organization's pipeline then executed automatically (SentinelOne, 2026-05-15).

    The post's other examples target people and runners: Contagious Interview sent developers in cryptocurrency and blockchain fraudulent job offers leading to fake skill-assessment sites, where a fabricated error told them to run commands that silently installed malware with access to their SSH keys, tokens and credentials, and the Sha1-Hulud campaign registered infected systems as self-hosted GitHub runners under attacker-defined names and used workflows that ran code when someone commented in a repository discussion (SentinelOne, 2026-05-15). Malicious versions of popular build packages ran reconnaissance scripts during installation that harvested tokens and inspected environments, and phished maintainer accounts appended malicious code to legitimate package updates (SentinelOne, 2026-05-15).

    Unit 42: Gremlin stealer hides its payload in XOR-encoded .NET resources and adds a crypto clipper, Discord token theft and WebSocket session hijacking

    Unit 42 identified a new Gremlin stealer variant that bundles stolen browser cookies, session tokens, clipboard contents, cryptocurrency wallet data and FTP and VPN credentials into a ZIP archive named with the victim's public IP address and uploads it to an attacker-controlled site, while exfiltration through private web panels or the Telegram Bot API stays as in earlier builds (Unit 42, 2026-05-15). The variant moves its payload into the .NET resource section masked with single-byte XOR encoding, decrypts and maps each critical function into memory only when needed, and hides its strings behind a runtime decoder that reads them from an embedded encrypted resource; one sample is wrapped in a commercial packer that turns the code into custom bytecode run by a private virtual machine (Unit 42, 2026-05-15). New modules extract Discord tokens by scanning multiple paths with regex validation, watch the clipboard for wallet-address patterns and replace the victim's address with the attacker's in real time, and hijack live sessions in Chromium-based browsers over WebSocket by requesting the data directly from the running browser process, which bypasses modern cookie protections (Unit 42, 2026-05-15). The post does not describe how Gremlin is delivered.

    Exposure
    Endpoints with Chromium-based browsers; Gremlin targets web browsers, the clipboard and local storage for payment card details, cookies, session tokens, cryptocurrency wallet data and FTP and VPN credentials (Unit 42, 2026-05-15).
    Detection
    In egress telemetry, a ZIP archive named after the host's public IP address uploaded to an unfamiliar web panel, and Telegram Bot API traffic from a process that is not a messaging client; in endpoint telemetry, a process requesting session data from a running Chromium browser process, and a process that rewrites clipboard contents matching wallet-address patterns (Unit 42, 2026-05-15). Signatures on strings and exports miss current builds, because the strings and functions only appear decrypted at runtime (Unit 42, 2026-05-15).
    Defender takeaway
    Replace detections tuned to legacy Gremlin samples, whose function exports and internal symbols were left intact, with behavioral ones (Unit 42, 2026-05-15). On an infected host, revoke its browser sessions and rotate the Discord, FTP and VPN credentials it held, since hijacked sessions and stolen credentials stay usable after the malware is removed.
