    Škoda Auto Deutschland accessories-shop breach: attackers exploited the shop software, customer data and password hashes were accessible, logs cannot show what was copied

    Škoda Auto Deutschland's security monitoring found that unauthorised parties had exploited a vulnerability in the standard shop software of its online accessories shop and gained temporary access to the shop system; the shop was taken offline, the vulnerability fixed, an external IT forensics firm engaged and the incident reported to the competent data-protection authority (Škoda Auto Deutschland). The shop stores names and addresses, email addresses and sometimes telephone numbers, order information and customer-account credentials made up of the email address and a password kept as a cryptographic hash; full credit card data is processed only by the payment providers (Škoda Auto Deutschland). Škoda's analysis found that access to the stored data was possible in principle, but the nature of its logs does not allow it to reconstruct whether and to what extent data was actually copied or retrieved, and it has no concrete indication of misuse (Škoda Auto Deutschland). Only the accessories shop run by Škoda Auto Deutschland GmbH for customers in Germany was affected, not other Škoda online shops, the systems of the manufacturer Škoda Auto a.s. or the Škoda Connect portal (Škoda Auto Deutschland). SecurityWeek reported the disclosure on 2026-05-11 and noted that the number of affected customers had not been disclosed (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-11).

    Exposure
    Customers of the German Škoda accessories shop, above all those who reuse its password elsewhere: Škoda expects phishing that refers to earlier orders and login attempts with the known credentials, especially where the same password serves several services (Škoda Auto Deutschland).
    Defender takeaway
    Affected customers change any password they reused from the shop and treat messages about Škoda orders with suspicion, as Škoda advises (Škoda Auto Deutschland); for other organisations the case changes no decision, since neither the shop software nor the vulnerability is named (Škoda Auto Deutschland).

    ICO fines South Staffordshire Water £963,900 under UK GDPR for the 2020 to 2022 intrusion: phishing foothold, ZeroLogon on two unpatched domain controllers, 5% of IT monitored

    The UK Information Commissioner's Office fined South Staffordshire Plc and South Staffordshire Water Plc £963,900 for infringing Articles 5(1)(f) and 32(1) of the UK GDPR (ICO enforcement action), after a 40% reduction for the company's early admission, which it accepted without appeal (ICO, 2026-05-11). The attack began in September 2020 with a phishing email whose attachment, once opened, let the attacker install malicious software that stayed undetected for 20 months; in May 2022 the attacker moved through the network and compromised domain administrator privileges, and the breach was found only when IT performance problems started an internal investigation on 15 July 2022, followed by a ransom note on 26 July (ICO, 2026-05-11). Two domain controllers were unpatched against ZeroLogon (CVE-2020-1472) in May 2022, and while the incident did not start through it, the attacker exploited it during the incident (ICO penalty notice). The company held data on about 1.85 million customers, around 750,000 current and 1.1 million former, and on its staff; the personal data of 633,887 people was published on the dark web, part of over 4.1 TB of data, including online-service usernames and passwords, bank account numbers and sort codes, employees' National Insurance numbers and information from which disabilities of Priority Services Register customers could be inferred (ICO, 2026-05-11). The ICO does not name the attacker; The Record and The Register attribute the attack to Cl0p, which claimed it, and the penalty notice makes no reference to any compromise of operational or water-treatment systems (The Record, 2026-05-11; The Register, 2026-05-11).

    Exposure
    The failures the ICO found: limited controls that let the attacker escalate to administrator after the first foothold, monitoring of only 5% of the IT environment, Windows Server 2003 still in use, unpatched critical systems and no regular internal or external security scans (ICO, 2026-05-11). ZeroLogon lets an unauthenticated attacker who connects to a domain controller over the Netlogon Remote Protocol obtain domain administrator access (Microsoft, CVE-2020-1472).
    Detection
    Coverage of endpoint telemetry in the SIEM is the gap the notice names: in December 2021 a third-party security operations centre monitored only 5% of the IT environment, and endpoint telemetry and logging were not integrated into the security monitoring platform (ICO penalty notice).
    Defender takeaway
    For UK data controllers, the ICO treats missing monitoring coverage, unsupported software and unpatched domain controllers as a breach of the UK GDPR security duty, and asks whether logging and monitoring cover the IT environment and alerts are acted on (ICO, 2026-05-11); domain controllers still without Microsoft's Netlogon updates of August 2020 remain exposed to the same escalation (Microsoft, CVE-2020-1472).

    Checkmarx Jenkins AST plugin backdoored: rogue build 2026.5.09 swept build hosts for credentials on 2026-05-09/10, the third wave of malicious Checkmarx artifacts since March (TeamPCP claimed)

    On Saturday 2026-05-09 a rogue build, version 2026.5.09, of the Checkmarx Jenkins AST plugin was uploaded to repo.jenkins-ci.org outside the plugin's release pipeline; it broke the plugin's version scheme and had no git tag or GitHub release (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-11). Checkmarx says an external service account modified the plugin and published it to the Jenkins Marketplace, that the malicious window ran from 2026-05-09 01:25 UTC to 2026-05-10 08:47 UTC, and that the attacker used access obtained in the March incident, which began with credentials taken in the Trivy supply-chain attack on 2026-03-19 (Checkmarx, updated 2026-07-06). It is the third wave of malicious Checkmarx artifacts after GitHub Actions and Open VSX extensions on 2026-03-23 and a KICS Docker image, VS Code and DevAssist extensions and a GitHub Action on 2026-04-22 (Checkmarx, updated 2026-07-06). The payload determines the operating system (Windows, Linux or macOS), then walks a list of file paths for common applications on that system looking for credentials, with crypto wallets, VPNs, AWS and GitHub among the targets; Checkmarx had found no references to Jenkins in it, and Mandiant found no threat-actor access to the Checkmarx One SaaS environment (Checkmarx, updated 2026-07-06).

    TeamPCP claimed the compromise (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-11), and researcher Adnan Khan and SOCRadar report that the group gained access to the plugin's GitHub repository and renamed it "Checkmarx-Fully-Hacked-by-TeamPCP-and-Their-Customers-Should-Cancel-Now", with the description "Checkmarx fails to rotate secrets again. with love – TeamPCP" (The Hacker News, 2026-05-11). Google Threat Intelligence Group tracks TeamPCP as UNC6780 and says that in the late-March compromises (Trivy, Checkmarx, LiteLLM, BerriAI) the group embedded the SANDCLOCK credential stealer to extract cloud secrets such as AWS keys and GitHub tokens from build environments (GTIG, 2026-05-11).

    Exposure
    Any Jenkins instance that installed or updated the Checkmarx AST plugin to build 2026.5.09 during the malicious window (SOCRadar, 2026-05-11); Checkmarx names 2.0.13-829.vc72453fa_1c16 of 2025-12-17 or earlier and the clean replacements 2.0.13-848.v76e89de8a_053 and 2.0.13-847.v08c0072b_2fd5 as safe (Checkmarx, updated 2026-07-06).
    Detection
    In Jenkins plugin inventories and update history, the checkmarx-ast-scanner plugin at version 2026.5.09; on hosts that ran it, file-access telemetry showing the build process reading the credential files of wallets, VPN clients, AWS and GitHub, which is what the payload's path sweep produces (Checkmarx, updated 2026-07-06). Checkmarx also advises restricting CI runner egress to an allowlist and monitoring outbound connections for unexpected domains (Checkmarx, updated 2026-07-06).
    Defender takeaway
    Where a pipeline ran build 2026.5.09, rotate all credentials that pipeline had access to, as Checkmarx recommends (Checkmarx, updated 2026-07-06), and investigate for lateral movement or persistence (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-11). Checkmarx's standing advice holds beyond this build: pin to specific SHAs rather than mutable tags and treat CI runner credentials as short-lived and tightly scoped (Checkmarx, updated 2026-07-06).

    CVE-2026-0300: PAN-OS User-ID Authentication Portal unauthenticated root RCE, exploited by a likely state-sponsored cluster; fixed builds for every supported branch

    CVE-2026-0300 is a buffer overflow in the User-ID Authentication Portal (Captive Portal) service of PAN-OS that lets an unauthenticated attacker execute code with root privileges on PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls by sending crafted packets; Prisma Access, Cloud NGFW and Panorama are not affected (Palo Alto Networks PSIRT). Palo Alto scores it CVSS 4.0 9.3 where the portal is reachable from untrusted networks and 8.7 where it is restricted to trusted internal addresses, and reports limited exploitation against portals exposed to untrusted addresses or the internet (Palo Alto Networks PSIRT). CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 2026-05-06 (CISA KEV catalog, 2026-05-06). Fixed builds are listed for every supported branch: 12.1.4-h5 and 12.1.7; 11.2.4-h17, 11.2.7-h13, 11.2.10-h6 and 11.2.12; 11.1.4-h33, 11.1.6-h32, 11.1.7-h6, 11.1.10-h25, 11.1.13-h5 and 11.1.15; 10.2.7-h34, 10.2.10-h36, 10.2.13-h21, 10.2.16-h7 and 10.2.18-h6; older unsupported versions must move to a supported fixed version, and the advisory's table still marks 12.1.7 with an ETA of 05/28 (Palo Alto Networks PSIRT).

    Unit 42 attributes the exploitation to CL-STA-1132, a cluster of likely state-sponsored activity: unsuccessful exploitation attempts began on 2026-04-09, and a week later the attackers achieved code execution and injected shellcode (Unit 42, 2026-05-07). They then cleared crash kernel messages and nginx crash entries and records, deployed the tunnelling tools EarthWorm and ReverseSocks5, enumerated Active Directory with the firewall's service-account credentials, and deleted ptrace injection evidence; on 2026-04-29 a SAML flood against the compromised device promoted a second device to active, inheriting the same internet-facing traffic, and that device was then targeted too (Unit 42, 2026-05-07).

    Exposure
    A PA-Series or VM-Series firewall below the fixed build for its branch on which both of Palo Alto Networks' conditions hold: the User-ID Authentication Portal is configured (Device > User Identification > Authentication Portal Settings), and an interface management profile with Response Pages enabled is attached to an L3 interface in a zone where untrusted or internet traffic can enter; restricting the portal to trusted zones and disabling Response Pages in the Interface Management Profile of every L3 interface where untrusted traffic can enter, or disabling the portal, removes the exposure (Palo Alto Networks PSIRT). Threat ID 510019 (Applications and Threats content 9097-10022) blocks the attack for Threat Prevention subscribers and needs PAN-OS 11.1 or later (Palo Alto Networks PSIRT).
    Detection
    On the firewall, gaps in crash and kernel logs, deleted nginx crash records, outbound tunnelling connections from the firewall, and Active Directory queries made with the firewall's service account are the behaviours Unit 42 describes; a second device promoted to active after a burst of SAML traffic is a further signal (Unit 42, 2026-05-07). Patching does not remove a foothold, so a device that was exposed before its fixed build needs a compromise check.

    GTIG AI Threat Tracker (May 2026): an AI-developed zero-day 2FA bypass prepared for mass exploitation, AI decoy code in Russia-nexus malware and PROMPTSPY's Gemini agent

    Google Threat Intelligence Group's AI Threat Tracker of 2026-05-11, drawn from Mandiant incident response engagements, Gemini and GTIG's own research, follows its February 2026 report on AI-related threat activity (GTIG, 2026-05-11).

    An AI-developed zero-day prepared for mass exploitation

    GTIG observed prominent cybercrime actors partnering to plan a mass vulnerability-exploitation operation around a zero-day exploit, implemented in a Python script, that bypasses two-factor authentication on a popular open-source, web-based system administration tool; GTIG worked with the vendor to disclose the flaw and disrupt the activity, and says its counter-discovery may have prevented the exploit's use (GTIG, 2026-05-11). The bypass requires valid user credentials and stems from a high-level semantic logic flaw, a trust assumption the developer hardcoded, rather than from memory corruption or input sanitisation (GTIG, 2026-05-11). GTIG does not believe Gemini was used but has high confidence that an AI model supported the discovery and weaponisation, citing abundant educational docstrings, a hallucinated CVSS score and a textbook Pythonic structure with detailed help menus and a clean _C ANSI colour class (GTIG, 2026-05-11). Google did not name the tool (The Hacker News, 2026-05-11). Contradiction: The Hacker News calls this the first time AI was put to use in the wild for exploit generation (The Hacker News, 2026-05-11), whereas GTIG says the planned mass exploitation may have been prevented (GTIG, 2026-05-11).

    State actors using AI for vulnerability research and infrastructure

    UNC2814 prompted Gemini with expert security personas to support vulnerability research into embedded targets including TP-Link firmware and Odette File Transfer Protocol (OFTP) implementations, and APT45 sent thousands of repetitive prompts that recursively analyse different CVEs and validate proof-of-concept exploits (GTIG, 2026-05-11). APT27 used Gemini to develop a fleet-management application likely meant for an operational relay box network, with the hop count hardcoded to three and mobile Wi-Fi and router devices among the supported types (GTIG, 2026-05-11). Actors also prompt models for the organisational hierarchies and third-party relationships of large enterprises to tailor phishing lures, one tried to identify the exact computer model a high-value target used, and a suspected PRC-nexus actor ran the agentic tools Hexstrike and Strix against a Japanese technology firm and an East Asian cybersecurity platform (GTIG, 2026-05-11).

    Malware with AI-generated decoys and an AI-driven Android backdoor

    Russia-nexus activity against Ukrainian organisations delivers CANFAIL and LONGSTREAM, which use LLM-generated decoy code to obfuscate their malicious functionality: CANFAIL's source carries LLM comments labelling unused filler blocks, and LONGSTREAM queries the system's daylight-saving status 32 times to pad the script with benign-looking activity (GTIG, 2026-05-11). PROMPTFLUX and HONESTCUE, which call the Gemini API to generate or obfuscate code, featured in GTIG's earlier reports (GTIG, 2026-05-11). The Android backdoor PROMPTSPY, first identified by ESET, runs an agent module that serialises the visible user interface through the Accessibility API, sends it to the gemini-2.5-flash-lite model in an HTTP POST and turns the JSON reply into simulated taps and swipes; it can replay captured PINs or lock patterns, lays an invisible overlay over the uninstall button and can rotate its Gemini API keys and VNC relay through its C2 (GTIG, 2026-05-11).

    Pooled LLM access and attacks on AI components

    UNC6201 tried a public script that registers and immediately cancels premium LLM accounts, and UNC5673, a PRC-nexus cluster with notable overlaps with TEMP.Hex that has targeted government sectors mainly in South and Southeast Asia, uses Claude-Relay-Service and CLI-Proxy-API to pool Gemini, Claude and OpenAI accounts (GTIG, 2026-05-11). TeamPCP (UNC6780) gained initial access through compromised PyPI packages and malicious pull requests to GitHub repositories including those of Trivy, Checkmarx, LiteLLM and BerriAI, embedded the SANDCLOCK credential stealer to extract AWS keys and GitHub tokens from build environments and monetised them with ransomware and data-theft extortion groups; GTIG adds that the LiteLLM compromise could expose considerable AI API secrets (GTIG, 2026-05-11).

    Exposure
    The zero-day needs valid user credentials on an administration tool GTIG does not name (GTIG, 2026-05-11); build environments that pulled the TeamPCP-compromised Trivy, Checkmarx, LiteLLM or BerriAI components held the AWS keys and GitHub tokens SANDCLOCK went after (GTIG, 2026-05-11).
    Detection
