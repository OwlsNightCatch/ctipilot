    HIGHCVE-2017-7692updatedNATOB2

    Webworm (China-aligned) turns to European government targets with the EchoCreep (Discord) and GraphWorm (Microsoft Graph and OneDrive) backdoors, ESET reports

    ESET Research published its analysis of Webworm, a China-aligned group active since at least 2022 that it links to SixLittleMonkeys and FishMonger, on 2026-05-20: in 2025 the group moved from Asia to governmental organizations in Belgium, Italy, Serbia and Poland, compromised a South African university, and replaced its Trochilus and McRat backdoors with two new ones, EchoCreep and GraphWorm (ESET, 2026-05-20). ESET told The Hacker News that a link to Space Pirates is tenuous at best (The Hacker News, 2026-05-20). ESET could not find the entry point, but an operator server showed nuclei and dirsearch scans of 56 targets in countries including Spain, Hungary, Belgium, Nigeria, Czechia and Serbia, and a public exploit for the SquirrelMail post-authentication RCE CVE-2017-7692 run against a Serbian webmail target, which suggests the group held that victim's credentials (ESET, 2026-05-20).

    EchoCreep and GraphWorm. EchoCreep, written in Go, sends all its traffic through Discord API endpoints, decodes commands with base64 and AES-CBC-128, runs them through cmd.exe and uploads files as attachments; it reports to per-victim channels, named after the victim's IP address or IP address and hostname, that the operators created before it first ran, and it runs under a custom scheduled task and carries a modified timestamp (ESET, 2026-05-20). ESET decrypted 433 messages going back to 2024-03-21 across four victim channels; the first real compromise came on 2025-04-09, followed by curl downloads (ESET, 2026-05-20). GraphWorm runs at every user logon through registry Run keys and talks only to OneDrive endpoints of Microsoft Graph, with a valid cloud account and API tokens, fetching jobs from and uploading results to a per-victim folder; it encrypts traffic with AES-256-CBC and base64, stages command output in a local file that it deletes after upload, and likely uses Graph's upload-session endpoint for large files (ESET, 2026-05-20).

    Proxies, staging and stolen data. The proxy tools WormFrp, a modified frp that reads AES-encrypted configurations from a compromised third-party Amazon S3 bucket, ChainWorm, SmuxProxy and WormSocket build internal, external and multi-hop proxies through rented servers and VPS hosts, the group staged tools in a GitHub repository, and both the repository and the S3 bucket have been taken down (ESET, 2026-05-20). The S3 bucket held virtual-machine snapshots, one with the state of an Italian governmental machine, and from December 2025 to January 2026 an mRemoteNG connection-configuration file and a Visio infrastructure diagram taken from a Spanish governmental entity; in October 2025 the operators also uploaded SharpSecretsdump, a tool that mimics Impacket's secretsdump (ESET, 2026-05-20).

    Exposure
    Governmental organizations in Europe, Webworm's focus since 2025, and in particular internet-facing web servers and webmail, which the group scans and probes (ESET, 2026-05-20).
    Detection
    Egress and endpoint telemetry: Discord API traffic from hosts or processes with no business use of Discord, Microsoft Graph OneDrive calls from an unexpected process, new scheduled tasks and Run-key entries starting unfamiliar binaries, and frp-style reverse and SOCKS5 proxies opening outbound connections (ESET, 2026-05-20). Because ESET reads the SquirrelMail exploit as run with the victim's own credentials, a webmail account it touched needs a new password after clean-up.
    Defender takeaway
    European government networks hunt for the Discord and OneDrive command channels and the proxy tooling described above, and review webmail and web servers for the scanning and the SquirrelMail exploitation ESET found (ESET, 2026-05-20).

    SonicWall Gen6 SSL-VPN: patched-firmware appliances still open to the CVE-2024-12802 MFA bypass without manual LDAP remediation, exploited February to March 2026 by a suspected access broker

    SonicWall's SNWLID-2025-0001 describes CVE-2024-12802 (CWE-305, CVSS 6.5): when SSL-VPN authenticates against Microsoft Active Directory, UPN and SAM account names are handled separately, so MFA can be configured independently for each and an attacker can bypass it through the alternative account name (SonicWall SNWLID-2025-0001); in practice an attacker with valid credentials authenticates without the MFA step (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-20). On Gen6 the fixed firmware does not remove the vulnerable LDAP configuration, so six manual steps are needed: delete the LDAP configuration that uses userPrincipalName, remove cached LDAP users, remove the SSL VPN user domain, reboot, recreate the LDAP configuration without userPrincipalName and take a fresh backup (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-20); Gen7 firmware version 7.2.0-7015 and Gen8 firmware version 8.0.1-8017 build those steps in (SonicWall SNWLID-2025-0001). ReliaQuest responded to intrusions between February and March 2026 on Gen6 devices that looked patched and assesses with medium confidence that they are the first in-the-wild exploitation; the attacker brute-forced VPN accounts with an automated tool, in one case after only 13 attempts, and reached a file server within 30 minutes (ReliaQuest, 2026-05-19), connected to it over RDP with a shared local administrator password, and tried to deploy a Cobalt Strike beacon and a vulnerable driver to disable endpoint protection, both blocked by EDR (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-20). ReliaQuest describes the tools as consistent with actors in the ransomware ecosystem and the logouts and later re-entries with other accounts as initial access broker activity (ReliaQuest, 2026-05-19); the researchers could not attribute the attacks, though the activity resembled the Akira ransomware group's (Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-05-19).

    Exposure
    SonicWall Gen6 firewalls and NSv appliances running SSL-VPN with LDAP authentication against Active Directory where the LDAP configuration used userPrincipalName, even on the fixed firmware, until all six remediation steps are done; affected builds are firmware version 6.5.4.15-117n and older and NSv firmware version 6.5.4.4-44v-21-2457 and older (SonicWall SNWLID-2025-0001) (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-20). The advisory also lists Gen7 builds up to 7.1.2-7019, the Gen8 TZ80 on 8.0.0-8035 and SMA100 10.2.1.13-72sv and older, fixed from 7.0.1-5169 or 7.2.0-7015, 8.0.1-8017 and 10.2.1.14-75sv (SonicWall SNWLID-2025-0001). Gen6 reached end-of-life on 2026-04-16 (Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-05-19).
    Detection
    SonicWall authentication logs forwarded with the Authentication category: rapid logins with session type sess="CLI" from VPS and VPN hosting ranges, together with Event ID 238 (failed VPN login) or Event ID 1080 (successful SSL VPN zone login), and after a success a change of the session type from CLI to GMS, which marks hands-on access to internal resources (ReliaQuest, 2026-05-19). Completing the LDAP remediation does not undo access already taken: the actor came back days later with other accounts, so the VPN accounts that authenticated through the bypass need new credentials and an audit of their privileges (ReliaQuest, 2026-05-19).

    Triage: where no CLI-based VPN client is authorized or typical, any sess="CLI" event is a high-fidelity indicator; where scripted VPN authentication is legitimate, ReliaQuest says to baseline it and tune on volume, source IP and timing (ReliaQuest, 2026-05-19).

    Defender takeaway
    Treat a Gen6 SSL-VPN as exposed until all six remediation steps are confirmed, whatever its firmware version shows, because patch dashboards cannot tell patched from fully remediated (ReliaQuest, 2026-05-19); with Gen6 out of support, the lasting fix is a move to supported hardware (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-20).

    NOTABLECVE-2026-43494updatedNATOB2

    PinTheft: Linux kernel RDS zerocopy double-free (CVE-2026-43494) turned into a root exploit through io_uring fixed buffers, public PoC; V12 found RDS loaded by default only on Arch Linux among the distributions it tested

    V12 Security released PinTheft with a proof-of-concept exploit on 2026-05-19, and Gentoo's Sam James relayed it to the oss-security list the same day; Aaron Esau of the V12 team found the bug (oss-security, 2026-05-19). In the RDS zerocopy send path, rds_message_zcopy_from_user() pins user pages one at a time; when a later page faults, the error path drops the pages already pinned while the scatterlist entries stay live, so message cleanup drops them again and each failed send steals a reference from the first page (oss-security, 2026-05-19). The exploit registers an anonymous page as an io_uring fixed buffer, which gives it a FOLL_PIN bias of 1024 references, steals those references with failing RDS zerocopy sends, lets the page be reclaimed as page cache of a SUID-root binary and overwrites it through the stale fixed-buffer pointer with a small ELF payload, so running the binary drops into a root shell (oss-security, 2026-05-19). It needs the RDS modules, io_uring enabled, a readable SUID-root binary and x86_64 for the included payload (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-20). The bug was patched upstream earlier in May (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-20), and the kernel CNA published that fix, which resets op_nents in rds_message_zcopy_from_user(), as CVE-2026-43494 (CVSS 7.8) on 2026-05-21 (ENISA EUVD-2026-31267).

    Exposure
    Linux hosts that load the RDS modules (CONFIG_RDS with CONFIG_RDS_TCP), which V12 found enabled by default only on Arch Linux among the common distributions it tested (oss-security, 2026-05-19), and that have the io_uring I/O API enabled (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-20).
    Detection
    Process and syscall telemetry: an unprivileged process that opens RDS sockets, registers an io_uring fixed buffer and issues a burst of failing RDS zerocopy sends, then executes a SUID-root binary that yields a root shell, is the exploit's sequence (oss-security, 2026-05-19).
    Defender takeaway
    Install the fixed kernel; where that cannot happen at once, unload rds_tcp and rds and block both from loading through modprobe configuration, the mitigation BleepingComputer gives (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-20); hosts that never load RDS are not reachable by this exploit.
    Improvement21 Oct 2026 20:35 UTCrun 2026-10-21T1148Z-auditaffected_productsbodyclassificationcvesevent_dateevidenceheadlinekindsourcessourcing_notesummarytechniquestitleverification

    V12 found the RDS module (CONFIG_RDS with CONFIG_RDS_TCP) enabled by default only on Arch Linux among the common distributions it tested; it did not establish that the module is off on every other distribution (oss-security, 2026-05-19). Aaron Esau of V12 found the bug and V12 released the exploit, which Gentoo's Sam James relayed to oss-security (oss-security, 2026-05-19).     CVE-2026-45829: ChromaDB's Python FastAPI server runs an attacker's model code before checking authentication (CVSS 4.0 10.0), no fix in 1.5.9

    HiddenLayer discovered CVE-2026-45829 in ChromaDB's Python FastAPI server and gave it the maximum severity score (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-19); Hadrian, which published a technical analysis the same day, gives CVSS 4.0 10.0 and repeats HiddenLayer's name for it, "ChromaToast", (Hadrian, 2026-05-19). The create-collection handler loads the request's embedding-function configuration before it checks authentication: with trust_remote_code set to true and model_name pointed at a HuggingFace repository or local path the attacker controls, the server downloads and instantiates the model and runs the attacker's Python inside the uvicorn server process (Hadrian, 2026-05-19), and only then rejects the request, with a 500 in HiddenLayer's description (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-19). Every Python FastAPI server from 1.0.0 is affected while the default Rust frontend does not use this code path, and there was no fixed version at the time of writing, 1.5.9 being the latest (Hadrian, 2026-05-19). HiddenLayer reports an initial acknowledgement on 2025-12-16 followed by silence across more than ten follow-ups (Hadrian, 2026-05-19), and its Shodan queries found roughly 73% of internet-exposed instances running a vulnerable version (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-19); Hadrian dates the report to 2025-11-28 (Hadrian, 2026-05-19), BleepingComputer to February 17 (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-19).

    Exposure
    Deployments that expose the Python FastAPI server, used by many self-hosters, development instances and the chromadb[server] distribution (Hadrian, 2026-05-19); local deployments without an exposed API server and the Rust frontend are not affected (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-19).
    Detection
    Web access logs and egress: a POST /api/v2/tenants/.../collections that returns 500 with a body mentioning trust_remote_code, sentence_transformers, transformers, auto_map or model-loader errors, and outbound connections to HuggingFace from the Chroma server process (Hadrian, 2026-05-19). A server with such hits ran the payload with its own rights before the request was rejected, so mitigating afterwards does not clear it.

    Triage: HuggingFace downloads started by your own ingestion code are normal; the same download from the Chroma server process that your ingestion code did not initiate is the signal (Hadrian, 2026-05-19).

    Defender takeaway
    Until a fix is confirmed, run the Rust frontend or keep the Python server off public networks and restrict network access to the ChromaDB API port (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-19); Hadrian also describes a local hotfix for 1.5.9 that moves the authentication check ahead of the configuration loader and strips trust_remote_code from the embedding-function arguments (Hadrian, 2026-05-19).

    Microsoft published CVE-2026-42822 on 2026-05-18: improper authentication (CWE-287) in Azure Local Disconnected Operations (ALDO) allows an unauthorized attacker to elevate privileges over a network, scored CVSS 10.0 with a scope change and rated Exploitation More Likely, though not exploited or publicly disclosed at release (MSRC, 2026-05-18).     Only ALDO deployments, which Microsoft offers to approved customers; the attacker needs a foothold and identity context inside the disconnected environment (MSRC, 2026-05-18).
    Defender takeaway
    Approved ALDO customers schedule the full system update to version 2604 or later (MSRC, 2026-05-18); with an insider precondition and no exploitation known it is a planned update, not an emergency, and Azure customers without ALDO have no decision to make.

    Verizon 2026 DBIR: vulnerability exploitation overtakes stolen credentials as the leading initial access vector for the first time in 19 years

    Verizon published its 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report on 2026-05-19, built on more than 31,000 security incidents, over 22,000 of them confirmed breaches, at organizations in 145 countries (Verizon 2026 DBIR); the incidents date from November 2024 to October 2025 (Help Net Security, 2026-05-20). Exploitation of vulnerabilities is the most common initial access vector at 31% of breaches, up from 20%, and for the first time in the report's 19 years it has passed stolen credentials (Verizon 2026 DBIR) (Verizon, 2026-05-19); credential abuse fell from 22% to 13%, partly because Verizon now tracks pretexting as its own vector, and would have been 16% without that change (Verizon 2026 DBIR). Only 26% of KEV-listed vulnerabilities were fully remediated in 2025, against 38% the year before, and the median time to full patching grew from 32 to 43 days (Verizon 2026 DBIR) (Help Net Security, 2026-05-20). Breaches involving a third party rose 60% and now account for 48% of all breaches (Verizon, 2026-05-19), while only 23% of third-party organizations fully remediated their MFA issues; ransomware was present in 48% of breaches, up from 44%, and 69% of ransomware victims did not pay (Help Net Security, 2026-05-20).

    Defender takeaway
    The benchmark bears on patching more than on detection: Help Net Security notes that the analysed data predates the rise of AI-assisted vulnerability research and that the problem is organizations not patching known vulnerabilities quickly or thoroughly enough (Help Net Security, 2026-05-20), so KEV remediation completeness and time to full patch are the measures to compare against the 26% and 43-day figures.
