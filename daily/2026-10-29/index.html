    MDaemon mail server below 26.0.4 and Windows ClamAV 0.101.0 to 1.5.3: a crafted RAR emailed to any address runs code on the server through the bundled UnRAR flaw CVE-2025-8088, and ESET saw Russia-aligned attackers use it in Ukraine to deploy the HELLAGE and HELLTAG backdoors

    ESET reports an active campaign in Ukraine in which Russia-aligned attackers exploited MDaemon mail servers and deployed two backdoor families, HELLAGE and HELLTAG (ESET Research, 2026-10-28). MDaemon uses ClamAV to scan attachments of incoming mail; old ClamAV versions carry CVE-2025-8088, the WinRAR path-traversal flaw ESET uncovered last year, which ESET says was fixed in ClamAV's source on 27 July 2025 (the release notes below date the fixed ClamAV builds 2026-08-07), while MDaemon kept shipping an old libclamunrar.dll until MDaemon 26.0.4 of 2026-08-25 (ESET Research, 2026-10-28). The chain ESET describes: a crafted RAR archive is mailed to any address at a vulnerable server, MDaemon invokes ClamAV to scan it, and the exploit writes a malicious DLL to the server's file system with no user interaction, so the message carries no lure (ESET Research, 2026-10-28). CERT-UA names the backdoors: HELLAGE steals email messages and other data, talks to a hardcoded server and can deploy more malware, and HELLTAG takes commands, transfers files and does reconnaissance through a hardcoded Telegram bot; ESET cannot attribute the campaign to a known Russia-aligned actor (ESET Research, 2026-10-28).

    The exposure is wider than MDaemon. ClamAV's release notes of 2026-08-07 say it adopted the upstream UnRAR fix for CVE-2025-8088, which rejects path separators in NTFS alternate data stream names so that extraction cannot leave ClamAV's temporary scan directory on Windows, and that the issue affects ClamAV 0.101.0 through 1.5.3 with the fix in 1.4.6 and 1.5.4 (ClamAV, 2026-08-07). CISA has listed CVE-2025-8088 in its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog since 2025-08-12 (CISA KEV, 2026-10-28).

    Exposure
    An MDaemon server on Windows below 26.0.4 that accepts inbound mail (ESET Research, 2026-10-28), and any other Windows host that scans mail or files with ClamAV 0.101.0 to 1.5.3 (ClamAV, 2026-08-07).
    Detection
    Because the attack needs no lure, the mail log shows an ordinary message with a RAR attachment. The telemetry that matters is file creation of a DLL by the ClamAV or mail-service scanning process outside ClamAV's temporary scan directory, inbound RAR attachments whose member names carry alternate-data-stream syntax with path separators, and the mail service process starting unexpected children or connecting to Telegram's bot interface; these follow from the described chain and are not an ESET detection list. An update does not remove a DLL or backdoor that was already written, so check the server after patching.
    Defender takeaway
    Patch the mail scanning path first on any internet-facing Windows mail server, then look for unexpected DLLs and persistence and treat stored mail as exposed, since HELLAGE steals email (ESET Research, 2026-10-28).

    Moldova's government digital services: attackers used flaws in services reachable without authentication to query about one million national identification numbers and extract data on about 750,000 of them between 19 and 23 October, and the Prime Minister says AI allegedly helped find the flaws

    Moldova's Electronic Governance Agency (AGE) reported on 2026-10-27 that between 19 and 23 October attackers queried about one million national identification numbers (IDNPs) through state digital services and that, on preliminary estimates, data sets for about 750,000 of them were extracted; MOLDPRES had put the access requests at about 11 million on 2026-10-24 (Moldova Electronic Governance Agency, 2026-10-27; MOLDPRES, 2026-10-24). AGE's technical explanation is that the attackers exploited vulnerabilities in services reachable without authentication and then used the data they obtained for further extractions; the first attacks hit an electronic service that logged the legal basis of processing incorrectly, which is why citizens saw entries for civil-status document requests and driving-licence issue in the data-access history of the EVO app (Moldova Electronic Governance Agency, 2026-10-27). Data that may be affected, not every category for every person, includes name, date and place of birth, first-degree relatives, insured status, family doctor, social benefits, pensions, disability, driving licence, photograph, vehicles and companies owned or run, IBAN, tax debts and payment history; AGE says card data is not held on the platform and it found no unauthorised change to state registers (Moldova Electronic Governance Agency, 2026-10-27).

    AGE stopped the extraction on 23 October at 11:30, reviewed 82 digital services and suspended the exposed ones, and its timeline says 82 public services and more than 30 information systems and administration panels were disconnected as a precaution on 23 October; it blocked further attempts on 25 and 27 October, and the Prime Minister's account is that 30 of the 82 services were disconnected (Moldova Electronic Governance Agency, 2026-10-27; MOLDPRES, 2026-10-28). The Prime Minister says the incident is not isolated, that foreign state actors are being investigated and that the attackers automated the extraction, with the use of artificial intelligence allegedly facilitating the identification and exploitation of the vulnerabilities (MOLDPRES, 2026-10-28). Moldova has not attributed the attack to any country or group (The Record, 2026-10-28).

    Exposure
    A public e-service of a federal, cantonal or communal administration that returns person data for an identifier without authentication, or whose output feeds other registers, since AGE says the extraction chained from unauthenticated services into further data (Moldova Electronic Governance Agency, 2026-10-27).
    Detection
    Citizens noticed the abuse because the data-access history in the EVO app showed processing for civil-status document requests and driving-licence issue that they had not made, so a data-subject-visible access log is a detection channel (Moldova Electronic Governance Agency, 2026-10-27). On the server side the relevant telemetry is per-service request volume and sequential or bulk identifier lookups from one source; the sources give no breakdown of the request pattern, so that is an inference from about 11 million requests against about one million numbers.
    Defender takeaway
    List the public services that answer for a person identifier without authentication and check what each one returns, whether it feeds other registers and whether its access log records the true purpose; rate limits and alerts on bulk lookups belong in front of every such service.

    KeyDrift: Talos reports UAT-11473 exploiting CVE-2026-20127 in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN controllers to run an implant inside the control-plane daemon that collects data-plane keys, with infrastructure overlapping Volt Typhoon's Silent Train network

    Cisco Talos reports that UAT-11473 exploited CVE-2026-20127, an authentication bypass in the peering authentication of Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controller (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28), and then installed a custom framework it calls KeyDrift (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-28). CISA has listed the flaw in its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog since 2026-02-25 (CISA KEV, 2026-10-28). Cisco's advisory (CVSS 10.0) says an unauthenticated remote attacker can log in as an internal high-privileged non-root user and reach NETCONF to change the SD-WAN fabric configuration, and that there is no workaround (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28). Cisco's advisory, revised on 2026-10-28 to link Talos's post, says its PSIRT is aware of limited exploitation (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28). Lumen's Black Lotus Labs reported on 2026-10-28 that Volt Typhoon had exploited the flaw since late 2025 and after Cisco's February disclosure (Lumen Black Lotus Labs, 2026-10-28), and Talos assesses with high confidence that UAT-11473's operating infrastructure overlaps considerably with Volt Typhoon's Silent Train network (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-28).

    KeyDrift runs inside the control-plane daemon. The actor edits the runit launchers of sshd and of a look-alike aaamgr service and remounts /rootfs.rw executable: the sshd launcher starts a script that injects the PolyFlame library into every running vdaemon process by ptrace, and the aaamgr launcher runs a packed PolyFlame loader directly (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-28). PolyFlame is tasked in-band over the control-plane traffic and offers a command, file-write and file-read backdoor and an intermediate command relay; Talos recovered no hardcoded address, port or domain (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-28). A second component, LittleFilch, hooks SSL_read in vdaemon and collects peer system addresses and 32-byte values Talos assesses to be OMP-distributed IPsec data-plane keys, writing them RSA-encrypted to /var/tmp (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-28). Talos lists observed targeting as space and satellite services, defense and aerospace technology, and policy research, calls the activity historical and does not say how many controllers were affected (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-28).

    Exposure
    Every Catalyst SD-WAN Controller, Manager and Validator regardless of configuration, on-premises or in Cisco's hosted clouds, on a release below the first fixed one in the advisory's table; Cisco says controllers with ports exposed to the internet are at risk (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28).
    Detection
    On the controller's authentication log, Cisco says to audit /var/log/auth.log for accepted public-key logins of the vmanage-admin account from addresses that are not among the configured System IPs, and to validate control-connection peering events, those of peer type vmanage first, against maintenance windows and asset inventory (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28). On the host, Talos names modified runit run scripts that remount the overlay root executable and start a background script, an aaamgr service that runs a compiled, packed ELF, extra libraries mapped into vdaemon with ptrace activity against it and encrypted staging files under /var/tmp (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-28). Cisco asks for admin-tech files and a TAC case when compromise is suspected (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28).

    Triage: an aaamgr service that runs as a compiled ELF is the discriminator, because the legitimate component is a Python package and does not normally appear that way (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-28).

    Defender takeaway
    Upgrade to the fixed releases and restrict the control components' management ports, then treat any controller that was internet-reachable on a vulnerable release as a compromise-assessment target, because Lumen dates exploitation of the flaw to late 2025, before Cisco's February disclosure, and the implant runs in the process that handles the fabric's control sessions.

    testmetest: an npm worm in the Miasma and Shai-Hulud family now starts when an application imports the package, so install with --ignore-scripts no longer protects, and JFrog finds 42 third-party GitHub repositories already armed with its publisher

    SafeDep counts 19 npm packages from the same operator between 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-27, eight of them published through four npm accounts, that carry a worm sharing code with the June Miasma worm and adding the token-revocation wiper of Mini Shai-Hulud; SafeDep has not confirmed the lineage, and JFrog says its evidence does not establish that the original Miasma operators are responsible (SafeDep, 2026-10-27; JFrog Security Research, 2026-10-26). The install trigger has changed twice: first a command expansion in a binding.gyp file, with no install script; then a postinstall script that loads a native addon which decodes and starts the worm; and, since SafeDep's update of 27 October, an addon that runs when an application imports the package, so a hardening step that disables install scripts does not stop it (SafeDep, 2026-10-27).

    The payload exits on CIS locales and in known analysis sandboxes. It steals SSH keys, registry and Git credentials, .env files, browser logins, AI-tool, cloud, Vault and Kubernetes secrets and GitHub Actions secrets read from runner memory (SafeDep, 2026-10-27). It exfiltrates by committing encrypted data, with the victim's encrypted GitHub token, to a new public repository in the victim's own account, injects a GitLab CI job that requests npm and PyPI OIDC tokens, adds its loader to the victim's npm packages and publishes new patch versions, republishes to PyPI and RubyGems, commits a fake CodeQL workflow and AI-agent configuration files that infect the host again, and copies itself over SSH to the hosts in known_hosts (SafeDep, 2026-10-27). A persistence service checks the stolen GitHub token every 45 seconds for 48 hours and on an authentication failure deletes the home directory (SafeDep, 2026-10-27). JFrog found 42 armed third-party GitHub repositories of 11 legitimate owners with hooks for AI coding assistants and IDEs, and says the payload blobs were large enough to evade GitHub's search and API paths (JFrog Security Research, 2026-10-26).

    Exposure
    Any developer endpoint, build agent or CI runner that installed or imported one of the operator's packages, and any GitHub or GitLab account whose token such a host held (SafeDep, 2026-10-27).
    Detection
    In package metadata, a binding.gyp whose sources field runs node in a package with no C or C++ sources, a postinstall that requires a .node file and discards errors beside a large high-entropy file, and prebuilt addons with no native source; on endpoints and CI runners, a node process that downloads or finds and runs the Bun runtime and a child node process started from a temporary path named after its process ID; in GitHub and GitLab audit logs, a new public repository in a developer's own account with a generic project description, commits with a fixed automated-sync message, commits that add AI-assistant configuration files (.claude, .cursor, .gemini, .vscode tasks, CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md), an injected CI job that requests OIDC tokens, and unexpected patch releases of your own packages (SafeDep, 2026-10-27).
    Defender takeaway
    An install that ran with scripts disabled is not clean once the package has been imported; for an affected host SafeDep's order is to disconnect it, remove persistence first, rotate all credentials, check the accounts for the repositories and commits above and reinstall the operating system (SafeDep, 2026-10-27). Because the wiper is triggered by the token's revocation, the host is isolated and cleaned before the GitHub token is revoked.

    NOTABLENATOB2

    Huntress: phished MDM enrolments in Hexnode and Rippling give attackers a management path that brings ScreenConnect back after it has been removed

    Huntress reports several recent incidents in which attackers used legitimate mobile-device-management platforms, not flaws in them, as a trusted management path, naming Hexnode and Rippling as the two it saw and noting the list is not definitive (Huntress, 2026-10-28). In the first case, proposal-themed emails led a user to download an MSI named like a DocuSign installer that installed Hexnode components (gateway, agent and PostgreSQL components); ScreenConnect followed shortly after and connected to an attacker relay, the managed-service provider removed it, and it came back several hours later to the same relay while the Hexnode client was still installed (Huntress, 2026-10-28). In the second, a receptionist clicked a link in an email about tax planning that led to a fake Teams calendar page; about a minute later a Rippling enrolment registry key appeared, the agent was downloaded into a SYSTEM profile path and installed with SYSTEM rights, and about 35 minutes after that the attacker used the MDM to stage and run a ScreenConnect installer as SYSTEM (Huntress, 2026-10-28).

    Exposure
    A managed Windows endpoint whose user can run an installer or follow an enrolment link, in an organisation without an inventory of its authorised MDM tenants, agents and RMM instances (Huntress, 2026-10-28).
    Detection
    The chain Huntress describes as a stronger signal is social engineering, then a new MDM enrolment, then an RMM installation, then outbound communication with an unfamiliar relay, correlated across identity, MDM, endpoint and network telemetry; in endpoint telemetry that is an MSI run from a browser download folder that installs an MDM agent, an enrolment registry key on a device nobody enrolled, a new SYSTEM-level agent in a vendor directory shortly after a click, and an RMM installed by that agent (Huntress, 2026-10-28). A repeat of the same RMM relay after the RMM was removed, as in the Hexnode case, points to a remaining MDM client as a possible source (Huntress, 2026-10-28).

    Triage: a corporate enrolment started by IT looks similar in process telemetry; the discriminators are a tenant that is not in the authorised inventory, a trigger that is an emailed link or a user-run installer instead of IT's enrolment workflow, and an RMM install within minutes (an inference from Huntress's two cases).

    Defender takeaway
    Treat an unexpected MDM enrolment as a security event and keep an inventory of authorised tenants; after removing a rogue RMM, unenrol the device from the unauthorised tenant, disable accounts the attacker created, revoke sessions and tokens, reset credentials and reimage the endpoint if its integrity cannot be confirmed, as Huntress advises (Huntress, 2026-10-28).
