    VAPOR PANDA: CrowdStrike reports a China-nexus actor running the ARTEX agent framework against more than 200 organisations from 17 October, among them foreign ministries in Europe and a Greek government entity, and deploying the BLOODALCHEMY backdoor

    CrowdStrike OverWatch reports that from 2026-10-17 a single ARTEX controller linked to VAPOR PANDA infrastructure directed exploitation against entities in at least 14 countries over 11 days, and that the campaign reached more than 200 organisations, including foreign ministries in Europe (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29). One engagement focused on a single European country, its foreign ministry, presidency, prime minister's office and defence, justice, finance and intelligence entities, and another targeted 19 US organisations in pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, defence, nuclear and finance (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29). CrowdStrike confirmed post-exploitation at nine organisations and password spraying and credential scanning against at least 12 more, among them government entities in Europe (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29). CrowdStrike states that the campaign is separate from the Storm-0940 ARTEX activity Microsoft reported and is VAPOR PANDA's first observed use of AI-driven tooling (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29).

    The framework ran mainly on the DeepSeek model, with a human operator steering the agents at decision points while they pursued web, cloud, container, Active Directory, mail and VPN paths in parallel (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29). In transcripts at a US pharmaceutical company the agent chained an unsigned SAML assertion with an XML external entity flaw to query the cloud instance metadata service and obtain a role that could issue administration commands to thousands of instances; at a second it deployed web shells, abused an unauthenticated orchestration service for root in a container environment, read LAPS passwords and abused resource-based constrained delegation (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29). Against a Greek government entity the operator supplied a 2022 directory export from the mail server whose authentication keys had likely not been rotated; the agent forged an administrator token, read mailboxes and used VPN configurations from a user's sent mail, with a VPN client key the operator described as coming from a May 2024 email leak, to reach the internal network (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29). The operator also told the agents to find domain Windows hosts without the CrowdStrike Falcon sensor (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29). At a Middle East interior ministry and a Latin American foreign ministry the actor loaded the BLOODALCHEMY backdoor through a legitimate GameHook binary renamed svchost.exe that loads a malicious DLL (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29). No Swiss target is named.

    Exposure
    Government, mail, identity and VPN estates where a directory export or backup holding authentication keys has never been rotated, SAML service providers that accept unsigned assertions, and cloud instances that still allow instance metadata service version 1; CrowdStrike names foreign ministries and government entities in Europe among the targets.
    Detection
    Telemetry classes are authentication logs (password spraying and credential scanning against many accounts, an administrator token minted outside the identity provider, VPN logins that use a configuration or key from an old mailbox or leak), cloud control-plane audit (administration commands issued to many instances through the cloud systems manager, instance metadata queries from an application), process lineage (a legitimate GameHook binary renamed svchost.exe loading a malicious DLL) and endpoint inventory queries for hosts without the security agent; CrowdStrike lists ARTEX-named command-line parameters, cloud roles and a directory under ProgramData as hunt markers (CrowdStrike, 2026-10-29).
    Defender takeaway
    Rotate keys held in old directory exports and backups, require signed SAML assertions and IMDSv2, and hunt authentication and cloud audit logs for the parallel, many-target pattern an operator-steered agent produces; CrowdStrike expects China-nexus actors to keep adopting agent frameworks.

    Silent Train: Lumen's Black Lotus Labs describes the rotating relay network Volt Typhoon used for one likely and four confirmed zero-day campaigns against management and control-plane platforms (Quest KACE, Versa Director, N-able N-central, Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN, Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator)

    Lumen's Black Lotus Labs reports that Volt Typhoon used a covert framework it calls Silent Train, first seen in late 2023, for the phases that need protection: exploitation, credential access, data exfiltration and continued access to victims (Lumen Black Lotus Labs, 2026-10-28). A session starts from a virtual private server outside the US, passes through compromised non-US SOHO and IoT devices and leaves through a compromised US device before the victim; more than 27 device classes were enrolled, from TP-Link routers and Netgear Orbi devices to Fortinet appliances and CyberPanel servers, and the enterprise platforms stayed in chains for one to three months as final hops (Lumen Black Lotus Labs, 2026-10-28). The operators exploit exposed web interfaces on the relay devices and deploy only what chained SSH needs, namely temporary SSH services, keys, configuration files and living-off-the-land utilities, with reverse tunnels over open-source proxies in later variants; sessions averaged about four hours, and at the end the operators removed all artifacts and dismantled the chain (Lumen Black Lotus Labs, 2026-10-28).

    Lumen says its monitoring helped identify one likely and four confirmed zero-day campaigns against endpoint and network management platforms and SD-WAN orchestrators: Quest KACE, where the activity looked like an undisclosed flaw, affected more than 30 victims and fell after Quest's October 2025 updates (a moderate-confidence assessment); Versa Director in 2024; the N-able N-central flaws CVE-2025-8875 and CVE-2025-8876; Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN through CVE-2026-20127, exploited by late 2025 and after Cisco's February 2026 disclosure; and Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator through the zero-day later designated CVE-2026-16812 (Lumen Black Lotus Labs, 2026-10-28). Post-incident analysis found in-memory webshells, credential harvesting and abuse of native platform features such as endpoint control and remote file transfers, and in several cases erased or altered logs (Lumen Black Lotus Labs, 2026-10-28). The victims it names are US government, telecommunications, defence-industrial, managed-service and critical-infrastructure organisations, concentrated in Hawaii, California and Texas, and it names no European or Swiss victim (Lumen Black Lotus Labs, 2026-10-28). The Hacker News carries the same findings from a report Lumen shared with it (The Hacker News, 2026-10-28).

    Exposure
    Internet-reachable management and control-plane platforms (endpoint management, SD-WAN controllers and orchestrators, out-of-band administration), and edge devices with exposed web interfaces, which can be enrolled as relay hops without any data of the owner being the target.
    Detection
    Telemetry classes are administrative logins, API calls, remote file transfers and configuration changes on management platforms outside normal administration hours or workflow, in-memory web shells that leave no file on disk, and, on edge devices, temporary SSH services, unfamiliar keys and outbound reverse tunnels to unfamiliar upstream hosts (Lumen Black Lotus Labs, 2026-10-28).
    Defender takeaway
    Restrict the administrative interfaces of management and control-plane platforms to a VPN, jump host or management network and prioritise their patching, as Lumen advises, and treat an edge device that shows temporary SSH services or reverse tunnels as a possible relay in someone else's intrusion chain.

    CVE-2026-76418, Cisco Secure Firewall ASA and Secure FTD: a crafted request to the remote access SSL VPN reloads the device when a tunnel group uses secondary authentication; Cisco reports active exploitation and CISA lists it (CVSS 3.1 8.6)

    Cisco's advisory of 2026-10-28 describes CVE-2026-76418, a logic error in the remote access SSL VPN service of Secure Firewall ASA and Secure FTD: an unauthenticated, remote attacker who sends a crafted request while connecting to the SSL VPN makes the device reload, and a successful VPN login is not required to reach the affected processing path (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28). Cisco says its PSIRT became aware of active exploitation in October 2026 and names no actor or victim (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28); CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 2026-10-29 (CISA, 2026-10-29), and CERT-FR restates the advisory (ANSSI / CERT-FR, 2026-10-29). The stated impact is a reload and denial of service, scored CVSS 3.1 8.6 with availability as the only impact; no code execution or data access is described (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28).

    The first fixed releases are ASA 9.16.4.104 (for 9.16 and earlier), 9.18.4.96, 9.20.4.50, 9.22.3.200, 9.23.1.48, 9.24.1.27 and 9.24.10.1, and FTD 7.0.11 (for 7.0 and earlier), 7.2.13, 7.4.9, 7.6.7, 7.7.14, 10.0.3 and 10.1.1 (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28). Cisco lists no workaround; its mitigation is to remove secondary authentication from the tunnel group, which lowers the security posture of the remote access VPN, or, where DefaultWEBVPNGroup does not use secondary authentication, to disable the tunnel group list with no tunnel-group-list enable (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28). Cisco confirms that Secure FMC, Cloud-delivered Firewall, cdFMC and Secure Access are not affected (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28).

    Exposure
    An ASA or FTD unit below the first fixed release of its branch whose running configuration has webvpn enabled on an interface and secondary-authentication-server-group on any tunnel group; the default tunnel group DefaultWEBVPNGroup counts even when the tunnel group list is disabled. On FTD, read the deployed remote access VPN policy and the resulting configuration in FMC or FDM (Devices > VPN > Remote Access) (Cisco PSIRT, 2026-10-28).
    Detection
    Neither Cisco nor CISA publishes an indicator. The mechanism points at device availability telemetry: unexpected reloads, HA failover flaps, uptime resets and crash files on units that terminate the remote access VPN, read against VPN authentication logs for connection attempts from external addresses that never complete a login.

    Triage: a reload also follows upgrades, HA events and hardware faults; a reload that coincides with unauthenticated SSL VPN connection attempts from an external address is the pattern the stated mechanism produces.

    Defender takeaway
    Upgrade the units that terminate remote access SSL VPN with secondary authentication this week, perimeter units first; Cisco states denial of service as the only impact, so patching leaves no further step, and removing secondary authentication as a stop-gap trades an outage risk for a weaker VPN login.

    NOTABLENATOA2

    WordPress core 4.7 to 7.1.3: an unauthenticated race in the core update process can lead to remote code execution, fixed in 7.1.4 and backported to every supported branch, with Wordfence reporting exploitation attempts and no CVE assigned in the sources

    WordPress 7.1.4, released on 2026-10-28, fixes a weakness in the upgrade process that can lead to remote code execution during the upgrade window, and backports follow for every branch that receives security fixes, currently through 4.7 (WordPress.org, 2026-10-28); WordPress's release documentation counts 22 vulnerabilities, as Wordfence reports (Wordfence, 2026-10-29). During a core update with a complete package, the new files are unpacked into a predictable directory under wp-content/upgrade; the temporary copy includes the installer, and an unauthenticated attacker who requests it at the right moment can interact with the staged copy and reach PHP code execution (Wordfence, 2026-10-29). Wordfence scores it CVSS 8.1 for versions 4.7 through 7.1.3 and states that the attack complexity is high, because the attacker needs web access to the staging tree and must win a race that lasted only seconds in its lab (Wordfence, 2026-10-29). Patchstack reads the same flaw as needing wp-content/upgrade/ to be reachable over the web, notes that the sink dates back to WordPress 2.8 and that the window returns on every update, unattended ones included, and would not call it practical to exploit (Patchstack, 2026-10-28).

    Wordfence reports that its new firewall rule matched within nine minutes of deployment and that it recorded more than 18,500 requests for staged setup-config.php installer paths in the first 21 hours, with two large sources polling small groups of sites for about 14.5 hours, which it reads as attempts to catch the update window (Wordfence, 2026-10-29). No source reports a successful compromise, and no source assigns a CVE identifier. The fix randomises the staging directory, adds an index guard and prevents unsafe promotion of staged configuration data (Wordfence, 2026-10-29). Most sites receive the release through automatic background updates, which Wordfence says should be checked rather than assumed (Wordfence, 2026-10-29).

    Exposure
    Any WordPress site on 4.7 to 7.1.3 that runs a full-package core update, automatic ones included, with wp-content/upgrade/ reachable from the internet; Dashboard > Updates shows the running version, and the fixed releases are 7.1.4 and the backport of each branch (7.0.8, 6.9.11, 6.8.12, 6.7.11, 6.6.11 and the earlier branches listed by Wordfence down to 4.7.39) (Wordfence, 2026-10-29).
    Detection
    In web server access logs, requests under wp-content/upgrade/ and for setup-config.php inside a staging directory, repeated from one source against a few sites as Wordfence saw (Wordfence, 2026-10-29); between updates the directory holds only an index.php (Patchstack, 2026-10-28). Where an update ran while the site was unpatched and the directory was reachable, confirm that wp-config.php and the database it names are unchanged, because the fix exists to stop unsafe promotion of staged configuration data.

    Triage: a request to a staging path outside an update window finds only an index.php and is probing, not compromise; a successful response from the staged installer during an update is the event that matters.

    Defender takeaway
    Confirm that the automatic update completed on every site instead of assuming it, and deny web access to wp-content/upgrade/ at the web server, because a firewall that WordPress itself loads cannot block the request: the staged copy loads from its own files (Patchstack, 2026-10-28); Wordfence's blocking rule helps only where its firewall is set up to run before direct PHP requests reach their file (Wordfence, 2026-10-29).

    ROUTINENATOA2

    privatim revises its cloud guidance for Swiss public bodies (Merkblatt v3.02, 2 October 2026): sensitive data stays under the body's own keys, standard SaaS from Microsoft, Google, AWS and SAP is in most cases inadmissible for secrecy-bound data, and top management signs the residual risk

    privatim, the conference of the Swiss data-protection authorities, dates version 3.02 of its guidance "Cloud-specific risks and measures" to 2 October 2026 (privatim, 2026-10-02); it replaces the version of 3 February 2022 (DCOD, 2026-10-27). For particularly sensitive personal data the public body must encrypt, the keys may be available only to it, and outsourcing such data, or data under a statutory duty of confidentiality, to SaaS from large international providers, with Microsoft, Google, AWS and SAP named, is in most cases inadmissible because of the untenable risk (privatim, 2026-10-02). Keys may stay with the provider only where no untenable risk follows and the provider is bound by contract to use them only with the body's express consent and to log every access (privatim, 2026-10-02).

    The body, or its top management, is to confirm in writing that it has understood the risks and accepts the residual risk, and data the cloud services generate about their users, such as telemetry and logging data, is to be treated with the same care and contract terms as the content data (privatim, 2026-10-02). The five risk areas are contract design, places of processing including foreign authority access, confidentiality with encryption and key management, user data, and sub-processors (privatim, 2026-10-02).

    Defender takeaway
    The guidance addresses public bodies subject to Swiss data-protection law; a body that runs or plans standard SaaS from the named providers for sensitive or secrecy-bound data is advised to keep its own encryption keys, to have top management sign the residual risk, and to hold provider telemetry and logs about its users to the same contract terms.

    ROUTINENATOB2

    ANNUAL REPORT: Cisco Talos Incident Response trends Q3 2026: public administration tied with finance as the most targeted sector, help-desk vishing into remote-management tools, and ransomware operators uninstalling and killing EDR

    Cisco Talos Incident Response reports that in Q3 2026 financial organisations made up 18 percent of its engagements, tying with public administration as the most targeted sector (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-29). Phishing was the leading initial-access technique at 37 percent of engagements and exploitation of public-facing applications second at 33 percent; the phishing cases include numerous actors who phone the victim as the IT help desk to get remote access allowed through a legitimate remote monitoring and management (RMM) tool (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-29). Ransomware and pre-ransomware cases were 27 percent of engagements, with The Gentlemen, Qilin, Rhysida and the closed group Interlock among the families and a new variant, VYPR, that Talos calls operationally immature: it had no leak site when seen, reused leaked Conti-derived source code and exfiltrated over several days (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-29).

    The VYPR intrusion began with a vishing call that had the user start a Microsoft Quick Assist session; the actor then used RMM software that ran PowerShell to download and install an MSI from a remote address, with MeshAgent, Rclone, PsExec, an IP scanner and mstsc as tools, and disabled EDR with several vulnerable drivers (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-29). Interlock operators repeatedly tried to uninstall Cisco Secure Endpoint, used PsExec and taskkill to end security services and integrity checkers, turned off Windows Defender real-time protection and cleared event logs (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-29). Talos names insufficient logging (over 40 percent of engagements), weak credential and secret handling, and absent or incomplete MFA as the most common weaknesses, and recommends centralised logging with at least 90 days of retention (Cisco Talos, 2026-10-29).

    Exposure
    An organisation whose help desk or staff can be reached by phone and whose endpoints permit Quick Assist or an unapproved RMM agent; Talos names public administration among the most targeted sectors in its caseload.
    Detection
    In process-creation and software-install telemetry, an RMM or Quick Assist session started after an inbound call, followed by PowerShell run under the RMM agent that downloads an installer from an external address; in endpoint and service-control telemetry, attempts to uninstall the EDR agent, PsExec or taskkill ending security services, Defender real-time protection switched off, vulnerable kernel drivers loaded, and event logs cleared.
    Defender takeaway
    Treat a remote-support session that nobody at the help desk started as the vishing signal and inventory which RMM tools and Quick Assist are permitted on endpoints; alert on EDR uninstall and security-service termination as a ransomware precursor; and centralise logs with the 90-day minimum retention Talos recommends, because actors delete logs on the endpoint.
