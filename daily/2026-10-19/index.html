    01Active threats, incidents & disclosures1 item

    NOTABLENATOB2

    Phishing lures sent as ICS calendar invites land in Outlook and Gmail calendars without a click and stay after the email is deleted; EvilTokens used them for Microsoft 365 device-code phishing

    Netcraft reports that attackers send phishing lures as ICS calendar files because the "text/calendar" type is treated as low risk by major email clients, and that the mail client's normal invite handling then places the lure in the victim's calendar with no user interaction, often keeping it there after the original email is deleted (Netcraft, 2026-10-16). In Netcraft's client table, Outlook desktop, Outlook on the web and Outlook Mobile with Exchange Online and Gmail with Google Calendar add the event automatically, mark it tentative and keep it after the email is deleted, Apple Calendar connected to Exchange Online often does the same, and Apple Mail with iCloud does not add it (Netcraft, 2026-10-16).

    Campaigns using the EvilTokens toolkit, which steals Microsoft 365 tokens, used ICS invites disguised as meetings, alerts or voicemail notifications; the link leads to a page that gives the user a device code and tells them to enter it on Microsoft's device authorization page, which grants an attacker's device access to the account through OAuth (Netcraft, 2026-10-16). Netcraft now also sees smaller scams carrying ordinary phishing content inside ICS files (Netcraft, 2026-10-16). Sublime Security reports "exponential growth" in calendar phishing, with lures such as voicemail notices, payment receipts and renewal warnings that link to fake Microsoft, Google or PayPal logins or give a phone number, and Cofense says some senders use legitimate platforms such as Zoom to send the invitations, so filtering those platforms would also block real meetings (The Guardian, 2026-10-11).

    Exposure
    Users of Outlook with Exchange Online and of Gmail with Google Calendar, whose clients add external invites to the calendar on their own (Netcraft, 2026-10-16).
    Detection
    Netcraft names no telemetry; from the delivery path it describes, the traces are inbound external messages carrying a calendar part or ICS attachment in mail-flow logs, tentative events from external organizers the user never accepted in calendar audit logs, and, for the EvilTokens variant, a device-code sign-in to the account shortly after such an event (Netcraft, 2026-10-16). When responding to a reported phish of this kind, remove the calendar entry as well as the email, because the entry persists after the email is deleted (Netcraft, 2026-10-16).
    Defender takeaway
    Treat email-attached invites as part of the phishing attack surface: review whether the mail client settings add invites from outside the organization automatically for staff who do not expect them, and extend phishing guidance and purge procedures to calendar entries for those who do (Netcraft, 2026-10-16).
