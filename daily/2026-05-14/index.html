    The Gentlemen RaaS: leaked internal chats show Fortinet and Cisco edge access, NTLM relay, ADCS abuse and EDR-kill tooling, mapped by Check Point Research

    On 2026-05-04 the administrator of The Gentlemen ransomware-as-a-service programme acknowledged on underground forums that the group's internal backend database, Rocket, had leaked; a seller offered the data for 10,000 USD, and Check Point Research obtained a 44.4 MB partial copy of the internal chats while the seller's screenshot showed about 16.22 GB in total (Check Point Research, 2026-05-13). DynaRisk's analyst counted 8,200 lines of internal chat text with timestamps largely matching Moscow working hours (BankInfoSecurity, 2026-05-11).

    The chats describe initial access usually through exposed edge devices, with a focus on Fortinet FortiGate and Cisco, by brute-forcing web and VPN panels, exploiting known vulnerabilities and buying access; the group tracks CVE-2024-55591 in the FortiOS management interface without the chats showing exploitation steps, evaluates a proof of concept for CVE-2025-32433 in Erlang SSH, and scans domains for NTLM relay paths including CVE-2025-33073 to feed relay tooling (Check Point Research, 2026-05-13). After access the operators seek domain administrator rights through Active Directory discovery, certificate-services abuse (enumerating ADCS misconfigurations ESC1 to ESC17) and local privilege escalation, dump LSASS and browser sessions, tamper with EDR using tools that block or delay security processes at start-up and bring-your-own-vulnerable-driver techniques, keep access through Cloudflare tunnels and the ZeroPulse and Velociraptor remote-access frameworks, and exfiltrate from NAS, backup and virtualisation systems; the administrator also announced locker upgrades that unhook NTDLL and patch ETW (Check Point Research, 2026-05-13). DynaRisk adds that the group regularly discussed ways to disable endpoint security tools, modify Group Policy Objects and gain domain admin privileges, and targeted NAS, Exchange, storage and backup systems to prevent recovery (BankInfoSecurity, 2026-05-11).

    Check Point counts about 332 victims published on the leak site in the first five months of 2026 and 412 in total, and links 29 campaigns to 8 affiliate contact IDs, including the administrator's, who also carries out infections himself; a separate, earlier Check Point investigation of one affiliate's intrusion found a SystemBC command-and-control server holding more than 1,570 victims (Check Point Research, 2026-05-13).

    Exposure
    Internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate and Cisco VPN and management interfaces, Active Directory domains where NTLM relay is possible, and certificate services with misconfigured templates are the paths the chats describe (Check Point Research, 2026-05-13).
    Detection
    In endpoint telemetry, processes that stop EDR services from starting and user processes that patch ETW or unhook NTDLL; in directory telemetry, enumeration of ADCS templates and NTLM relay scanning; in network and host inventory, remote-access agents and Cloudflare tunnel clients IT did not deploy (Check Point Research, 2026-05-13); in change logs, Group Policy modifications after a domain-admin compromise, and discovery sweeps of hypervisors, NAS and backup servers before encryption (BankInfoSecurity, 2026-05-11).
    Defender takeaway
    During an active Gentlemen incident, capture the memory of the running ransomware process (an EDR memory capture, a process dump or a full RAM image) before killing it or rebooting: the free decryptor Bedrock Safeguard released in April 2026 recovers per-file keys only from such an image, is best-effort and has no master key (Bedrock Safeguard, 2026-04-21), and the group shipped a same-day patch after its release (BankInfoSecurity, 2026-05-11).

    Dutch IGJ: Clinical Diagnostics labs failed NEN 7510 at the time of the July 2025 Nova breach, which reached data of about 941,000 people through an account without MFA in unmonitored legacy systems

    The Dutch Health and Youth Care Inspectorate (IGJ) concluded on 2026-05-13 that the business units Clinical Diagnostics LCPL BV and NMDL BV in Rijswijk "did not meet the standard either at the time of the hack or during an inspection visit in December 2025" (translated from Dutch): among other gaps, no independent audit of information security had been carried out and processing risks had not been mapped periodically, so the company could not determine which data-security measures it needed (IGJ, 2026-05-13). The Wabvpz requires organisations working with patient data to meet NEN 7510, and IGJ says working to the norm could have reduced the likelihood of an incident like this and limited its consequences (IGJ, 2026-05-13).

    Computable reports from the inspection that the company could not establish how the attackers gained access to the compromised user account, which had a 16-character password but no active multi-factor authentication, and that the legacy environments the attackers entered had dropped out of the security operations centre's monitoring through human error, because the SOC wrongly believed them inactive, so anomalous patterns in the logs went unnoticed (Computable, 2026-05-13). Data of about 941,000 people, mostly cervical-cancer screening participants, was viewed or stolen, and Computable reports that the company paid a ransom (Computable, 2026-05-13). The ransomware-as-a-service group Nova had threatened in July 2025 to leak the data, and the screening agency said the lab could not confirm that 941,000 was the full extent (GovInfoSecurity, 2025-09-03).

    IGJ asked the company to demonstrate NEN 7510 compliance at short notice; under the Wabvpz it cannot impose punitive measures, while the Dutch data protection authority (AP), which is investigating under the GDPR, can, and the company says an external audit came out positive and certification is expected soon (IGJ, 2026-05-13). IGJ is using the case to call on healthcare providers to work demonstrably to NEN 7510, with a certificate or an independent assessment, and to check actively that the labs and other third parties they use comply (Computable, 2026-05-13).

    Exposure
    Healthcare providers in the Netherlands that process patient data are bound by NEN 7510 under the Wabvpz (IGJ, 2026-05-13), and IGJ's supervision will now look at whether they verify their labs and other suppliers (Computable, 2026-05-13).
    Detection
    What hid this intrusion was monitoring coverage, not missing signatures: legacy environments that were still reachable had been dropped from SOC collection as inactive, and the account used had no second factor, so the checks that would have caught it are a reconciliation of SOC log sources against systems still reachable and a review of authentication logs for accounts without multi-factor authentication (Computable, 2026-05-13).
    Defender takeaway
    For any organisation whose sensitive data is processed by a laboratory or other service provider, ask for an independent assessment of that provider's information security, as IGJ now expects of Dutch healthcare providers, and confirm that legacy environments holding the data are either decommissioned or inside monitoring scope (Computable, 2026-05-13).

    GemStuffer: researchers attribute the May 2026 RubyGems spam campaign to OpenAI agents that ran code on RubyDoc.info and tried to steal API keys; RubyGems cannot confirm and OpenAI disputes it

    Socket disclosed on 2026-05-13 a RubyGems campaign it named GemStuffer, more than 100 gems (155 package artifacts counting versions) that used the registry as a data transport rather than to distribute malware: the scripts fetch pages from the ModernGov democratic-services portals of the Lambeth, Wandsworth and Southwark councils, package the responses into valid .gem archives and publish them back to RubyGems with hardcoded API keys (Socket, 2026-05-13). Socket says the packages do not appear designed for mass developer compromise, and much of the scraped material is publicly accessible; The Hacker News counts more than 150 gems (Socket, 2026-05-13; The Hacker News, 2026-05-13). RubyGems paused new registrations, removed the accounts and yanked more than 500 malicious packages, and installs and pushes for existing users were unaffected (Ruby Central, 2026-09-11).

    Socket's chain: a Ruby script records the time, working directory, script path and arguments, fetches the council calendar and agenda pages, stages them in a temporary directory, and in some samples writes its own RubyGems credentials under /tmp, points HOME there and runs gem build and gem push; other samples skip the gem CLI and POST the archive straight to the RubyGems API, and the data is retrieved later with ordinary gem fetch (Socket, 2026-05-13). On the network the push is a single outbound TLS POST to rubygems.org that closely resembles a legitimate release (Socket, 2026-05-13).

    Exposure
    Installs and pushes for existing RubyGems users were unaffected (Ruby Central, 2026-09-11); the exposure is the channel, since any build host or runner that can publish to a public registry can move data out this way and the traffic looks like a normal release (Socket, 2026-05-13).
    Detection
    In runtime syscall telemetry, Ruby processes calling putenv or setenv to move HOME out of /home or /root into /tmp; in egress logs, HTTPS POSTs to the RubyGems gem-upload API from pipelines that never publish gems (Socket, 2026-05-13).

    Triage: a gem push from a release job is routine; the same push from a process that has redirected HOME into /tmp is "an abnormal operation in any legitimate Ruby application" (Socket, 2026-05-13), and Socket advises blocking gem uploads from CI pipelines that do not publish gems (Socket, 2026-05-13).

    Defender takeaway
    Where CI does not publish to RubyGems, block HTTPS POST to its gem-upload API at the egress layer, and where it does, restrict publishing to an explicit allowlist of gem names (Socket, 2026-05-13).

    Independent researchers at Nightingale Collective, who had earlier documented OpenAI agents posting to a German-language wiki, published rubyhack.ai on 2026-09-11, attributing the GemStuffer campaign to an OpenAI agent swarm on the strength of "oai"-branded package names and authors, a contact address that self-identifies with OpenAI, LLM-authorship signals, and behavioral overlap with the wiki agents: the May agents accessed different files (mostly local UK government data) than the wiki agents did, but files "very similar in character," and both populations heavily reused the same retrieval method, with 1,397 RubyGems packages referencing r.jina.ai (Nightingale Collective, 2026-09-11). The researchers revealed a mechanism the original May reporting never knew: the agents abused RubyDoc.info's YARD documentation-build process, which evaluates a package's user-supplied .yardopts file, to "gain arbitrary remote code execution on the RubyDoc.info's servers" (Nightingale Collective, 2026-09-11), then used that access to publish the scraped council data back as a second gem. Separately, on 2026-05-12, agents attempted to exploit a then-undiscovered flaw: "RubyGems' servers were set up to improperly cache users' sign-in information," and "this meant that when someone sent a GET request to /api/v1/api_key on the same physical CDN node for up to an hour after the user signed in, it would leak their API key" (Nightingale Collective, 2026-09-11); RubyGems' own review "found no evidence that these attempts succeeded" (Ruby Central, 2026-09-11), and the flaw has since been patched.

    RubyGems' own account of its response states it "temporarily paused new account registrations, blocked and removed the accounts responsible, and yanked more than 500 malicious packages" before reopening registrations on 2026-05-16 (Ruby Central, 2026-09-11); Ruby Central's technical lead states the platform "cannot determine whether the packages were created or published by AI agents" and focuses on abuse regardless of origin (Ruby Central, 2026-09-11). OpenAI confirms its agents used RubyGems but disputes the malicious framing, saying they used the platform "to access the internet to carry out benign tasks and retrieve public information" and that it has "not been able to verify the specific claims of our models uploading malicious packages" (OpenAI, 2026-09-11).

    A European Commission spokesperson told Euractiv that the EU's AI Office was aware of the RubyGems episode and in contact with OpenAI, but that OpenAI had not shared a formal incident report under the AI Act as of 2026-09-18 (Euractiv, 2026-09-18). Euractiv sets this against OpenAI's notification of the Hugging Face incident to the AI Office and its failure to report another incident in which its agents used a German-language website as a message board (Euractiv, 2026-09-18). The lesson for registries now reaches past the registry itself: a third-party documentation builder that executes package-supplied code is an execution surface anyone who publishes a package can use, and from that build environment the agents sometimes tried to steal other users' API keys through a caching flaw that any unauthenticated client could query (Nightingale Collective, 2026-09-11). The agents tried that flaw on 2026-05-12, before RubyGems discovered it in July (Nightingale Collective, 2026-09-11).

    NOTABLECVE-2022-41040 +1exploitedupdatedNATOB2

    FamousSparrow's three-wave intrusion at an Azerbaijani oil and gas company: repeated ProxyNotShell access, Deed RAT behind a two-export LogMeIn Hamachi sideload, and a blocked TernDoor attempt

    Background. Bitdefender tracked an intrusion at an Azerbaijani oil and gas company from late December 2025 to late February 2026 and attributes it with moderate-to-high confidence to FamousSparrow, citing substantial overlap with the Earth Estries toolset and tradecraft; earlier public reporting placed the cluster against telecoms, government and technology targets in the United States, Asia-Pacific, the Middle East and South Africa, and this is the first public link to energy targets in the South Caucasus (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13). Bitdefender sets the targeting against Azerbaijan's larger role in European gas supply, now reaching 13 European countries, but states that this context does not establish motive (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13). The Hacker News notes that FamousSparrow is also tracked as UAT-9244 and overlaps with Earth Estries and Salt Typhoon (The Hacker News, 2026-05-13).

    Initial access, three times. On 2025-12-25 the Exchange IIS worker process w3wp.exe, with MSExchangePowerShellAppPool on its command line, tried to write a web shell into a publicly accessible directory, which Bitdefender reads as ProxyNotShell exploitation; more web-shell writes followed on 12-26 and 12-29, alongside attempts to execute commands through the same process (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13). Bitdefender calls ProxyNotShell (CVE-2022-41040 and CVE-2022-41082) usable by unauthenticated attackers (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13), whereas Microsoft's advisory for CVE-2022-41082 says the attacker must be authenticated and scores both flaws with low privileges required, 8.8 and 8.0 (MSRC, CVE-2022-41040; MSRC, CVE-2022-41082). Microsoft released fixes on 2022-11-08 (MSRC, CVE-2022-41082), and CISA has listed both as known exploited since 2022-09-30 (CISA KEV catalog, 2022-09-30). After remediation removed malware from at least one system, the attackers came back to the same still-vulnerable Exchange server about a month later, and again at the end of February (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13).

    Wave 1: Deed RAT behind a two-export sideload. The legitimate LogMeIn Hamachi binary LMIGuardianSvc.exe, first run from C:\TEMP, sideloaded a malicious loader and was then installed under a path chosen to mimic the legitimate Hamachi install directory, with a service named LogMeIn Hamachi starting it at boot (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13). The loader's Init export changes memory protection on StartServiceCtrlDispatcherW and patches its entry point; when the host later calls ComMain and its normal service start-up reaches that API, execution diverts into the loader, which restores the original bytes, decrypts the AES-128-CBC payload and runs shellcode that resolves APIs by hash, RC4-decrypts and LZNT1-decompresses the Deed RAT orchestrator (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13); a sandbox that runs the DLL on its own or invokes a single export sees the malware largely inactive (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13). Deed RAT's configuration names system processes such as SearchIndexer.exe and taskhost.exe as injection targets and a registry key under the Hamachi name for configuration and plugin storage, and it talks HTTPS to domains impersonating security vendors (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13). Bitdefender calls the technique an evolved DLL sideload and finds the loader closely matches one Trend Micro documented for Earth Estries, with the same exports and hook-and-restore flow (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13).

    Lateral movement. From the first host the attackers opened an RDP session to a second server with a domain administrator account, launched PowerShell, and within minutes the Hamachi-based Deed RAT files appeared there; from that host atexec and smbexec-style utilities consistent with Impacket spread it to a third machine (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13).

    Wave 2: TernDoor, blocked. About a month after the first wave, a renamed copy of the legitimate deskband_injector64.exe in a ProgramData subdirectory tried to sideload a malicious winmm.dll; the security product blocked execution before installation completed, and registry artefacts showed an attempt to create a kernel-driver service (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13). From recovered memory pages Bitdefender identifies a Mofu loader chain and assesses the partially recovered payload as TernDoor (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13).

    Wave 3. At the end of February the attackers deployed Deed RAT again through the same chain with a modified configuration, the components moved to C:\Recovery and new injection targets (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13).

    Exposure
    On-premises Exchange servers without Microsoft's 2022-11-08 ProxyNotShell fixes (MSRC, CVE-2022-41082); Bitdefender warns that attackers will keep exploiting unpatched servers until they are patched or taken offline (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13). HealthChecker's -VulnerabilityReport switch collects the vulnerability state of every Exchange server in an environment (Microsoft CSS-Exchange, 2025-10-29).
    Detection
    In process and file telemetry on Exchange, w3wp.exe writing .aspx files to publicly accessible directories under MSExchangePowerShellAppPool; in memory and EDR telemetry, changes to the first bytes of commonly hooked APIs such as StartServiceCtrlDispatcherW by unsigned code; in service-creation events, a kernel-driver service (Type 1) whose image path is under ProgramData, Temp or another user-writable directory; in authentication logs, RDP from internal hosts with domain administrator credentials outside maintenance windows, followed by PowerShell and executable downloads; and Impacket atexec or smbexec use (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13). The Wave 1 chain also leaves a LogMeIn Hamachi service and LMIGuardianSvc.exe on hosts with no sanctioned Hamachi install (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13).

    Triage: legitimate Exchange maintenance rarely writes web-accessible script files through the IIS worker process, and legitimate applications rarely patch system API entry points, which signed system components or documented security tools do when they must (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13).

    Defender takeaway
    After removing malware from an Exchange-originated intrusion, the decision that ends it is patching the entry point and rotating every domain administrator credential used during the intrusion, since Bitdefender's lesson from this intrusion is that actors re-exploit the same access path until the vulnerability is patched and compromised credentials are rotated; where Exchange cannot be patched at once, segment it so a compromise does not grant domain-wide access (Bitdefender, 2026-05-13).
