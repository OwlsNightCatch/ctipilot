## Findings — systemic and operational

### 1. The source rotation had stopped rotating, and it cost six publications

Phase 0 allocation rule 2 ranks standard-tier sources oldest-`last_successful_fetch` first. That field moves only when a source is **fetched AND used**. A source that is swept every fire and yields nothing publishable therefore keeps its stale date, stays pinned to the head of a stable ranking, and is re-selected on the next fire, indefinitely.

The window's S3 slices are the proof. Five consecutive fires drew an almost identical list:

| Cohort | `last_successful_fetch` | In how many of the six S3 slices |
|---|---|---|
| `unit42` | 2026-09-04 | 5 |
| `akamai-sirt`, `csa-labs`, `dfirreport`, `gambit-security`, `paradigm-shift-research`, `sygnia`, `trellix`, `vulncheck`, `zdi` | 2026-09-05 | 5 |
| `elastic-seclabs`, `esentire` | 2026-09-07 | 5 |
| `talos` | 2026-09-11 | **0** |
| `sentinellabs` | 2026-09-08 | **0** |
| `huntress` | 2026-09-07 | 1 (09-19, after both its missed posts) |
| `kaspersky-securelist` | 2026-09-14 | 1 (09-14, not used) |

111 research sources exist. Roughly 14 were being swept. Five of the six research publications G3 recovered came from publishers no sub-agent was given all week; the sixth, Elastic's 2026-09-14 post, came from a publisher that *was* in five slices, so that one is a sweep miss rather than an allocation miss.

**Shipped:** an anti-starvation clause in allocation rule 2 (exclude every source the previous two intel fires already attempted, essential tier exempt), and `tools/run_summary.py --recent-attempts` plus `runs.recent_attempts` in the digest so the exclusion set is computed rather than reconstructed by hand. On the current store that would remove 76 ids from the next fire's ranking. Audit run records are excluded from the set, so the audit's own re-sweeps never suppress a source for the next intel fire.

### 2. The `oracle-cpu` source record taught every fire the wrong cadence

The record's notes read "Quarterly cadence (Jan / Apr / Jul / Oct)" and "don't expect daily change". Oracle has run a second, higher-frequency Critical Security Patch Update line since May 2026, on the third Tuesday of February, March, May, June, August, September, November and December: eight further release dates a year, each a full risk matrix. The store published the June and August editions; the recipe that every fire reads still described a four-times-a-year source. Combined with `tier: standard`, a source that is only interesting four times a year is a source a fire has no reason to prioritise.

**Shipped:** the record's notes now carry the true dual cadence, the CSPU URL pattern, a working `extract` recipe and the instruction to check it in the week after every third Tuesday. Promotion to `tier: essential` is recommendation 1 rather than a unilateral change, because tier is not in the documented source-lifecycle transitions.

### 3. Verifier telemetry does not reconcile with its own findings

The previous audit recorded this as an incidental observation on one record and asked for one check. Measured store-wide with the shipped check's own rule and corpus: **118 of 748 iterations, across 60 of 181 run records**, have `truth + editorial + advisory` not equal to the length of their own `findings[]` list. (A looser first pass over every file under `runs/` returned 140 of 776 across 68; the figures here are the ones `check_verification_counters` itself produces, and this report's first draft carried the looser number until the verifier caught it.)
