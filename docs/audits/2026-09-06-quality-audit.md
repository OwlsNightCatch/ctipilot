## Verdict

**40 of 51 entries verified clean. Two carried a factual error, nine an imprecision.** That is the best soundness result this pipeline has recorded, and it is a real reversal rather than an easier week: the previous audit found 11 factual errors in 44 entries, and the dominant defect class it identified, quotation and attribution drift, produced **zero** confirmed instances this window. Every `evidence[]` quote the three batches checked was a verbatim substring of its cited page; batch C alone reports more than thirty. Both surviving factual errors are of a single, narrower kind: structured version data transcribed incompletely from an advisory that stated it correctly.

Completeness is the weaker half and the same shape as always. Two items cleared the gate and were in no entry at all, both for days: an unpatched local privilege escalation in CrowdStrike Falcon with public working exploit code, and a Dell gateway advisory whose top flaw replays one unauthenticated request into permanent administrative access with no workaround. Both are recovered here. The KEV channel, by contrast, is now clean: all ten in-window additions were already covered, which is v4.8's mechanical sweep working exactly as intended one window after it shipped.

**This audit's own work was verified, and it was not clean either.** The Phase 5.7 loop's first pass independently re-verified both factual-error corrections against WatchGuard PSIRT and MITRE's CNA record, and all five EPSS conversions against the FIRST.org and ENISA EUVD APIs, confirming each. It then found seven defects in this fire's own new work: an unsourced attribution in a recovered entry imported from the entity registry's summary rather than from a source fetched this run, an uncited paragraph around it, a CVE record whose score and authentication prerequisite were carried from an advisory's acknowledgements line without reading its vulnerability-table row, an overstated affected-component scope on the same CVE, two unsupported ATT&CK ids, and an earlier changelog section the WatchGuard correction had left inconsistent with the frontmatter it fixed. All seven are remediated. They are recorded here rather than only in the run record because an audit that reports other fires' defects and not its own is not measuring the same thing.

The loop then ran to eight iterations and **reached a confirmed CLEAN**, which no fire in the window managed. It took thirty-five remediations to get there, and the shape of what it caught is the point: entry-level substance was clean from iteration 3 onward, and everything after that was this audit's own reporting, its record-keeping, or its citations. Two findings were worth the whole loop. The fifth iteration caught this audit rewriting a 2026-08-19 changelog record while correcting an EPSS figure, which hard invariant #19 forbids and which the mechanical gate structurally could not see; that is now enforced by a new check. The sixth caught three correction sections citing a live FIRST.org API URL to support a historical value, so the link returned a figure ten times what the sentence asserted. Neither would have been caught by a shorter loop, and both were in work this audit produced while auditing others.

The machinery has one real problem, and it is not a defect in any single fire. **The verification loop stopped converging.** No intel fire in this window reached a confirmed CLEAN (this audit did, at the eighth iteration), mean iterations rose to 7.6 against roughly 4.5 two windows ago, and the loop consumed 60 to 79 % of every fire's wall clock, which is the whole of the five runaway-duration warnings this audit acknowledged. The findings it produces are overwhelmingly genuine, so the loop is working; what it is not doing is finishing.
