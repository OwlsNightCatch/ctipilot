# Weekly quality audit — 2026-08-09

**Mandate.** Institutionalised continuous improvement over the trailing week, weighted equally between **soundness** (is everything published true, precisely sourced, correctly classified and relevant?) and **completeness** (did the window miss anything a reader relying on ctipilot.ch alone needed?), plus the meta-question neither scheduled run asks: is the machinery drifting?

**Window.** 2026-08-02T13:09:58Z → 2026-08-09T13:15:57Z (168.1 h), anchored on the previous audit `runs/2026-08-02/2026-08-02T1309Z-audit.md`. **80 entries**, defined as every entry whose `discovered_at` falls inside the window — which includes the five the anchoring audit itself published moments after its own `started` timestamp, and which batch B1 duly truth-passed. They carry 9 distinct `run_id` values (7 intel fires, 1 weekly, and the anchoring audit); the window also contains a second weekly that stood down with zero entries, and this fire. Every entry-population figure in this report is computed over those 80. **Verifier-loop statistics are computed over a deliberately wider population — the 12 run records dated in August** — so the convergence trend is not truncated by the window boundary, and each such figure says so where it appears.

**Run record.** `runs/2026-08-09/2026-08-09T1315Z-audit.md` · **Prompt version** v3.31 (this run ships it).

**Method.** Four retrospective truth passes over the window's 80 entries in batches of 20, model-rotated (B1/B3 `cti-verification`/Opus, B2/B4 `cti-verification-alt`/Sonnet), each fetching primary sources rather than re-reading the entries' own citations — every `cves[]` id and CVSS re-read from the record that owns it at `cveawg.mitre.org` or the vendor/CERT advisory, every `evidence[]` quote checked as a literal contiguous substring of the fetched page, every `techniques[]` id checked mechanically against the pinned dataset. Three independent coverage re-sweeps (`cti-research`) re-researched the window as if for the first time — G1 vulnerabilities and exploitation, G2 incidents and home region, G3 vendor research-blog listing sweeps — and their returns were diffed against the store. Roughly 250 primary URLs fetched across the seven sub-agents; ~135 CVE records read from their owning authority. Systemic review ran over the window's run records, `work/` artifacts and state.

## Verdict

**65 of 80 entries verified clean against primary sources. One factual error, fourteen imprecisions, and the error was consequential enough that a reader acting on it would have done the wrong thing.** Both halves of the mandate returned real work: the truth passes found an entry that told a forensic and clinical audience there was no patch to install when five patched versions with download links were sitting in the advisory it cited, and the coverage sweeps found two genuine misses of severe, actionable material — a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated takeover of a privileged-access-management appliance that a national CERT thought worth relaying to its own constituency, and a twelve-year-old cryptographic-randomness flaw under active exploitation.

The soundness picture is otherwise good and improving where the previous audit intervened: per-fact attribution defects continue to fall, no hallucinated CVE or fabricated quote survived anywhere in 80 entries, every `techniques[]` id in the store resolves as active in the pin, and every entry carries a classification. Nine of the fourteen imprecisions are one recurring shape — a `sourcing_note` or body sentence that describes its own source slightly more confidently or more tidily than the source supports — and four are traceable to sources that were edited after publication.

The completeness picture is where this audit found its systemic defect, and it is not in the entries. The pipeline is good at covering what its research surfaces: thirteen of twenty-one research-blog items were confirmed already published, all six in-window KEV additions were already published, and the previous audit's open Gladinet gap had in fact been closed on 2026-08-03. What it is bad at is holding on to work it has already done. On 2026-08-03 a weekly fire stood down as a duplicate week *after* researching and verifying the whole ISO week, listed nine verified in-scope items its sibling had not carried, and **not one of those nine was ever published** — because nothing consumed the list and both recency gates put them out of reach forever. That is a silent, structural blind spot of exactly the kind the sound-and-complete doctrine treats as equal in gravity to a false positive, and it is fixed in this commit.

### Factual error (1)

|---|---|---|---|
| `2026-08-05/thermo-fisher-genetic-analyzer-dna-file-integrity` | The entry's central claim — repeated in its title, headline, summary, `cves[].status: [no-patch]`, `cves[].fixed`, the `no-patch` tag, the body twice and its action item — is that Thermo Fisher offers no fix for CVE-2026-17583 and that "there is no patch to wait for", so only an architectural control can help | CISA ICSMA-26-216-01 carries fifteen remediation records for CVE-2026-17583: eight per-product `vendor_fix` records and seven `mitigation` records covering the interim measures. **Root cause — a transport that silently dropped a section, not a reading error.** The truth pass established that the HTML rendering of the CISA advisory omitted the Mitigations block on two of the three transports it tried, while the machine-readable CSAF JSON at `raw.githubusercontent.com/cisagov/CSAF/…` carried all fifteen remediation records, the eight per-product fixes among them. The composing run read a page that genuinely did not show the fixes. This is the second time in three audits that a CISA-hosted document has misled a run through its transport rather than its content. The generalisable rule — **for any CISA ICS/ICSMA advisory, the CSAF JSON is the ground truth and the HTML render is a lossy view of it** — is recorded in the run record and is already the recommended path in the fetch-tooling reference; what was missing was the reason to prefer it, which is now on file.

**Why this one matters more than its severity score suggests.** The entry's audience is laboratories whose genetic-analyzer output feeds forensic and clinical reporting. Telling them the flaw is unfixable converts a patching task into an architecture project, and the entry went further, framing the "wait for the patch" instinct as reaching the wrong answer here. That is the rare defect that makes a reader worse off than not reading the entry at all.

### The claim that was wrong when written (1, found by a carried-forward watch item)

`2026-07-19/weekly-w29-exploited-internet-facing-enterprise-software` stated that four classes of internet-facing enterprise software had crossed into confirmed exploitation, "every one KEV-listed". Checked against the catalogue on 2026-08-09 (catalogVersion 2026.08.07, 1662 entries): eight of the ten identifiers involved are present and were all added before 2026-07-19, so the bulk of the claim held. **CVE-2026-2699 and CVE-2026-2701, the Progress ShareFile Storage Zone Controller pair, are absent and have never been added.** Because entries are never removed from the catalogue once added, the claim was already false when it was written.

The exploitation itself was real and independently sourced — Shadowserver honeypots recorded in-the-wild attempts on 2026-07-10 — and none of the pipeline's own per-CVE entries on either identifier ever made a KEV claim. **The error entered only in the weekly synthesis**, where ten identifiers from four separate stories were compressed into one clause. Root cause: an aggregate claim over a set is only as true as its weakest member, and nothing in the composition discipline forces a per-member check before a universal quantifier ships. Fixed by publication of `2026-08-09/sharefile-cve-2026-2699-2701-never-kev-listed` as an `update_of`, which corrects both CVE records' status notes in the store.

### Imprecisions (14) — documented, no repair warranted

Nine of the fourteen are the same shape: a `sourcing_note` or body sentence claiming slightly more, or slightly tidier, than its source states.

- **`2026-08-02/sp-page-builder-…`** — the `sourcing_note` says four discloser self-scores "are named in the body"; only one is, and the four-number sequence follows a sentence enumerating CVEs in a different order, so a positional read yields three wrong mappings. *This is the previous audit's own recovered entry, and it is the previous audit's own headline lesson — a positional mapping is a guess — reappearing in the `sourcing_note` of the entry where that lesson was learned.* Everything substantive in it verified correct, including its subtle and correct claim that CVE-2026-65879 carries no CNA metrics.
- **`2026-08-05/liechtenstein-vwbp-…`** and **`2026-08-06/…twelve-state figure`** — `credibility: 1` where the corroborating outlets republish the government's own figure rather than assessing it. One assessor, several publishers. *Now caught mechanically — see Fixes.*
- **`2026-08-05/aisi-openai-cyber-range-…`** — generalises AISI's detection mechanism to "unusual-data-transfer monitoring" where AISI names Tor-network egress detection specifically.
- **`2026-08-05/talos-adversary-ai-…`** — "on the order of 18 million target hosts" reads a resume-position line offset (`line 18,222,511`) as a host count; Talos gives 9,180 unique hosts.
- **`2026-08-06/unit42-nova-…`** — collapses a sum into a ratio: Unit 42's 5,421 is the total of 1,280 dependency flaws plus 4,141 downstream exposures, and the 4,141 figure — which quantifies the entry's own analytical point — never appears.
- **`2026-08-06/cve-2026-63077-teamcity-…`** — substitutes "HTTP(S) access" for JetBrains' named "agent polling protocol" in three places, widening the apparent attack surface, and says CISA names no intrusion path when the KEV description names the protocol.
- **`2026-08-07/fake-zoom-…`** — the body hardens a first-ness claim past Jamf's own hedged wording, though the title and summary hedge correctly.
- **`2026-08-08/cve-2026-8037-kemp-…`** — `cves[].cvss: "9.8"` is NVD's analyst score (`AV:N`); the Progress Software CNA record gives 9.6 with `AV:A` (adjacent) — a materially different exposure assessment, undisclosed.
- **`2026-08-08/flowise-…`** — a `sourcing_note` says the CNA record "contradicts" a 9.9 with 8.5; VulnCheck's record publishes both as parallel CVSS 4.0 and 3.1 metrics. Nothing was contradicted.
- **`2026-08-02/gladinet-…`**, **`2026-08-05/adobe-apsb26-120`** — a true claim carrying no inline citation, and one citation-attribution slip.

Four are **source mutation after publication**, not composition defects, and are recorded so the pattern is visible rather than blamed: the N-able blog was rewritten in place on 2026-08-06 so a correctly-quoted `evidence[]` string no longer appears at its URL; Cisco's FMC advisory revision dates moved after the entry cited them; Traefik's "no CVE identifiers have been assigned" was true at compose time and CVEs were published the next day; and one Reuters citation is now behind a CAPTCHA on every transport.

None of these clears the bar for the in-place immutability-exception repair class (wrong CVE id, wrong CVSS as a transcription error, dead ATT&CK id, wrong registry key). The Kemp score is the closest call and was deliberately left alone: both 9.6 and 9.8 are genuinely published figures by different scoring bodies, so this is a source-selection question rather than a transcription error, and rewriting a defensible choice in place would stretch a carve-out that exists for facts that are simply wrong. The generalisable lessons from the two CVSS cases — that NVD's "Primary" score is a third opinion alongside the CNA and any CISA-ADP block, and that a CNA publishing CVSS 4.0 and 3.1 for one CVE is publishing parallel metrics rather than correcting itself — are recorded in `.claude/memory/csaf-msrc-transcription.md`.

## Fixes shipped in this commit

- **Prompts v3.31** (`cti-run.md`, `weekly-summary.md`, `quality-audit.md` banners in lockstep; CHANGELOG entry with Why / What changed / What stays):
  - **`state/coverage_backlog.md` (new) + a Phase 0 read duty + a stand-down write duty.** Verified-but-unpublished items now have a queue. Backlog rows are exempt from the recency gate — each carries its own `event_date` and was verified in-window by the fire that surfaced it — and subject to every other gate unchanged. Rows are struck on publication with the entry id, or on a reasoned decision not to publish; never silently dropped.
  - **The weekly's duplicate-week guard now sweeps unpromoted `claude/**` feature branches** at both guard points, so a primary that has completed but not yet auto-merged is visible.
  - **A blocked alternate verifier now walks a four-rung ladder** before the rotation is surrendered: spawn the alternate → retry once → **spawn the other definition with an explicit `model:` override to the missing model** → only then a same-model pass as a recorded exception, with the override attempt itself recorded.
- **`tools/check_run.py`** — four additions:
  - Rotation integrity is checked across the **whole** iteration chain on every publish path rather than only at the confirming pair, FAILing consecutive same-definition iterations that no recorded waiver explains (new `verification-rotation` label). Correctly reports 2026-08-06's four pairs and 2026-07-23's seven as explained by their waivers.
  - The confirmation waiver is honoured at either key placement, with a nudge on fresh runs toward the canonical one.
  - `credibility: 1` on an entry whose `verification` is `single-source*` is now a mechanical FAIL — a frontmatter self-contradiction the gate can settle without spending a verifier iteration. Zero current violations, so this locks in a property the verifier is already achieving at cost.
  - The informational composition report gains the window's `high` share, actions per operational entry, and the share of entries with no action, each against a trailing-28-day baseline.
