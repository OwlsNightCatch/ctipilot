    HPESBNW05162, HPE Networking (Aruba) AOS-8 and AOS-10 wireless: 72 CVEs fixed in one bulletin, 19 of them unauthenticated and network-reachable at CVSS 9.0 or above, across controllers, gateways and access points

    HPE's Aruba wireless bulletin fixes 19 unauthenticated network flaws at CVSS 9.0+; HPE knows of no public exploit code

    first published 2026-10-23 03:36 UTC

    Defender actions

    • Upgrade every HPE Networking (Aruba) Mobility Conductor, Controller, Gateway and access point on AOS-10 or AOS-8 to the fixed build of its train (10.8.1.2, 10.7.2.7, 10.4.1.14, 8.13.3.1 or 8.10.0.24), and until then confine the CLI and web management interfaces to a dedicated layer 2 segment or VLAN behind firewall policy, as HPE advises.

    Analysis

    HPE Networking's bulletin HPESBNW05162 (initial release 2026-10-22) fixes 72 CVEs in the wireless operating systems AOS-8 and AOS-10 for Mobility Conductors, Controllers, Gateways managed by HPE Networking Central, AOS-10 access points (AOS-AP) and AOS-8 Instant access points (AOS-IAP) (HPE Networking, 2026-10-22). By the CSAF's CVSS 3.1 scores, 33 of the 72 rate 9.0 or higher (eight at 10.0, ten at 9.8, ten at 9.6, one at 9.4 and four at 9.3); the 19 rated 10.0, 9.8 or 9.4 need network access and no privileges, and the other 14 are reachable from the adjacent network (HPE Networking CSAF, 2026-10-22). The named mechanisms include authentication bypasses and command injection in the AOS-10 interface and in the PAPI protocol, a buffer overflow and a command injection in the AOS-8 PAPI protocol that arrive as crafted packets on its UDP port, an authentication bypass in the AOS-8 SSH daemon, and SQL injection in the web-based management interface (HPE Networking CSAF, 2026-10-22). HPE says the flaws were generally found by its internal security research, that it is not aware of public discussion or exploit code as of the release, and that customers are strongly urged to patch; for some of the critical rows exploitation also needs preconditions outside the attacker's control, which the bulletin does not itemise (HPE Networking, 2026-10-22)(HPE Networking CSAF, 2026-10-22).

    The bulletin and its CSAF name AOS-10 10.8.x up to 10.8.1.1, 10.7.x up to 10.7.2.6 and 10.4.x up to 10.4.1.13, and AOS-8 8.13.x up to 8.13.3.0 and 8.10.x up to 8.10.0.23; fixed builds are AOS-10.8.1.2, 10.7.2.7 and 10.4.1.14 and AOS-8.13.3.1 and 8.10.0.24 (HPE Networking, 2026-10-22)(HPE Networking CSAF, 2026-10-22). HPE's workaround is to restrict the CLI and web-based management interfaces to a dedicated layer 2 segment or VLAN, controlled by firewall policies at layer 3 and above with accounting controls (HPE Networking, 2026-10-22).

    Exposure
    Any HPE Networking mobility conductor, controller, gateway, access point or virtual machine that runs a listed version; HPE says every compatible hardware model is affected and that its own hardware list is illustrative only, so the check is the software version, not the model (HPE Networking, 2026-10-22). HPE presumes versions past end of maintenance affected and says installations past end of support should be considered potentially impacted (HPE Networking, 2026-10-22). The named surfaces are the CLI and web management interfaces and the PAPI access-point management protocol on UDP port 8211 (HPE Networking, 2026-10-22)(HPE Networking CSAF, 2026-10-22).
    Detection
    Management-plane access and accounting logs for logins and sessions from outside the management segment, and network flow data for PAPI traffic on UDP 8211 or controller management ports from segments that carry users or guests. HPE publishes no detection guidance, so no post-compromise check is defined (HPE Networking, 2026-10-22).
    Defender takeaway
    Upgrade controllers, gateways and access points to the fixed build of their train, management and PAPI-exposed units first, and apply the management-segment restriction at once where the upgrade has to wait; HPE says it is not aware of public discussion or exploit code, so the order follows exposure of the management and access-point protocol paths rather than a known campaign (HPE Networking, 2026-10-22).
