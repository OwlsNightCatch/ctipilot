    Android 'SMS of Death': one hidden SMS drops calls, emergency calls included; Android 12 and 13 get no fix

    first published 2026-11-02 03:46 UTCupdated 2026-11-08 13:58 UTC

    Defender actions

    • Move every managed Android phone below the 2026-11-01 security patch level to that level or later, phones of emergency-service and on-call staff first.

    Analysis

    A researcher who signs as Mark B published a working proof-of-concept app on 2026-10-29 for a zero-click remote denial of service in Android's telephony framework (smsofdeath.com, 2026-10-29). One malformed WAP Push data message to port 2948 makes WapPushOverSms.decodeWapPdu() cast a 64-bit header length to a signed 32-bit integer and allocate a negative-sized array; the uncaught NegativeArraySizeException kills the com.android.phone process, which drops active calls, emergency calls included, and cuts service for one to two seconds per message (smsofdeath.com, 2026-10-29). The poison message stays in the pending queue and is retried on every boot and on carrier redelivery: the researcher reports that about 20 messages leave a device unusable and that one Android 12 phone rebooted into recovery roughly every 90 seconds until the messages were deleted over ADB (smsofdeath.com, 2026-10-29). The crash reproduced on Android 12 to 16, on a Pixel 9 and on Samsung Galaxy and A-series phones (smsofdeath.com, 2026-10-29); Security Affairs restates the mechanism and the October fix for Android 14 to 17 (Security Affairs, 2026-11-01).

    Google's fix, titled "Fix remote DoS in decodeWapPdu", catches the exceptions the malformed messages trigger and deletes the malicious messages from the SMS database (BleepingComputer, 2026-10-31); per the researcher it shipped in the October 2026 update for Android 14 to 17, so Android 12 and 13 phones outside the update window run the same code unfixed (smsofdeath.com, 2026-10-29). Google's November 2026 bulletin lists CVE-2026-58882, the identifier BleepingComputer gives, as a critical denial of service in the System component fixed at the 2026-11-01 security patch level for Android 14, 15, 16, 16-qpr2 and 17 (Google, 2026-11-02; BleepingComputer, 2026-10-31). BleepingComputer adds that the fix is not listed in Google's October bulletin (BleepingComputer, 2026-10-31). The proof-of-concept app and its source are public (smsofdeath.com, 2026-10-29; BleepingComputer, 2026-10-31), and none of the sources reports use in the wild.

    Exposure
    Any Android phone below the 2026-11-01 security patch level, which Google names as the fix level of CVE-2026-58882, BleepingComputer's identifier for the flaw (Google, 2026-11-02), and Android 12 and 13 phones outside the security-update window, which receive no fix; the attack needs only the target's phone number (smsofdeath.com, 2026-10-29). The patch level in device-management inventory shows which handsets have the fix.
    Detection
    On managed handsets, repeated crashes of the com.android.phone process in the device log, with a NegativeArraySizeException raised in WapPushOverSms.decodeWapPdu, calls that end with disconnect cause CS_DEATH, and a phone that restarts into recovery about every 90 seconds (smsofdeath.com, 2026-10-29); the message never appears in the inbox and the sender is not shown, so the crash log is the only trace on the phone.

    Triage: a single telephony restart has many benign causes; a restart that recurs on every boot or on each carrier redelivery, with the exception above in the crash log, matches this flaw.

    Defender takeaway
    Move every managed Android handset to the 2026-11-01 security patch level or later, emergency-service and on-call staff first, and list for replacement the Android 12 and 13 phones outside the security-update window, which receive no fix and stay crashable by one message. A phone stuck in the loop should not be wiped from the recovery prompt: the researcher documents deleting the pending WAP Push rows over ADB from a computer, which avoids a factory reset (smsofdeath.com, 2026-10-29).
