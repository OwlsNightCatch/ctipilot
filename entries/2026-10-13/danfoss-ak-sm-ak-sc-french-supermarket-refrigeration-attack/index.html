    Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC, Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb refrigeration controllers compromised, with forced defrost at hundreds of French supermarkets, refrigeration failures at more than 115 shops in at least six more countries and first cases in Germany, and on 2026-11-02 altered settings in the cold rooms of the French blood service through a refrigeration supplier, and on 2026-11-05 the first publicly known cases in Switzerland; responsibility not established; ENISA and Europol call for immediate action

    Refrigeration attacks reach Switzerland, a Köniz butcher and three Volg stores; no actor established

    first published 2026-10-13 03:41 UTCupdated 2026-11-07 03:53 UTC

    Defender actions

    • Find every Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC, Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb refrigeration controller, and any other refrigeration controller with a remote management interface because not every second-wave site's vendor is confirmed, in facilities you own or that a contractor manages for you and take its management interface off the internet now, restoring access only through a secure VPN or protected network (filtering to the contractor's source addresses is an emergency stop-gap, and CERT-FR asks you to check your insurance conditions before cutting the contractor's data uplink); an end-of-life unit that gets no fixes needs replacing, because a factory reset leaves it vulnerable and probably compromised again.
    • On every such controller that was reachable from the internet, check the whole configuration, defrost ranges, alarm thresholds and notification recipients included, against the intended one, verify the actual temperatures and test that alarms reach staff; for a Danfoss unit that may have been taken over, restart it or otherwise factory-reset it, restore access only through a secure VPN or protected network and reconfigure it for service, then update its firmware, set the security level to Strict and replace the default and any weak passwords, and on every Copeland or Dixell XWEB unit confirm the factory Admin password was changed and give each contractor a named account with an expiry date.

    Analysis

    Coopérative U, Intermarché and E. Leclerc confirmed to AFP a cyberattack on their refrigeration systems overnight on 11 to 12 October 2026, at hundreds of stores (AFP, 2026-10-12). CERT-FR knows of numerous compromises of Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC controllers and of Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb, and says exposing a management interface to the internet is contrary to good practice (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16); NCSC Switzerland rates the activity actively exploited (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13). ANSSI's director general told the French Senate on 2026-10-21 that elementary flaws were exploited, above all equipment directly on the internet without particular protection, plus weak passwords (Public Sénat, 2026-10-21). No actor is named on the record, and SentinelLABS says responsibility has not been publicly established (SentinelLABS, 2026-11-03).

    On 2026-11-02 retailers in Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Sweden, Ireland, Estonia and, per The Guardian, Finland reported refrigeration failures, and BleepingComputer counts more than 115 affected stores (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-02; The Guardian, 2026-11-02). The first German cases followed, and the BSI knows of cases at individual supermarkets (heise, 2026-11-03; heise, 2026-11-04). SRF reported on 2026-11-05 the first publicly known Swiss cases, at a butcher in Köniz and at three Volg stores (SRF, 2026-11-05).

    The attacks reached healthcare cold storage. The French blood service EFS confirmed that on 2026-11-02 a refrigeration-equipment supplier suffered a cybersecurity incident that affected the supervision and control installations of cold rooms at three EFS sites and raised their temperature and defrost thresholds to very high values (translated from French) (EFS, 2026-11-03). EFS says it was not directly targeted, its own alarms caught the anomalies quickly and no loss of blood products is to be reported (EFS, 2026-11-03). BleepingComputer, heise and SRF name the supplier as Coolworld, SRF adding that the attackers reportedly entered through its remote maintenance; Coolworld says the incident involved remote monitoring software and that the vulnerability reported in the media is in a component from a third-party supplier outside its control (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-04; heise, 2026-11-04; SRF, 2026-11-04; Coolworld, 2026-11-04). None of these sources names Danfoss, Copeland or Dixell equipment for the blood-service case.

    ENISA, the European Commission, Europol's EC3, the EU CSIRTs Network and EU-CyCLONe say that since 11 October 2026 several commercial refrigeration systems and similar devices have been hit in a number of EU Member States and, after a new wave on 1 to 2 November, advise organisations to take immediate action to mitigate exposure (ENISA, 2026-11-04). CSIRT Italia tells operators of cold-chain technology in any sector to apply its measures urgently, removing direct internet exposure of control interfaces and verifying alarms (CSIRT Italia, 2026-11-04).

    Exposure
    A Danfoss AK-SM or AK-SC refrigeration controller, or a Copeland or Dixell xweb unit, whose management interface is reachable from the internet, above all legacy units: Danfoss told AFP that most affected units no longer receive updates (AFP, 2026-10-12). A second class is cold rooms supervised through a contractor's solution: EFS was not itself targeted, yet its cold-room settings were altered through its supplier (EFS, 2026-11-03).
    Detection
    On the controller, defrost, setpoint and account changes outside a maintenance window, and defrost duration, which Le Monde reports the attackers lengthened from one hour to eight so that alarms trigger late (translated from French) (Le Monde, 2026-10-12); in firewall, provider VPN or ISP logs, inbound connections to the management interface from addresses other than the contractor's. EFS's own monitoring and alarm systems caught the altered thresholds (EFS, 2026-11-03), and CSIRT Italia says to check actual temperatures and alarm function with instruments independent of the supervision platform (CSIRT Italia, 2026-11-04).

    Triage: a defrost cycle is normal; BFMTV reports that installations can be programmed for two hours of defrost per 24 hours, whereas the reported attack is a continuous forced defrost at a 50-degree setpoint (translated from French) (BFMTV, 2026-10-12).

    Defender takeaway
    Take any Danfoss AK-SM or AK-SC, Copeland xweb or Dixell xweb management interface off the internet now and restore access only through a secure VPN or protected network, as CERT-FR advises (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13), and ask the contractor whether its own remote access or monitoring platform was affected. For the AK-SM800 and AK-SC355, replacement is the lever: no fix exists for them according to NCSC Switzerland (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13), and CERT-FR says a factory reset of an unpatched end-of-life unit leaves it vulnerable and almost certainly compromised again (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13).

    Updates11

    Update14 Oct 2026 03:44 UTC

    CERT-FR revised its alert on 2026-10-13: the list of compromised remote controllers now includes Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb besides Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC, campaigns against these devices are probably still ongoing, and an unpatched end-of-life unit kept on the internet is very likely to be compromised, so a factory reset alone is insufficient (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13). NCSC Switzerland published advisory 13054 on 2026-10-13 with the exploitation status Actively Exploited. It lists the Danfoss AK-SM 800 (end of life, no longer patched), the AK-SC 355 (phased out, no longer patched) and the current AK-SM 800A series when it runs outdated firmware, says no fixes exist for the two older models and that Danfoss urges replacing them, and says the attack could affect other sectors, such as healthcare for specialized cooling equipment, so organisations should check whether affected devices are in use (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13). Danfoss told Cooling Post that the AK-SM800 was declared end of life in 2021, that the AK-SC355 was phased out in 2016, and that the affected AK-SM800A systems ran outdated firmware (Cooling Post, 2026-10-12). Blick reports that Migros uses the same Danfoss refrigeration systems in a limited number of Swiss stores and sees no sign they were targeted, and that Aldi, Lidl and Coop say they are not affected (Blick, 2026-10-13); as of 2026-10-14 no source reported a Swiss victim. The advisory relays a six-step CERT-FR sequence (limit access to trusted addresses, factory reset, apply all fixes, rotate passwords and secrets, review accounts, reconfigure) and lists filtering to trusted addresses as a temporary measure (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13), whereas the revised CERT-FR page puts removal of internet exposure first, accepts source-address filtering only as an emergency stop-gap and lists no secret rotation or account review (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13).

    Improvement15 Oct 2026 03:49 UTC

    Censys, which scans for Danfoss StoreView control panels, says Danfoss has not confirmed the initial access vector and points to three AK-SM 800A flaws that Danfoss patched more than a year earlier: CVE-2025-41450 (March 2025), which Censys describes as letting an unauthenticated actor log in as an administrator with a code computed from the current calendar date, and CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452 (August 2025), which it says let authenticated users run commands and disrupt the management interface (Censys, 2026-10-14). Censys maps the firmware by build: below 4.2 carries all three, 4.2.4 fixes only the date-based login and still carries the two later ones, and 4.3.1 or later fixes all three (Censys, 2026-10-14). Its scan counted 1,825 exposed StoreView panels in France on 2026-10-11 and 537 on 2026-10-14, a fall of 71 percent, with builds below 4.2, build 4.2.4 and builds from 4.3.1 leaving at about the same rate, which it reads as operators taking controllers off the internet rather than patching them (Censys, 2026-10-14). Outside France the count fell from 1,166 to 966, and in ten of the twelve countries with the most exposed hosts more than 60 percent of the hosts run a build below 4.3.1, led by Poland at 81 percent and Romania at 80 percent (Censys, 2026-10-14).

    Update18 Oct 2026 03:33 UTC

    CERT-FR's alert now has a third version, dated 2026-10-16 and listed on the page as an update of the recommendations; the page does not show which sentences changed (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). Its current text separates the two cases. For every unit it asks to remove the internet exposure of the affected units and of any unit on a public IP address, and to check the insurance contract conditions before cutting the data uplink to the refrigeration contractor (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). For a unit not known to be compromised it then asks to check the whole configuration, notably the defrost ranges; for a compromised Danfoss unit it says to restart the equipment or otherwise restore it with a factory reset, and then to reconfigure it for service (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). In both cases access returns only through a secure VPN or protected network, with source-address filtering for the contractors an emergency measure and not a lasting one, and the maker's practices apply (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). The alert still says campaigns are probably ongoing, still names no flaw or entry vector, and gives the risk as not specified by the vendor (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16).

    The alert's documentation section links an operator guide from Dixell France (Copeland) (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). The file at that address was replaced on 2026-10-18; its current text, described in the correction of 2026-10-19T04:06:00Z, is Copeland's bulletin of 14 October 2026 followed by Dixell France's own complement (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). That complement says XWEB PRO and EVO ship with the same factory account, Admin with the password Admin, advises keeping the original Admin password sealed or in a password manager and not using it daily, gives each user a named account with an expiry date for an external contractor's account, and requires remote access through an encrypted VPN, with no port forward or DMZ and no remote-control software such as AnyDesk or TeamViewer on a site PC that stays on (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). CERT-FR does not say how the attackers got in, and SecurityWeek writes that neither Danfoss nor French authorities have tied the attack to the flaws Claroty disclosed (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16; SecurityWeek, 2026-10-15).

    Correction19 Oct 2026 04:06 UTC

    The Dixell France guide that CERT-FR links was replaced on 2026-10-18, the file's date on the vendor's server, and now opens with Copeland's own security bulletin dated 14 October 2026 (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). The earlier update section's statements about XWEB PRO firmware 1.12.1, a CISA advisory and a March 2024 bulletin are no longer on that page and are removed from the summary and the body. The bulletin acknowledges the vulnerability on a generation of XWEB EVO controllers that is no longer marketed and has reached end of life, says to remove every XWEB EVO from the public internet immediately and then look for suspicious activity from the local network, and says XWEB EVO no longer receives security patches, while XWEB PRO is the supported platform and must not be exposed to the internet either (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). For an XWEB EVO that may have been accessed it gives a procedure: isolate the unit, restore the latest backup made before the access or otherwise check the users and profiles, set new passwords, check the alarm categories and recipients with a test alarm, and restore the device parameters from backup files or, without one, read each device's parameters and check setpoints, alarm thresholds and defrost parameters; it also says to check the product temperatures in the cabinets directly, since altered setpoints or alarm thresholds can compromise food safety (Dixell France, 2026-10-14).

    Le Monde reports that besides raising the defrost temperature to 50 degrees the attackers lengthened the defrost operation from one hour to eight so that alarms trigger late, which delayed interventions and amplified the damage (translated from French) (Le Monde, 2026-10-12). SecurityWeek relays that detail and adds that the Paris prosecutor's office said the attack caused fires as well as food losses, that the Finance Minister put the number of affected supermarkets at about 700 of roughly 10,000, and that investigators are reportedly puzzled by the lack of a claim of responsibility or a ransom demand, citing Reuters (SecurityWeek, 2026-10-15). Cybersecurity Dive had already reported that a Copeland spokesperson confirmed the vulnerability was found in discontinued end-of-life controllers (Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-10-15).

    Update25 Oct 2026 03:37 UTC

    ANSSI's director general, Vincent Strubel, told the French Senate foreign-affairs committee on 2026-10-21 that the attack rested on elementary security flaws, starting with refrigeration equipment directly on the internet and administered without particular protection, and weak passwords, and he dismissed analyses that tied it to vulnerabilities researchers had published over the previous two years (Public Sénat, 2026-10-21). Le Monde Informatique reports the same hearing and adds that the systems were reachable without a firewall or network segmentation and that the management interface was protected only by weak passwords (Le Monde Informatique, 2026-10-22). Neither outlet reports evidence beyond the testimony, and ANSSI names no actor; the account sits against Copeland's acknowledgment of a vulnerability in its end-of-life XWEB EVO controllers and the earlier report that ANSSI had identified a vulnerability in a Danish company's software, and no source reconciles them; the two outlets also differ on the date and the count, Public Sénat giving the night of 12 to 13 October and more than 700 supermarkets, Le Monde Informatique the night of 11 to 12 October and at least 300 Intermarché and Netto shops. Le Monde Informatique adds that ANSSI can alert and advise but has no means to impose connection rules on retailers (Le Monde Informatique, 2026-10-22), and Public Sénat reports that ANSSI ran a rapid check with health-sector players because laboratories and pharmacies that store vaccines use the same kind of refrigeration equipment (Public Sénat, 2026-10-21).

    Forescout's Vedere Labs says on 2026-10-22 that the access vector, exploited vulnerabilities, attacker infrastructure, persistence and responsible actors in the French incidents remain unconfirmed, and that the reported changes, made in rapid succession, used ordinary supervisory functions (Forescout, 2026-10-22). Its tracking of hacktivist groups counts over 20 claimed attacks on refrigeration systems since January 2026, the most active being the pro-Russian groups Z-Pentest Alliance and NoName057(16) through internet-exposed human-machine interfaces, and its count of exposed services is 12,976 observations on 6,017 public addresses in 99 countries, with 3,762 exposed Danfoss StoreView observations on AK-SM 800A units, 80 percent of the 4,697 StoreView observations, on firmware older than 4.3.1 (Forescout, 2026-10-22). Forescout adds that the manufacturers' documentation presents internet remote maintenance through port forwarding as a supported option, so the exposure is not only an accident of configuration (Forescout, 2026-10-22).

    Update02 Nov 2026 03:47 UTC

    Reuters reported on 2026-10-29, citing four officials, that the Pentagon is treating the late-August freezer failures at military bases across the United States as a suspected hack (Reuters, 2026-10-29). On Reuters' own review of the bases' social-media posts at least six commissaries were hit, and in at least one case the cooling units' temperatures were raised overnight instead of the units stopping; Fort Huachuca said its freezer "went into defrost mode where it heated up all the food" (Reuters, 2026-10-29). A Cyber Command unit and the Army Criminal Investigation Division's Cyber Field Office are involved, and one official said the investigators are in touch with their French counterparts about the French attack; Reuters could not establish whether Danfoss or any other controller brand was in use at the bases, nor how far the investigation had got or who might be responsible (Reuters, 2026-10-29).

    Nextgov reported on 2026-10-30, citing two people familiar with the matter, that US officials suspect Iran-linked hackers, that the intrusions began around late August, that how the intruders got in is not clear and that the assessment may change (Nextgov, 2026-10-30). No official has attributed the activity on the record, and the sources give no technical evidence for the suspicion. The only stated link between the two cases is the investigators' contact, and Reuters notes that the French attack, like the Fort Huachuca failure, raised temperatures instead of switching units off (Reuters, 2026-10-29).

    Update03 Nov 2026 03:45 UTC

    On 2026-11-02 refrigeration failures struck stores in Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Sweden, Ireland and Estonia, and Finland according to The Guardian; BleepingComputer counts more than 115 affected stores from company statements and says it is unclear whether they were hit in a coordinated attack, and several companies confirmed they had been hacked (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-02; The Guardian, 2026-11-02). Sabcobel's chief executive says the units' evaporators were switched to defrost mode (The Guardian, 2026-11-02) and, per BleepingComputer, that the attacker used the same vulnerability as in France (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-02); Euronews names Danfoss as the software supplier in both incidents and quotes it saying customers who had not updated their software were affected (Euronews, 2026-11-02), and in Estonia attackers reached the control computer of an older Danfoss system with outdated software (translated from German) (Tagesschau, 2026-11-02). CyberInsider nonetheless states that the manufacturer, controller models and initial access method of the new wave remain unknown (CyberInsider, 2026-11-02), so the vendor of the Belgian and Swedish sites is not independently confirmed. Coop Switzerland told 20 Minuten that corresponding attempts were registered without success, and Migros reports no indication of an attack (20 Minuten, 2026-11-02). The head of Sweden's national cybersecurity centre says it is working on incidents around the country and that Sweden has quite a lot of exposed operational technology devices (The Guardian, 2026-11-02).

    Update04 Nov 2026 03:30 UTC

    The first German cases followed on 2026-11-02: heise reports that the cooling systems of an Edeka market in Reisbach, Lower Bavaria, were heated to 50 degrees overnight into Monday and that the police have not confirmed a cyberattack as the cause, and WDR quotes a division manager at a CAP supermarket in North Rhine-Westphalia saying the actual attack hit the firm that maintains the cooling plants remotely, which is the operator's statement and not an authority finding, and Rewe's head office confirmed to WDR a cyberattack aimed specifically at the cooling-system supplier Danfoss, whose technology only a few regional Rewe stores use (translated from German) (heise, 2026-11-03; WDR, 2026-11-03). SentinelLABS' assessment of 2026-11-03 says responsibility for the European incidents has not been publicly established, calls Russian-aligned disruptive groups one credible hypothesis based on precedent, and says shared maintenance access deserves scrutiny (SentinelLABS, 2026-11-03). Le Canard enchaîné reports that Coolworld, a company that maintains refrigerated storage containers for the French blood service, was hacked overnight on 1 to 2 November and that cold rooms held at minus 30 degrees were programmed to reach 110 degrees; the blood service says an on-duty technician saw the temperature rise and acknowledges that frozen plasma intended for transfusion was among the products, and the Health Ministry says there was zero operational consequence because the situation was spotted (translated from French) (Le Canard enchaîné, 2026-11-03). The 110 degrees is the programmed set-point and not a measured temperature, and no source names the equipment, the vendor or the access method, or ties this case to the Danfoss and Copeland controllers.

    Update05 Nov 2026 04:02 UTC

    The French blood service EFS confirmed on 2026-11-03 that on 2 November a refrigeration-equipment supplier suffered a cybersecurity incident that affected the supervision and control installations of cold rooms at three EFS sites, with temperature and defrost thresholds raised to very high values (translated from French) (EFS, 2026-11-03). EFS says its own monitoring and alarm systems identified the anomalies quickly, the affected equipment was brought under control without impact on the products or neutralised, the products stored in those cold rooms were transferred preventively to other installations, and no health impact or loss of blood products is to be reported; it names no supplier and gives no temperature figure (translated from French) (EFS, 2026-11-03).

    BleepingComputer, heise and SRF identify the supplier as Coolworld, and SRF reports that the attackers reportedly entered through its remote maintenance (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-04; heise, 2026-11-04; SRF, 2026-11-04). Coolworld's statement of 2026-11-04 says the incident involved remote monitoring software, that the vulnerability reported in the media relates to a component from a third-party supplier outside its control and that a limited number of its units were affected; it does not say that attackers exploited that component (Coolworld, 2026-11-04). BleepingComputer's account reads it the other way, saying Coolworld says the attackers exploited a vulnerability in a third-party component, which the statement does not state (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-04). No source names Danfoss, Copeland or Dixell equipment for this case.

    ENISA, the European Commission (DG CNECT), Europol's EC3, the EU CSIRTs Network and EU-CyCLONe issued a joint statement on 2026-11-04: since 11 October 2026 several commercial refrigeration systems and similar devices have been hit in a number of EU Member States, a new wave occurred on 1 to 2 November, affected Member States are investigating and issuing guidance, and organisations are advised to take immediate action to mitigate exposure in the affected systems; it names no product, actor or count and does not mention Switzerland (ENISA, 2026-11-04). CSIRT Italia's bulletin of 2026-11-04 extends its call to every operator of cold-chain technology, whatever the sector, to apply its earlier measures urgently, removing direct internet exposure of administrative and control interfaces and verifying that operating parameters and alarms are intact first (CSIRT Italia, 2026-11-04). Its bulletin of 2026-10-13, re-issued on 2026-11-04 as version 1.2, asks operators to limit supplier access to authorised resources and periods, to require multi-factor authentication on remote access, applied on the mediating system when the device cannot do it, and to check actual temperatures and alarms with instruments independent of the supervision platform (CSIRT Italia, 2026-11-04). The BSI had urged operators of networked freezer controllers to disconnect them from the internet or at least secure and patch them (heise, 2026-11-04).

    Update06 Nov 2026 03:44 UTC

    SRF reported on 2026-11-05 the first publicly known attack of this kind in Switzerland: the butcher Lüthis Fleischwaren in Köniz, canton Bern, told customers that manipulation of the electronic control of its cooling and freezer systems caused considerable temperature deviations, the owner told SRF he found 50 to 70 degrees in the freezer, and the firm disconnected the installation from the network and closed the branch (translated from German) (SRF, 2026-11-05). Keystone-SDA adds that the shop can probably only reopen on 20 November and that the Bern cantonal police were briefly informed on Monday and had received no complaint yet (Keystone-SDA via swissinfo.ch, 2026-11-05). Volg says three of its stores noticed that parameters in the cooling shelves had been changed (SRF, 2026-11-05). No source names the controller make, the access route or a maintenance provider in the Swiss cases; Keystone-SDA notes that operators can supervise cooling plants centrally over the internet and that the butcher apparently has such an installation, which is the agency's inference and not the owner's statement (Keystone-SDA via swissinfo.ch, 2026-11-05). BACS told Keystone-SDA that it does not comment on specific incidents and has received one reportable notification about a cyberattack on commercial refrigeration so far, and it stressed that voluntary reports are welcome at any time (Keystone-SDA via swissinfo.ch, 2026-11-05). Blutspende SRK Schweiz told Keystone-SDA that no attacks on Swiss blood-donation services are known to date (SRF, 2026-11-05).

    On the same day CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and DOT published a joint fact sheet on malicious cyber activity against industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems worldwide: the actors may exploit vulnerabilities or weaknesses in unpatched or internet-accessible controllers to remotely alter settings and force units into continuous heating and defrost cycles, through authentication bypass, arbitrary code injection or weak configurations, with loss of visibility into current temperatures as a further effect (CISA, 2026-11-05). It names no vendor, CVE, victim or actor; its mitigations include keeping controller panels off the internet, allowlisting the devices and IP addresses that may change settings, defining reasonable setpoint limits for downstream devices with alerting on deviations, and collecting network traffic between OT assets where logs are not available (CISA, 2026-11-05).

    Update07 Nov 2026 03:53 UTC

    NCSC Switzerland edited its advisory on 2026-11-06 with the reason "added Swiss incidents, Copeland/Dixell XWEB and healthcare attacks" and states that "The public information reviewed does not establish a common attack method across these incidents or confirm exploitation of the specific Danfoss CVEs listed above"; it lists CVE-2025-41451, CVE-2025-41450 and CVE-2025-41452 as "alleged as potentially related" (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13). Its checklist asks operators to review refrigeration management systems across all relevant operations, "including equipment managed by external service providers"; to remove direct internet access to management interfaces and provide remote access through a secure VPN; to check temperature settings, defrost schedules, alarm thresholds, notification recipients and user accounts, verify actual temperatures and test alarm delivery; and to apply current firmware and plan to replace equipment that no longer gets security updates (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13). For a suspected compromise it says "A factory reset alone is insufficient if the system remains vulnerable and directly accessible from the internet" (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13).

    Ransom-ISAC published the event log of a Copeland XWEB Pro controller, reportedly on firmware 1.9.0 and reachable through a public address, compromised on 2 November: two Administrator sessions from different external addresses read parameters across 31 devices, then made 588 writes across 26 devices in about six minutes, which Ransom-ISAC reads as scripted or bulk programming rather than manual edits, and 155 further writes across five cold rooms, moving setpoints to 110 and 150 degrees C and recording keypad-lock, alarm-mute and defrost commands (Ransom-ISAC, 2026-11-06). The post says the controller still used its default Administrator credentials, publishes no evidence for that statement, says the log cannot show how the attackers got in or what temperatures were reached, and says it has no evidence linking the case to NoName057(16), Z-Pentest Alliance or the French attacks (Ransom-ISAC, 2026-11-06). Its suggested alerts are more than about 20 parameter-write events a minute from one account, writes to more than three devices within five minutes, and keypad-lock, alarm-mute or defrost commands that do not come from the scheduler (Ransom-ISAC, 2026-11-06).

    Unit 42 counts over 5,000 internet-exposed devices across countries from the manufacturers CERT-FR names on 5 and 6 November, and for France alone 1,853 in the first half of October, 558 in the second half and 316 in the first six days of November, and says the claims of NoName057(16) and Z-Pentest Alliance remain unverified (Unit 42, 2026-11-06). The counts do not show which versions are vulnerable.
