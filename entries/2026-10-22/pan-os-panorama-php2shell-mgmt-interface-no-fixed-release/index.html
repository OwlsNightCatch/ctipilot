    CVE-2026-109618 / CVE-2026-109619, Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS and Panorama: the PHP2Shell flaws affect every release below its train's hotfix through the management web interface, and Palo Alto reports active exploitation against internet-exposed management interfaces; all twenty hotfixes are out

    PAN-OS management interfaces were rooted in the wild via PHP2Shell and a kernel flaw: hotfix, then check for compromise

    first published 2026-10-22 03:41 UTCupdated 2026-11-01 03:27 UTC

    Defender actions

    • Take the management interface of every PA-Series and VM-Series firewall and Panorama off the internet now by restricting it to a jump box or trusted internal addresses, because Palo Alto reports active exploitation against internet-exposed management interfaces, and install the hotfix for your maintenance release, which Palo Alto's table lists for every line from 10.2.7 to 12.2.4 (11.2.10-h17 adds the Brotli fix).
    • Rotate the directory bind account, HA replication keys, certificate private keys and device authentication keys of every PAN-OS firewall and Panorama that was reachable from the internet while unpatched and shows a PHP web shell or a tunnel, because the chain Dream Research recovered harvested exactly those from rooted firewalls, and the hotfix does not remove a web shell or tunnel already in place.

    Analysis

    Palo Alto Networks' advisory of 2026-10-21 lists PAN-OS 12.2, 12.1, 11.2, 11.1 and 10.2 and Panorama as affected by CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, the unauthenticated PHP remote-code-execution flaws (Palo Alto Networks, 2026-10-21) (PHP, 2026-10-21). Only the management web interface is affected; the GlobalProtect portal and gateway and the captive portal are not (Palo Alto Networks, 2026-10-21).

    The advisory says active exploitation has been observed against the management interface exposed to the internet (Palo Alto Networks, 2026-10-28). Dream Research reports that a custom PHP2Shell build for the PAN-OS management web server, chained with the Linux kernel flaw CVE-2026-46300 to turn the web server's unprivileged context into root in one unauthenticated HTTPS request, rooted 25 PAN-OS firewall and management servers in the United States, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea (Dream Research, 2026-10-29). On the second of two firewalls of a US government-affiliated organization (the first crashed) it added a reboot-persistent reverse SSH tunnel and, through it, ran the same chain against the organization's internal firewall management server, decrypted the directory service account's password from the device configuration, enumerated the Active Directory and attempted a password spray and backdoor accounts, which Dream cannot say succeeded (Dream Research, 2026-10-29). Unit 42 says it independently verified the attacker's tooling and results and that customers who applied the available patches are not affected (Unit 42, 2026-10-29); Dream relays that Unit 42 tracks it as CL-UNK-1280, a Chinese-speaking attacker in UTC+8, while Dream names none (Dream Research, 2026-10-29).

    All twenty hotfixes are released (Palo Alto Networks, 2026-10-30); its Threat Prevention coverage (Threat IDs 543004, 97622 to 97625 and twenty further IDs between 97643 and 97678) applies only where inbound management traffic is decrypted on a dataplane interface with the certificate replaced and threat prevention enabled (Palo Alto Networks, 2026-10-27).

    Exposure
    Every PAN-OS firewall and Panorama appliance below its train's hotfix through its management web interface; it is reachable from the internet where the management port, or a dataplane interface with a management profile, answers from outside the administrator range, which Palo Alto says is what is being attacked (Palo Alto Networks, 2026-10-28); Dream found attacker profiles for PAN-OS 12.1, 12.2 and 11.1 only and says the web server also fronts the GlobalProtect portal, which Palo Alto's advisory says is not affected (Dream Research, 2026-10-29; Palo Alto Networks, 2026-10-30).
    Detection
    In management-plane web access logs, very large multipart POST requests from outside the administrator range (Dream Research, 2026-10-29). A hotfix does not remove an attacker already in: on an interface that was internet-reachable and unpatched, look for a PHP file nobody deployed in a web-reachable directory of the management web server, a systemd service or rc.local entry that starts scripts from a hidden directory under /usr/local/lib, and outbound SSH from the management plane; in directory logs, bulk LDAP enumeration and password-spray bursts against all users, launched from the firewall management server (Dream Research, 2026-10-29).
    Defender takeaway
    Move management access behind a jump box or trusted internal addresses, as Palo Alto recommends (Palo Alto Networks, 2026-10-27), and install the hotfix for your maintenance line. An interface that was internet-reachable and unpatched is a possible root compromise until those checks are clean; where a web shell or tunnel turns up, rotate what the device held: the directory bind account, HA replication keys, certificate private keys and device authentication keys (Dream Research, 2026-10-29).

    Update01 Nov 2026 03:27 UTC
