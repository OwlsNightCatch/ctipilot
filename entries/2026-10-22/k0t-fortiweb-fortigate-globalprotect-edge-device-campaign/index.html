    NOTABLECVE-2025-64446 +2exploitedNATOB2threat

    k0t: Group-IB and NyxLab describe a probable access broker that mass-exploits FortiWeb and PAN-OS GlobalProtect, brute-forces FortiGate and decrypts stolen FortiGate configurations offline with the default key, with victims later listed by Qilin and The Gentlemen

    Group-IB: k0t takes edge devices in bulk and decrypts FortiGate configurations offline; enable private-data-encryption

    first published 2026-10-22 03:46 UTC

    Defender actions

    • On every FortiGate (FortiOS 5.6.11, 6.0.7, 6.2.1 or later; check the FortiManager and HA note in Fortinet's FG-IR-19-007 first where it applies), enable private-data-encryption with an operator-defined key, and for any device that was internet-reachable with a brute-forceable administrator login or an unpatched exposed service, treat the LDAP and Active Directory bind and administrator credentials stored in its configuration as known to the attacker and rotate them, bind accounts first.

    Analysis

    Group-IB and NyxLab describe a campaign by an operator they track as k0t (Group-IB lists CryptoFeel as a potential alias, and Scrooge as a likely collaborator and possible alias) that enumerates internet-facing VPN and firewall appliances in bulk and attacks the product directly, chiefly Fortinet firewalls and Palo Alto GlobalProtect portals (Group-IB, 2026-10-21). A custom script exploits CVE-2025-64446 against FortiWeb, a relative path traversal that lets an unauthenticated attacker run administrative commands, or brute-forces exposed Fortinet devices as a fallback (Group-IB, 2026-10-21; CISA KEV, 2026-10-21). Against PAN-OS it exploits CVE-2026-0257, the GlobalProtect authentication bypass, with a forged authentication-override cookie (Group-IB, 2026-10-21). On a compromised FortiGate the tool downloads the configuration and decrypts its secrets locally with the default FortiOS key, the weakness tracked as CVE-2019-6693, recovering LDAP and Active Directory bind and, Group-IB says, administrator credentials, although Fortinet's advisory excludes the administrator's password and says FIPS-CC devices are not impacted; unless private-data-encryption is set, the device falls back to that known key (Group-IB, 2026-10-21; Fortinet, 2019-11-19).

    After access the operators work by hand with NetExec, Impacket, Kerbrute, relayed PetitPotam and Mimikatz, keep access through OpenConnect, MeshCentral agents, AdaptixC2, Atera and AnyDesk, and exfiltrate with Rclone to Wasabi and MEGA (Group-IB, 2026-10-21). Group-IB sees victim overlap with the Qilin and The Gentlemen leak sites and assesses the operators are probably initial-access brokers, but says no relationship is confirmed, and it found indications of a possible connection with the FortiBleed cluster alongside negligible overlap in the compromised credentials (Group-IB, 2026-10-21). CISA lists all three CVEs in KEV, with ransomware use marked known for CVE-2026-0257 and CVE-2019-6693 (CISA KEV, 2026-10-21).

    Exposure
    Internet-reachable FortiGate administrator and VPN logins, FortiWeb up to 8.0.1, 7.6.4, 7.4.9, 7.2.11 or 7.0.11 on its branch (Fortinet, 2025-11-14), and PAN-OS GlobalProtect portals and gateways below the per-branch fixed builds (Palo Alto Networks, 2026-05-13); every FortiGate except FIPS-CC models is exposed to the offline decryption step once its configuration has been downloaded unless private-data-encryption, available from FortiOS 5.6.11, 6.0.7 and 6.2.1 and off by default, is enabled with an operator-defined key, which Fortinet warns can cause install errors and split-brain on FortiManager-managed or HA FortiGates until FortiManager 6.2.7 and FortiGate 6.2.6, 6.4.4 or 6.6.0 and later (Group-IB, 2026-10-21; Fortinet, 2019-11-19).
    Detection
    On edge appliances, unexpected local administrator creation, configuration exports, trusted-host changes, repeated authentication failures, lockout-and-retry patterns and administration from unusual source addresses; on directory infrastructure, the NetExec, Impacket and relay pattern against domain controllers; on endpoints, remote-management agents and dual-use tools without an approved owner. The decryption leaves no trace on the appliance, so a patched or cleaned device may already have yielded its secrets (Group-IB, 2026-10-21). After upgrading FortiWeb, Fortinet advises reviewing configuration and logs for unexpected changes and unauthorised administrator accounts, and until then disabling HTTP and HTTPS on internet-facing interfaces (Fortinet, 2025-11-14).

    Triage: OpenConnect, MeshCentral, Atera, AnyDesk and Rclone are legitimate tools, so Group-IB advises judging execution context, installation source, operator account, destination and timing rather than tool names (Group-IB, 2026-10-21).

    Defender takeaway
    Enable private-data-encryption with an operator-defined key on every FortiGate and rotate the directory and administrator secrets stored in any configuration that may have left the device, because patching or rebuilding does not undo a copy already decrypted; Group-IB also advises investigating lockout events and raising the lockout time against brute force (Group-IB, 2026-10-21).
