    Instructure Canvas breach: ShinyHunters exploits a Free-for-Teacher flaw twice, defaces about 330 login portals, and Instructure reaches an agreement with the actor

    ShinyHunters claims 275 million records from Canvas; Instructure struck an agreement with the gang after a second intrusion defaced school portals

    first published 2026-05-08 05:00 UTCupdated 2026-05-13 05:00 UTC

    Analysis

    Instructure detected unauthorized activity on 2026-04-29 (The Register, 2026-05-12) and disclosed a security incident on Friday, 2026-05-01 (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-03). It said the information involved covers names, email addresses, student ID numbers and messages among users of affected institutions, with no evidence that passwords, dates of birth, government identifiers or financial information were involved (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-03). ShinyHunters claimed the attack, first for "nearly 9,000 schools" and 275 million individuals (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-03), later for about 3.65 TB holding about 275 million records from about 8,800 schools (The Register, 2026-05-12). Instructure says the criminals exploited a vulnerability in its Free-for-Teacher learning system; on 2026-05-07 ShinyHunters used the same vulnerability to deface about 330 Canvas school login portals, and Instructure took Canvas into maintenance mode to contain the activity (The Register, 2026-05-12). Seven Dutch universities disconnected Canvas (NL Times, 2026-05-09), and VU Amsterdam notified the Dutch data protection authority (Techzine EU, 2026-05-08).

    After ShinyHunters set a final deadline of end of day 2026-05-12 (The Register, 2026-05-12) for those affected to negotiate before the data was leaked (Infosecurity Magazine, 2026-05-11), Instructure said on 2026-05-11 that it had reached an agreement with the actor, received "digital confirmation of data destruction (shred logs)" and an assurance that no Instructure customers would be extorted as a result of the incident (Inside Higher Ed, 2026-05-11). The chairman of the US House Homeland Security Committee asked Instructure for a briefing on both intrusions (The Record, 2026-05-12). Instructure retained CrowdStrike for forensic analysis and incident response (The Register, 2026-05-12).

    Defender takeaway
    The agreement does not make the data safe: treat the names, email addresses, student IDs and messages of Canvas users as exposed when planning breach notifications and phishing awareness, whatever the vendor's destruction confirmation says.

    Updates5

    Update09 May 2026 05:00 UTC

    Instructure later attributed the intrusion to a vulnerability in its Free-for-Teacher learning system, and ShinyHunters set a pay-or-leak deadline for 2026-05-12 (The Register, 2026-05-12).

    Update10 May 2026 05:00 UTC

    ShinyHunters left a message on Canvas on the evening of Thursday 2026-05-07 after breaking in again (DutchNews.nl, 2026-05-08). Seven Dutch universities, VU Amsterdam, the University of Amsterdam, Erasmus University Rotterdam, Tilburg University, Eindhoven University of Technology, Maastricht University and the University of Twente, disconnected Canvas (NL Times, 2026-05-09), and VU Amsterdam reported the incident to the Dutch data protection authority (Techzine EU, 2026-05-08). After the first incident Instructure had deployed patches, increased monitoring and rotated application keys (BleepingComputer, 2026-05-03).

    Update12 May 2026 05:00 UTC

    On 2026-05-11 Instructure said it had reached a deal with the actor and received "digital confirmation of data destruction (shred logs)", without disclosing the deal's monetary value (Inside Higher Ed, 2026-05-11). Instructure also confirmed the second intrusion of 2026-05-07, in which about 330 school login portals were defaced through the same Free-for-Teacher vulnerability (The Register, 2026-05-12). ShinyHunters had set a final deadline of end of day 2026-05-12 for individual institutions to contact it, and Instructure's statement says no customers will be extorted as a result of the incident (The Register, 2026-05-12); (Inside Higher Ed, 2026-05-11). CrowdStrike is assisting with the forensic analysis (The Register, 2026-05-12).

    Update13 May 2026 05:00 UTC

    Late on 2026-05-11, US House Homeland Security Committee Chairman Andrew Garbarino wrote to Instructure CEO Steve Daly requesting a briefing by 2026-05-21 on both Canvas intrusions, the volume of data accessed, containment measures and coordination with federal law enforcement and CISA (The Record, 2026-05-12); (The Register, 2026-05-12). The same evening Instructure announced its agreement with ShinyHunters (The Record, 2026-05-12). ShinyHunters' claim covers about 275 million records from about 8,800 schools (The Register, 2026-05-12).

    Correction11 Oct 2026 14:19 UTC

    ShinyHunters claims about 275 million records from about 8,800 schools; about 330 is the number of school login portals it defaced on 2026-05-07 (The Register, 2026-05-12). No source supports the earlier figures of 330 institutions in six countries or 16 million records, and the 44 Dutch institutions are names on ShinyHunters' own victim list as BNR reviewed it, not confirmed victims (NL Times, 2026-05-05); seven Dutch universities disconnected Canvas (NL Times, 2026-05-09). Instructure attributes both intrusions to a vulnerability in its Free-for-Teacher system, not to a compromised LTI integration account, and the actor is ShinyHunters; the earlier references to WorldLeaks, a EUR 3.2 million demand, a 3 GB sample and German or Dutch regulator inquiries had no source. Instructure announced its agreement with the actor on 2026-05-11 and said no customers would be extorted as a result (Inside Higher Ed, 2026-05-11).
