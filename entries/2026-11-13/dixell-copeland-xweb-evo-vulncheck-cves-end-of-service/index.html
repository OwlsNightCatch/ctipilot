    Dixell/Copeland XWEB EVO refrigeration supervisors: VulnCheck published 14 CVEs on 2026-11-12, seven scored CVSS 4.0 9.2 to 9.3 (unauthenticated command injection that runs as root, a hard-coded Administrator password, Administrator and root passwords computed from the MAC address and date); Copeland ended support on 2026-03-16 and ships no fixes; no exploitation reported

    XWEB EVO refrigeration controllers: 14 CVEs, unauthenticated root RCE and a hard-coded password, no fix will ship

    first published 2026-11-13 04:02 UTC

    Analysis

    VulnCheck, the assigning CNA, describes unauthenticated command injection in XWEB EVO through the password field (VulnCheck, 2026-11-12) and the username field (VulnCheck, 2026-11-12) of its login and API scripts, and through the sh parameter of the setuid xwconsole.cgi (VulnCheck, 2026-11-12), all reaching root; a hard-coded password for the built-in Administrator account that gives a full session in one request (VulnCheck, 2026-11-12); and Administrator and root passwords that can be computed offline from the device MAC address and the date (VulnCheck, 2026-11-12; VulnCheck, 2026-11-12; VulnCheck, 2026-11-12). The cluster holds 14 records, and the seven scored 9.2 or higher are the ones covered here (ENISA EUVD, 2026-11-12). The records' ranges end before 5.6.2 or 5.7.0 or run through 5.8.8, but Copeland's download pages list EVO packages only up to 5.2.3 of 2018 (Copeland, 2026-11-13), and its bulletin says software support ended on 2026-03-16 with no further firmware or security updates (Copeland, 2026-03-16), so no source shows where a fixed build can be had.

    No source reports exploitation or a public proof of concept for these CVEs, and none ties them to the compromises of Danfoss, Copeland and Dixell controllers. Copeland's bulletin of 2026-10-14, carried in the Dixell France guide that CERT-FR links, acknowledges a vulnerability on an end-of-life generation of XWEB EVO controllers and tells operators to remove all XWEB EVO units from the public internet immediately (translated from French) (Dixell France, 2026-10-14); NCSC Switzerland says that guidance addresses end-of-service XWEB EVO systems and that the public information does not confirm exploitation of the Danfoss CVEs it lists (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-11-06), and BACS lists Copeland and Dixell XWEB as potentially affected (BACS, 2026-11-10). The one compromised-unit log read concerns an XWEB Pro, not an EVO (Ransom-ISAC, 2026-11-06).

    Exposure
    An XWEB EVO unit, sold until October 2023 (Copeland, 2026-03-16), whose login page appears when the site's public address is opened from outside the site network, which is the vendor's own test (Dixell France, 2026-10-14).
    Detection
    No source gives log artefacts for these flaws. After suspected access the vendor's procedure is to review the user list and profiles and the alarm categories, levels and recipients with a test alarm, and to read product temperatures directly in the cabinets because setpoints or alarm thresholds may have changed; CERT-FR says a factory reset removes persistence but leaves an end-of-life unit vulnerable (Dixell France, 2026-10-14; CERT-FR, 2026-10-16).
    Defender takeaway
    No fix is coming, so keep XWEB EVO units off the internet and reach them only through a VPN, and plan the move to the supported XWEB Pro (Dixell France, 2026-10-14).
