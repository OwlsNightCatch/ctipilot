    Apple spyware alerts reached German MPs and ministry staff; BSI: assume the iPhone may be compromised

    first published 2026-11-08 03:29 UTC

    Analysis

    Apple has sent a new round of threat notifications to suspected mercenary-spyware targets in 99 countries, according to a notification screenshot posted by the researcher Costin Raiu, who says he verified several victims' notices (The Record, 2026-11-02). Germany's BSI says several Bundestag members and other public figures have received it since 2026-10-29, that it does not know whether the alerts mean actual compromise or only attempted attacks, and that it wrote to the members on 2026-10-30 (translated from German) (BSI, 2026-11-02). Germany's digital minister confirmed that members of the federal government were also warned (Euronews, 2026-11-02), and The Record, citing Der Spiegel, reports that staff of federal ministries, lawmakers and journalists travelling with the Chancellor in Asia were among the recipients (The Record, 2026-11-03). Apple attributes nothing to any actor or region (Apple, 2026-08-13), and neither Apple nor BSI has named a spyware vendor or an infection vector. BSI notes that mercenary spyware is generally assumed to infect devices without any action by the user, for example through a prepared message that need not be opened, and that a compromised device exposes messages including those in encrypted messengers, calls, contacts, calendar, location, camera and microphone (BSI, 2026-11-02).

    BSI's guidance for a recipient: assume the device may be compromised; update the operating system at once unless a forensic analysis is intended; keep Lockdown Mode on permanently, also on a new device, noting that it hampers new attacks but does not remove an existing infection; where the risk looks high, consider replacing the device, resetting every account used on it and changing the phone number; then set a new device passcode, change the Apple Account and mail passwords, re-enrol authenticator apps instead of transferring them, enable Signal's registration lock and WhatsApp's two-step verification with new PINs, remove linked devices nobody recognises, and report the notification to BSI's service centre (BSI, 2026-11-02).

    Exposure
    Apple sends these notifications to individuals it judges to have been individually targeted, "likely because of who they are or what they do", and names politicians and diplomats among the usual targets (Apple, 2026-08-13); a recipient confirms authenticity on the lock screen, in Settings, in the e-mail to the Apple Account address and in the banner at account.apple.com after signing in (Apple, 2026-08-13).
    Detection
    Apple relies solely on its own threat intelligence and says it cannot say what causes a notification (Apple, 2026-08-13), and BSI's guidance names no artefact to check, so the organisational signal is a recipient reporting the notification; unusual device behaviour such as frequent crashes or fast battery drain is the only on-device hint BSI mentions (BSI, 2026-11-02).

    Triage: a genuine notification never asks to click a link, open a file, install an app or profile, or give a password or verification code; a message that does is a phishing imitation, and the recipient should open account.apple.com directly in the browser instead (Apple, 2026-08-13).

    Defender takeaway
    Give people who are plausible targets the authenticity check above and a named internal reporting path, and keep a replace-device-and-reset-accounts procedure that follows BSI's order; a recipient updates iOS at once unless a forensic analysis is intended, considers Lockdown Mode and keeps it on permanently once enabled, and considers replacing the device where the risk is rated high, treating it as possibly compromised until then.
