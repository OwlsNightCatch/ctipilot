    2026-11-08NOTABLEApple spyware alerts reached German MPs and ministry staff; BSI: assume the iPhone may be compromised

    Defender takeaway

    Give people who are plausible targets the authenticity check above and a named internal reporting path, and keep a replace-device-and-reset-accounts procedure that follows BSI's order; a recipient updates iOS at once unless a forensic analysis is intended, considers Lockdown Mode and keeps it on permanently once enabled, and considers replacing the device where the risk is rated high, treating it as possibly compromised until then.

    Exposure · triage · detection
    Exposure

    Apple sends these notifications to individuals it judges to have been individually targeted, "likely because of who they are or what they do", and names politicians and diplomats among the usual targets (Apple, 2026-08-13); a recipient confirms authenticity on the lock screen, in Settings, in the e-mail to the Apple Account address and in the banner at account.apple.com after signing in (Apple, 2026-08-13).

    Triage

    A genuine notification never asks to click a link, open a file, install an app or profile, or give a password or verification code; a message that does is a phishing imitation, and the recipient should open account.apple.com directly in the browser instead (Apple, 2026-08-13).

    Detection

    Apple relies solely on its own threat intelligence and says it cannot say what causes a notification (Apple, 2026-08-13), and BSI's guidance names no artefact to check, so the organisational signal is a recipient reporting the notification; unusual device behaviour such as frequent crashes or fast battery drain is the only on-device hint BSI mentions (BSI, 2026-11-02).

    NOTABLENATOA2

    Apple's mercenary-spyware threat notifications of late October reached Bundestag members and federal-government staff in Germany; BSI tells recipients to treat the iPhone as possibly compromised, update, keep Lockdown Mode on and consider replacing the device; no actor or vector is public

    Apple has sent a new round of threat notifications to suspected mercenary-spyware targets in 99 countries, according to a notification screenshot posted by the researcher Costin Raiu, who says he verified several victims' notices (The Record, 2026-11-02). Germany's BSI says several Bundestag members and other public figures have received it since 2026-10-29, that it does not know whether the alerts mean actual compromise or only attempted attacks, and that it wrote to the members on 2026-10-30 (translated from German) (BSI, 2026-11-02). Germany's digital minister confirmed that members of the federal government were also warned (Euronews, 2026-11-02), and The Record, citing Der Spiegel, reports that staff of federal ministries, lawmakers and journalists travelling with the Chancellor in Asia were among the recipients (The Record, 2026-11-03). Apple attributes nothing to any actor or region (Apple, 2026-08-13), and neither Apple nor BSI has named a spyware vendor or an infection vector. BSI notes that mercenary spyware is generally assumed to infect devices without any action by the user, for example through a prepared message that need not be opened, and that a compromised device exposes messages including those in encrypted messengers, calls, contacts, calendar, location, camera and microphone (BSI, 2026-11-02).

    BSI's guidance for a recipient: assume the device may be compromised; update the operating system at once unless a forensic analysis is intended; keep Lockdown Mode on permanently, also on a new device, noting that it hampers new attacks but does not remove an existing infection; where the risk looks high, consider replacing the device, resetting every account used on it and changing the phone number; then set a new device passcode, change the Apple Account and mail passwords, re-enrol authenticator apps instead of transferring them, enable Signal's registration lock and WhatsApp's two-step verification with new PINs, remove linked devices nobody recognises, and report the notification to BSI's service centre (BSI, 2026-11-02).

    Exposure
    Apple sends these notifications to individuals it judges to have been individually targeted, "likely because of who they are or what they do", and names politicians and diplomats among the usual targets (Apple, 2026-08-13); a recipient confirms authenticity on the lock screen, in Settings, in the e-mail to the Apple Account address and in the banner at account.apple.com after signing in (Apple, 2026-08-13).
    Detection
    Apple relies solely on its own threat intelligence and says it cannot say what causes a notification (Apple, 2026-08-13), and BSI's guidance names no artefact to check, so the organisational signal is a recipient reporting the notification; unusual device behaviour such as frequent crashes or fast battery drain is the only on-device hint BSI mentions (BSI, 2026-11-02).

    Triage: a genuine notification never asks to click a link, open a file, install an app or profile, or give a password or verification code; a message that does is a phishing imitation, and the recipient should open account.apple.com directly in the browser instead (Apple, 2026-08-13).

    Defender takeaway
    Give people who are plausible targets the authenticity check above and a named internal reporting path, and keep a replace-device-and-reset-accounts procedure that follows BSI's order; a recipient updates iOS at once unless a forensic analysis is intended, considers Lockdown Mode and keeps it on permanently once enabled, and considers replacing the device where the risk is rated high, treating it as possibly compromised until then.

Sekoia retrieved three chains from the delivery host, which profiles the visitor and serves a platform-specific exploit without user interaction: an iOS 16.7 chain (CVE-2025-43529, then CVE-2024-27834) that steals the cookies of running apps; a DarkSword-based iOS chain that Sekoia's table labels iOS 18.5 (CVE-2025-43529 with CVE-2026-20700, then CVE-2025-14174 with CVE-2025-43510, then CVE-2025-43520) that harvests the phone; and an Android chain for Chrome 139 to 145 except 143 (CVE-2026-3910, then the Adreno driver flaw CVE-2025-27038) that takes Chrome cookies, saved logins, history and Wi-Fi configuration (Sekoia, 2026-11-04). The payloads exfiltrate data without establishing persistence, and Sekoia says none of the chains contains a zero-day or works against fully updated devices (Sekoia, 2026-11-04).

    Delivery used JavaScript injected into legitimate sites, among them a Ukrainian gas producer, a US chemical-industry trade association, a Bulgarian military-related site and an Australian clinic; reverse-proxy clones of high-profile sites; and a one-week compromise, 27 August to 3 September 2026, of the Plerdy SEO tracker script, which Sekoia says exposed hundreds or thousands of sites, mostly Ukrainian (Sekoia, 2026-11-04). Sekoia names no Swiss victim, counts no infected devices and says it is working with national CERTs on victim identification.

    Exposure
    IPhones and iPads below the builds that fix the six iOS flaws, and Android phones on Chrome before the fix of CVE-2026-3910 or an unpatched Adreno driver. Sekoia's iOS 16.7 chain filters for iOS 16.7 and 16.7.10, and the sources do not establish whether the later 16.7.x builds Apple lists for iPhone 8, 8 Plus, X, iPad 5th generation and the first-generation iPad Pro models, up to 16.7.16 of 2026-05-11, are exploitable (Sekoia, 2026-11-04; Apple, 2026-05-11).

    Fixes: iOS 26.2 and 18.7.3 close CVE-2025-43529 and CVE-2025-14174, iOS 26.1 and 18.7.2 close CVE-2025-43510 and CVE-2025-43520, iOS 26.3 closes CVE-2026-20700, and Chrome 146 closes CVE-2026-3910 (Apple, 2025-12-12; Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026-03-18; Chrome Releases, 2026-03-12).

    Detection
    Sekoia names one on-device trace, a marker cookie that the iOS 16.7 chain's script sets as a replay guard so the exploit does not fire twice on a victim; it publishes some indicators and withholds several delivery and exfiltration hosts, so a visit to an injected site shows in web proxy and DNS telemetry from managed phones only against indicators obtained from Sekoia or a national CERT; the dependable check is the OS and Chrome version inventory (Sekoia, 2026-11-04).
    Defender takeaway
    Bring managed iOS devices to the newest release for each model and Android phones to current Chrome and the OEM patch level, because Sekoia says updated devices resist these chains and CISA lists every flaw they deliver as exploited (Sekoia, 2026-11-04; CISA KEV, 2026-11-04); the devices that cannot go past iOS 16.7.x are the open question.
