    Triage

    Detection

    Application and identity telemetry carried the evidence, not endpoint telemetry: Kudelski says abnormal application request volumes, extensive authentication failures and suspicious reconnaissance appeared well before data collection began and the attack still ran through the night (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27). Its recommended detections include distributed requests against one business function across rotating infrastructure, excessive one-time-code failures, authenticator enrollment without completed MFA, activity against retired APIs, source-map access by automated clients and abnormal onboarding and recovery sequences (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27). The sequence to alert on is a burst of identification requests, then accounts created in the names of existing customers without their original passwords, then sensitive information accessed by those accounts.

    Entries about European insurer offensive-AI intrusion (Kudelski Security case) (1)

    2026-11-28 · view entry permalink →

    NOTABLENATOB2

    Kudelski Security: a one-night intrusion at a European insurer, assessed as run by an offensive AI framework, abused customer onboarding, identity migration and a retired API to create accounts in customers' names and extract more than 100 documents, with no malware

    Kudelski Security's incident-response team describes a one-night intrusion at an unnamed European insurer in which about three million identification requests arrived in under nine hours, about 30 legitimate customer identities were identified, more than a dozen customer accounts were created in those customers' names without their passwords, and more than 100 documents with sensitive customer data were extracted (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27). Kudelski found no malware and no compromise of the servers hosting the customer portal, and each forged account could read only its own customer's data (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27). It assesses with high confidence that an offensive AI framework was used, from correlated indicators of compromise, infrastructure characteristics, previously documented attack patterns, machine-speed execution, application-specific reconnaissance and automated handling of complex identity workflows, names no framework and could not establish how much human supervision there was (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27).

    Among the weaknesses it lists, mostly business logic rather than software bugs: the API of a retired web interface remained accessible, identity information could be tested at scale, CAPTCHA was not enforced by the underlying API, rate limits were inactive after an infrastructure migration, alternative registration processes that did not consistently enforce prior identity checks, unverified contact information inherited a verified status, token validation was incomplete, authorization-related information was exposed to the client, and an identity-provider vulnerability weakened authenticator enrollment (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27). The identity-provider flaw is cited as GHSA-qqv8-37gw-ww2q, which is ZITADEL's advisory for a missing second-factor check on authenticator enrollment in its Login V2 interface, affecting 4.0.0 through 4.20.4 and fixed in 4.20.5; the post does not say the insurer ran ZITADEL (ZITADEL, 2026-11-18). Kudelski compares the case with BabidiGhost: that operation tried an AI agent against many organizations, this one explored a single organization's interconnected application and identity ecosystem in depth (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27).

    Exposure
    A customer or citizen portal that runs identity onboarding, identity migration or account recovery; the case came from an insurer, and the post names no public-sector victim.
    Detection
    Application and identity telemetry carried the evidence, not endpoint telemetry: Kudelski says abnormal application request volumes, extensive authentication failures and suspicious reconnaissance appeared well before data collection began and the attack still ran through the night (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27). Its recommended detections include distributed requests against one business function across rotating infrastructure, excessive one-time-code failures, authenticator enrollment without completed MFA, activity against retired APIs, source-map access by automated clients and abnormal onboarding and recovery sequences (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27). The sequence to alert on is a burst of identification requests, then accounts created in the names of existing customers without their original passwords, then sensitive information accessed by those accounts.

    Triage: a newly created account may satisfy the portal's registration requirements, so a single account can look legitimate; Kudelski says abnormal request volumes, extensive authentication failures and reconnaissance activity were detectable well before data collection (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27).

    Defender takeaway
    For any portal with identity lifecycle workflows, Kudelski's hardening list is the checklist: user-supplied contact attributes must not inherit verified status, identity migrations must preserve existing account activation states, retiring a front end includes decommissioning its supporting APIs, and rate limiting, CAPTCHA and server-side validation must be tested again after each change (Kudelski Security, 2026-11-27); alert on identification-request bursts, and move a ZITADEL deployment on 4.0.0 through 4.20.4 to 4.20.5 (ZITADEL, 2026-11-18).
