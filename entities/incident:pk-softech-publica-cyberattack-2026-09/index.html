    PK Softech cyberattack and Publica data outflow (September 2026)

    incident · incident:pk-softech-publica-cyberattack-2026-09

    Malware-enabled intrusion at PK Softech AG (Reinach BL), supplier of the pension-administration application of the Swiss Federal Pension Fund Publica, detected at the end of September 2026; PK Softech says it must be assumed that data left its systems, the Confederation says Publica informed its insured persons about the data outflow, the scope of Publica data is still being established and the Federal Prosecutor's Office is investigating (Swiss Federal Administration and PK Softech, 2026-10-08). Tamedia reports, and Inside IT relays, that the Akira group holds the data and demands ransom, which neither the Confederation nor Publica has confirmed (Tamedia and Inside IT, 2026-10-20).

    Entries about PK Softech cyberattack and Publica data outflow (September 2026) (1)

    HIGHupdatedNATOA2

    PK Softech, software supplier of Swiss pension funds: malware and a data outflow at Publica, with the City of Zurich fund PKZH saying its insured persons' test data was stolen, the Bernese funds BPK and BLVK and Pensionskasse Post also reporting the supplier incident, and the Federal Prosecutor's Office investigating

    The Confederation, in a release headlined "data outflow confirmed" (translated from German), says an external software supplier of the federal pension fund Publica detected a cyberattack at the end of September, informed its other customers, and that the Federal Prosecutor's Office has opened an investigation; no other federal office has a business relationship with the supplier (Swiss Federal Administration, 2026-10-08); the supplier, PK Softech AG, says unknown persons used malware to reach part of its IT infrastructure and that it must be assumed data left its systems, with type and scope still under investigation (PK Softech, 2026-10-08). Publica insures, among others, staff of the federal administration and the ETH domain, about 70,000 active members and 41,600 pensioners at the end of 2025 (watson.ch, 2026-10-08), and its own page lists name, date of birth, AHV number, address, business and private contact details, pension data such as salary and insured earnings, civil status, partner details and IBAN as data that could be affected, adding that its investigation indicates personal data of insured persons has presumably left and that it cannot rule out that an individual member's data left (Publica, 2026-10-08). Netzwoche reported on 2026-10-08 that it was still unclear whether data of the pension fund actually left (Netzwoche, 2026-10-08); no access vector is public, and on 2026-10-08 no actor was public either and Publica declined to say whether a ransom was demanded (watson.ch, 2026-10-08); the supplier is a software supplier of a federal institution, and Publica data may be affected (Swiss Federal Administration, 2026-10-08). The Bernische Pensionskasse says its supplier is the same company (BPK, 2026-10-09), and the Bernische Lehrerversicherungskasse (BLVK, 2026-10-08) and Pensionskasse Post (Pensionskasse Post, 2026-10-09) report the supplier incident too. The Pensionskasse Stadt Zürich, which still runs another provider's system and plans to move to PK Softech's in early 2027, says its inquiries indicate that personal data of its insured persons and pensioners, handed over as test data, was stolen (PKZH, 2026-10-15), and Pensionskasse Post says personal data did leave but not yet whether its insured persons' data is among it (Pensionskasse Post, 2026-10-15). Tamedia's Tages-Anzeiger reports that the Akira ransomware group holds about 100 GB of compressed pension data on well over 100,000 current and former federal employees, including staff of the security authorities, and demands ransom, which Inside IT relays without the volume; as of 2026-10-20 neither the Confederation nor Publica had confirmed the perpetrator or the demand, and Publica said it still had no indication that data was published on the darkweb (Tages-Anzeiger, 2026-10-20; Inside IT, 2026-10-20; Publica, 2026-10-20). Netzwoche, relaying the same paper, adds that the Federal Council and the security authorities now assume data on practically all current and former federal and federally affiliated staff left, federal judges and former Federal Council members among them (Netzwoche, 2026-10-21).

    PK Softech had confirmed in writing to Publica in January 2026 that it deleted the data of the 2019 migration and held no personal data in plaintext, and the Tages-Anzeiger reports that this apparently did not happen, because the data was still on its servers when the attackers got in (Tages-Anzeiger, 2026-10-21; Inside IT, 2026-10-22). Publica confirmed the sequence to the paper: after the Xplain case it asked PK Softech to delete all legacy data, including that of the 2019 migration, and received written confirmation, and it is now clarifying why personal data presumably lay on the servers, while PK Softech did not answer and says it does not comment on individual customer relationships (Inside IT, 2026-10-22). Inside IT, citing Tamedia, adds that the Federal Prosecutor's Office confirmed that data was extracted by the Akira ransomware group, which 20 Minuten words as according to findings so far; the statement is second-hand and Publica's own page of 2026-10-20 still says it is clarifying whether and whose Publica data left (Inside IT, 2026-10-22; 20 Minuten, 2026-10-22; Publica, 2026-10-20). The Ticino cantonal pension fund IPCT first said through Corriere del Ticino, relayed by Ticinonline, that potentially all of its insured could be involved (Ticinonline, 2026-10-20); its letter of 2026-10-23 to its roughly 17,000 active insured and 10,000 pensioners narrows that: PK Softech told it on 2026-10-14 that the authorities had established that personal data were taken from the supplier's servers, its own checks found data of about 1,800 persons among the documents on the attacked server, it has no certain evidence that these data were taken but finds it plausible, and it decided to write to all insured because it cannot exclude that more was affected (IPCT, 2026-10-23; Ticinonline, 2026-10-23).

    The supplier's customer base is now partly public and includes cantonal-level institutions. The Bernische Pensionskasse (BPK) states in a notice dated 2026-10-09 that an external software supplier of the BPK suffered a cyberattack at the end of September 2026 and that this is "the same company that also supplies the federal pension fund Publica" (translated from German); the supplier filed a criminal complaint, informed the relevant federal bodies and its affected customers, various federal bodies are clarifying with it which data is affected, and the Federal Prosecutor's Office has opened an investigation (BPK, 2026-10-09). The BPK says there are currently no indications that its data was stolen but that this cannot be entirely excluded, and that insured persons are being informed (BPK, 2026-10-09). Netzwoche reports that the Canton of Bern's own notice names the BPK and the Bernische Lehrerversicherungskasse (BLVK) as concretely affected, that the BPK insures canton staff and the personnel of the three cantonal universities (42,145 active insured and 18,321 pensioners on 31 December 2025) and that the BLVK insures 21,417 active Bernese teachers (Netzwoche, 2026-10-08). The BLVK's own notice does not name its supplier; it says the supplier took the affected environment off the network, brought in external specialists, informed the authorities and filed a criminal complaint, and that the supplier and the BLVK are still checking whether and to what extent BLVK data is affected (BLVK, 2026-10-08). Pensionskasse Post, the occupational pension fund of Swiss Post, names PK Softech and says whether and to what extent its insured persons' data is affected is under review; it states that its data at PK Softech is anonymised and holds no particularly sensitive personal data such as IV disability-insurance files, a claim that is the fund's own and not independently verified (Pensionskasse Post, 2026-10-09). Inside IT, working from the AWP agency, lists the BLVK, the BPK, Migros-Pensionskasse, Pensionskasse Post and Syngenta Pensionskasse as customers of the manufacturer "among others" and says some of the funds have already announced that they are checking with PK Softech for a possible data outflow (Inside IT, 2026-10-09). PK Softech says it has been fully available to its customers again since 2 October 2026 (PK Softech, 2026-10-08). The access vector, the actor, which data belongs to which fund and whether a ransom was demanded were not public in any source read as of that date.

    On the Cybercrimepolice platform, dated 2026-10-13, the cantonal police warn that after a cyberattack on a software provider of a pension fund, criminals could use the leaked personal and financial data to phone those affected and lure them into fraudulent investments of their pension assets (translated from German); the page describes a ransomware attack at the end of September and names no company (Cybercrimepolice, 2026-10-13).     Update17 Oct 2026 03:35 UTCrun 2026-10-17T0256Z-inteltitleheadlinesummarysourcesevidencesourcing_noteactionsbody

    The Pensionskasse Stadt Zürich (PKZH), the City of Zurich's pension fund, has moved from checking to confirming. Its notice of 2026-10-15 says its inquiries so far indicate that personal data of PKZH insured persons was also stolen in the attack, and that this data had been used in tests for the planned system switch (PKZH, 2026-10-15); its FAQ adds that data was taken from PK Softech's system, including personal data of insured persons and of pensioners (PKZH, 2026-10-15). It does not repeat its earlier statement that its data at PK Softech is anonymised, and lists name, address, date of birth, AHV number, contact details, e-mail address and pension data as data that could be affected (Pensionskasse Post, 2026-10-15). The access vector, the actor and any ransom demand were not public in any source read as of that date.

Inside IT, working from the Tamedia research, dates the written confirmation to January 2026 and says PK Softech confirmed it held no personal data in plaintext and had deleted the data of the 2019 migration, that Publica confirmed the sequence (after the Xplain case it asked the supplier to delete all legacy data, including that of the 2019 migration, and received written confirmation), that Publica is clarifying why personal data presumably lay on the servers regardless, and that PK Softech did not answer and does not comment on individual customer relationships (Inside IT, 2026-10-22). 20 Minuten reports the same sequence and notes that PK Softech received the data in 2019 in a migration for Publica (20 Minuten, 2026-10-22).

    Inside IT also relays, from Tamedia, that the Federal Prosecutor's Office confirmed that data was extracted by the Akira ransomware group, which still threatens to publish or sell it; 20 Minuten writes that according to findings so far Akira stole the data (Inside IT, 2026-10-22; 20 Minuten, 2026-10-22). This is a reported confirmation of the actor by an authority, but it reaches the reader second-hand through the paywalled Tamedia article: the Federal Prosecutor's Office is not quoted directly, and Publica's own page, in its entry of 2026-10-20, still says it is clarifying whether data of Publica left and whose (Publica, 2026-10-20).

    The Ticino cantonal pension fund IPCT says through Corriere del Ticino, relayed by Ticinonline on 2026-10-20, that potentially all of its insured could be involved but that there is no evidence yet that their data was actually taken; it has filed a complaint with the Federal Prosecutor's Office and is preparing a letter to the insured warning of telephone and e-mail fraud (Ticinonline, 2026-10-20). TicinoNews adds, from its own information, that a folder labelled IPCT with the data of a few hundred insured persons was on the supplier's server, while IPCT, which has about 27,000 affiliated persons, says it has no certainty yet that data of its insured is involved (TicinoNews, 2026-10-21).

    Update24 Oct 2026 03:41 UTCrun 2026-10-24T0255Z-intelactionsbodyevidencesourcessourcing_notesummary

    The Ticino cantonal pension fund IPCT has put numbers on its exposure in a letter of 2026-10-23 to its roughly 17,000 active insured and 10,000 pensioners (IPCT, 2026-10-23). PK Softech told it in the late afternoon of 2026-10-14 that the authorities had established that personal data were taken from the supplier's servers, without saying which funds the data belonged to, and the fund's checks to date found among the documents on the attacked server data of about 1,800 persons, held there for two ongoing projects of spot checks and recalculations (IPCT, 2026-10-23). About 1,700 are persons who left the fund through resignation or death in 2024 and 2025: surname, first name, postal address, date of birth, AHV number and insured number, the exit-benefit amount and the receiving fund with its bank account (IBAN), and the personal IBAN for about 50 who were paid out directly, with no e-mail address or telephone number (IPCT, 2026-10-23). About 80 are insured persons on a disability pension whose passive pension data was being updated: name, date of birth, AHV number, insured number, passive insured salary, passive retirement assets, degree and start of invalidity, with no contact data (IPCT, 2026-10-23).

    The fund says it has no certain evidence that these data were taken but that it appears plausible, that savings capital and pensions of all insured are untouched, that the cantonal administration's IT centre hosting the fund's infrastructure was not breached, and that it filed a criminal complaint against unknown persons and was recognised as private plaintiff, while the Federal Prosecutor's Office and the federal police investigate (IPCT, 2026-10-23; Ticinonline, 2026-10-23). It decided to send the letter to all insured because its information is partial, the analyses are still evolving and it may never have certainty, and it asks them to watch for unexpected e-mails, calls and messages over the coming weeks and never to disclose passwords, access data or personal data unless sure of the requester (IPCT, 2026-10-23). The letter names PK Softech's other customers as Publica, large cantonal public funds such as Bern and Zurich and company funds such as Migros and Post, for hundreds of thousands of insured persons in total (IPCT, 2026-10-23).
