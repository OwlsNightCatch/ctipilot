    NOTABLEupdatedNATOA2

    Dutch IGJ: Clinical Diagnostics labs failed NEN 7510 at the time of the July 2025 Nova breach, which reached data of about 941,000 people through an account without MFA in unmonitored legacy systems

    The Dutch Health and Youth Care Inspectorate (IGJ) concluded on 2026-05-13 that the business units Clinical Diagnostics LCPL BV and NMDL BV in Rijswijk "did not meet the standard either at the time of the hack or during an inspection visit in December 2025" (translated from Dutch): among other gaps, no independent audit of information security had been carried out and processing risks had not been mapped periodically, so the company could not determine which data-security measures it needed (IGJ, 2026-05-13). The Wabvpz requires organisations working with patient data to meet NEN 7510, and IGJ says working to the norm could have reduced the likelihood of an incident like this and limited its consequences (IGJ, 2026-05-13).

    Computable reports from the inspection that the company could not establish how the attackers gained access to the compromised user account, which had a 16-character password but no active multi-factor authentication, and that the legacy environments the attackers entered had dropped out of the security operations centre's monitoring through human error, because the SOC wrongly believed them inactive, so anomalous patterns in the logs went unnoticed (Computable, 2026-05-13). Data of about 941,000 people, mostly cervical-cancer screening participants, was viewed or stolen, and Computable reports that the company paid a ransom (Computable, 2026-05-13). The ransomware-as-a-service group Nova had threatened in July 2025 to leak the data, and the screening agency said the lab could not confirm that 941,000 was the full extent (GovInfoSecurity, 2025-09-03).

    IGJ asked the company to demonstrate NEN 7510 compliance at short notice; under the Wabvpz it cannot impose punitive measures, while the Dutch data protection authority (AP), which is investigating under the GDPR, can, and the company says an external audit came out positive and certification is expected soon (IGJ, 2026-05-13). IGJ is using the case to call on healthcare providers to work demonstrably to NEN 7510, with a certificate or an independent assessment, and to check actively that the labs and other third parties they use comply (Computable, 2026-05-13).

    Exposure
    Healthcare providers in the Netherlands that process patient data are bound by NEN 7510 under the Wabvpz (IGJ, 2026-05-13), and IGJ's supervision will now look at whether they verify their labs and other suppliers (Computable, 2026-05-13).
    Detection
    What hid this intrusion was monitoring coverage, not missing signatures: legacy environments that were still reachable had been dropped from SOC collection as inactive, and the account used had no second factor, so the checks that would have caught it are a reconciliation of SOC log sources against systems still reachable and a review of authentication logs for accounts without multi-factor authentication (Computable, 2026-05-13).
    Defender takeaway
    For any organisation whose sensitive data is processed by a laboratory or other service provider, ask for an independent assessment of that provider's information security, as IGJ now expects of Dutch healthcare providers, and confirm that legacy environments holding the data are either decommissioned or inside monitoring scope (Computable, 2026-05-13).
