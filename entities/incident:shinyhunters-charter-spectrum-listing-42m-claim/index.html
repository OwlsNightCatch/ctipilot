    ROUTINEupdatedNATOB2

    7-Eleven confirms breach of franchisee documents after ShinyHunters claims 600,000+ Salesforce records

    7-Eleven's notification letters went out on 2026-05-01 (Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-05-20) and say an unauthorized third party gained access on 2026-04-08 to systems used to store franchisee documents (Security Affairs, 2026-05-18), which held personal information from the franchise application process, including names and addresses; the Vermont filing adds Social Security numbers and the Massachusetts filing Social Security numbers and driver's license data (Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-05-20). Filings in Massachusetts, Maine and Vermont count 47, 2 and 1 affected people, the letters offer up to 24 months of IDX identity-theft protection, and 7-Eleven says it has no reason to believe customer data was affected (Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-05-20); the notices became public on 2026-05-18 (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-18). ShinyHunters had listed 7-Eleven on its leak site on 2026-04-17 claiming more than 600,000 Salesforce records with personal and corporate data, demanded a ransom by 2026-04-21 and then offered the data for $250,000 on a hacker forum (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-18); 7-Eleven did not respond when asked about that claim (Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-05-20). Its relevance lies in the actor: SecurityWeek says ShinyHunters has targeted organizations' Salesforce instances since mid-2025 through phishing, abuse of third-party integrations or misconfigurations rather than Salesforce vulnerabilities, and that the group and affiliated actors recently claimed Instructure, Vimeo, Wynn Resorts, Vercel and Medtronic (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-18), while Security Affairs lists the European Commission among its earlier claims (Security Affairs, 2026-05-18). No source says how the 7-Eleven systems were accessed.

    Exposure
    Organizations whose Salesforce tenants can be reached through phished accounts, third-party integrations or misconfigurations, the paths SecurityWeek gives for the group's campaign (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-18); CyberInsider describes recent victims whose environments allegedly held exposed credentials, authentication tokens or improperly secured integrations (CyberInsider, 2026-05-23).
    Defender takeaway

    Update25 May 2026 05:00 UTCrun 2026-05-25-d675ef38sectorssourcesbody

    ShinyHunters added Charter Communications, which operates the Spectrum brand, to its leak site in the week of 2026-05-23, claiming over 42 million records containing personal information and threatening to leak them unless negotiations opened before 2026-05-27; the nature of the data had not been independently verified (CyberInsider, 2026-05-23). Charter confirmed a cybersecurity incident and told CyberInsider it was "following our security protocols" and alerting authorities, adding: "No sensitive personal information (PI) or customer proprietary network information (CPNI) data was exfiltrated by the threat actor." (CyberInsider, 2026-05-23). Charter did not disclose how the attackers gained access, and CyberInsider links the claim to the ongoing campaign against Salesforce environments (CyberInsider, 2026-05-23). Troy Hunt's weekly update of 2026-05-24 relays a post listing Charter among three new ShinyHunters claims (Troy Hunt, 2026-05-24).

    Correction21 Oct 2026 20:33 UTCrun 2026-10-21T1148Z-auditbodyclassificationentitiesevidenceheadlinepriorityregionssectorssourcessourcing_notesummarytechniquestitle

    7-Eleven confirmed a breach of systems storing franchisee documents, not ShinyHunters' claim of 600,000+ Salesforce records: its notification letters date the access to 2026-04-08, the filings in Massachusetts, Maine and Vermont count 47, 2 and 1 affected people, and 7-Eleven did not respond when asked about the 600,000-record claim (Security Affairs, 2026-05-18; Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-05-20). SecurityWeek lists Instructure, Vimeo, Wynn Resorts, Vercel and Medtronic as recent claims by ShinyHunters and affiliated actors and describes the group's Salesforce intrusions since mid-2025 as phishing, third-party-integration abuse or misconfigurations; it does not place those victims in one campaign with 7-Eleven or name a vector for the 7-Eleven breach (SecurityWeek, 2026-05-18). Likewise, Charter did not disclose how the attackers got in, and CyberInsider only links the Charter claim to the Salesforce-focused campaign (CyberInsider, 2026-05-23). CyberInsider later updated its report: the attackers leaked the Charter dataset publicly, and Have I Been Pwned counts 4.9 million unique email addresses with names, phone numbers and physical addresses (CyberInsider, 2026-05-23).
