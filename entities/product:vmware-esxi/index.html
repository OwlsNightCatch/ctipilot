    2026-11-20HIGHexploitedvCenter auth bypass exploited since September; Metasploit module in public review: patch to 9.1.2, 9.0.2.0300 or 8.0 U3m

    Defender takeaway

    Treat the update as an emergency change and move 9.x vCenter straight to 9.1.2 or 9.0.2.0300, which cover this flaw and the second bypass; until then GTIG's hardening guidance drops inbound TCP 2012 from non-allowlisted addresses, tested first because the port carries SSO traffic (GTIG, 2026-11-19; Broadcom, 2026-11-24).

    Exposure · triage · detection
    Exposure

    VCenter 7.0, 8.0, 9.0 and 9.1 before the fixed builds; the prerequisites are network access to TCP 2012 and a valid SSO principal name, and GTIG's actor reached vCenter from an internal Linux appliance, so exposure does not require an internet-facing vCenter (Rapid7, 2026-11-24; GTIG, 2026-11-19).

    Triage

    A connection to TCP 2012 or an administrator SRP login alone proves nothing; the signal is the call pattern and log sequence above (Rapid7, 2026-11-24).

    Detection

    In network telemetry, repeated calls to operation 1 of the SRP helper interface on TCP 2012 with no RPC authentication verifier and context UUIDs that differ mainly in their timestamp fields (Rapid7, 2026-11-24), and sweeps of ports 2012, 5480 and 9080 from a Linux appliance that is not a management host (GTIG, 2026-11-19); in appliance logs, a database-transfer grant in the vmdird log, the SSH-settings request and a new local administrator in the vAPI endpoint access log and the SSH enablement itself in the appliance management log, then an SSH login followed by sudo (Rapid7, 2026-11-24); on ESXi, vpxuser or root logins, a new authorized_keys entry and a hidden directory on a VMFS volume (GTIG, 2026-11-19). A forged SAML session looks like an administrator session, and because BONESAW extracts the SAML signing key the compromise check covers SSO and local administrators, signing material and ESXi keys from early September on (GTIG, 2026-11-19).

    2026-11-25HIGHBroadcom VMSA-2026-0011: second vCenter auth bypass and ESX I/O filter RCE, all 9.8; the earlier fix does not cover 9.x

    Defender takeaway

    Treat it as the emergency change Broadcom describes: move vCenter and ESX to the builds above, re-patch any 9.x vCenter that stopped at the earlier advisory's build, and keep management interfaces and the ESX I/O filter port off untrusted networks until then; vCenter 7.0 and ESX 7.0 have no public fix and Broadcom directs extended-support customers to its support, so isolation is the interim lever there.

    Exposure · detection
    Exposure

    Any vCenter Server reachable over the network, with the build shown in the appliance's version summary, and any ESX host whose I/O filter service port is reachable; on ESX 8.x that service listens on TCP 9080 and KB463535 says only vCenter should reach it.

    Detection

    Broadcom publishes no indicators. Flows to TCP 9080 on ESX hosts from any address other than the vCenter Server are outside the pattern KB463535 describes, and administrator sessions or account changes on a vCenter that was reachable before patching deserve the review described for the earlier Directory Service bypass.

    2026-11-14NOTABLEKaspersky: BlackNevas used vCenter Host Profiles to enable SSH on ESXi, destroyed backups first, then wiped datastores

    Defender takeaway

    Treat the vCenter appliances as the control plane to protect: alert on Host Profile changes to TSM-SSH outside change windows and on SSH from vCenter to ESXi that follows them, review VMware-looking services on the appliances against a trusted list, and keep backup-platform credentials and administration separate from the systems that can reach vCenter, because the backup environment was destroyed before the hosts (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13).

    Exposure · triage · detection
    Exposure

    A vCenter and ESXi estate where accounts from systems outside the virtualisation layer can reach the vCenter appliances and where Host Profile remediation can change the SSH service state; the article names no version (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13).

    Triage

    Each Host Profile step is legitimate alone, so the distinguishing context is authorisation, timing, source account, change window and whether the desired state weakens security; a VMware-looking service is not malicious by default, since custom integrations exist, but one absent from the trusted list is a high-confidence anomaly, and chmod +x is ambiguous alone while execution of a newly staged executable from /tmp is itself a strong indicator (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13).

    Detection

    In vCenter events and tasks and in vpxa.log and vobd.log, Host Profile remediation that changes TSM or TSM-SSH from policy-disabled to running outside an approved change window, which Kaspersky rates critical when an SSH session from vCenter or another management appliance to the same host follows within 15 minutes; on the vCenter appliance, a new VMware-looking systemd unit absent from the organisation's list of trusted services; in ESXi shell logs, execution of a newly staged executable under /tmp followed by access to /vmfs; and preserved hostprofiletrace.log (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13). ESXi logs sit in a ramdisk that is cleared at reboot, so Kaspersky says not to begin by rebooting a host (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13).

    2026-11-11NOTABLEInsikt: GhostFrame implant re-disables ESXi execInstalledOnly every five seconds and steals guest credentials

    Defender takeaway

    Treat a VPN-appliance compromise as a possible hypervisor compromise and check the ESXi persistence files and the execInstalledOnly state before declaring the incident closed; restrict ESXi and vCenter management to dedicated administration hosts.

    Exposure · triage · detection
    Exposure

    ESXi hosts whose root credentials, SSH or shell, or vCenter are reachable after an edge compromise; no ESXi vulnerability is involved, so patching ESXi does not address it, and an estate that runs both a SonicWall, Palo Alto Networks or Fortinet VPN appliance and ESXi without isolated hypervisor management is in the pattern Insikt describes.

    Triage

    Administrators legitimately edit local.sh, create snapshots and use VNC; the discriminators are a firewall-off and execInstalledOnly loop, a /opt/vmhostd-core file or vmhostd process where the genuine daemon is vmware-hostd, and snapshot-file reads by a non-backup account.

    Detection

    In ESXi file-integrity and shell-audit telemetry, changes to local.sh, a process masquerading as the host daemon or an unexpected executable under /opt, and repeated firewall-off and execInstalledOnly resets; in network telemetry, six near-simultaneous SYNs from the hypervisor management address to one destination on those ports, and any outbound command traffic from a hypervisor; in vCenter and ESXi logs, snapshot creation or deletion, VNC enablement and power-state changes outside change windows; in datastore telemetry, reads of VM memory-snapshot and virtual-disk files by an identity that is not the backup service.

    2026-11-07NOTABLEKaspersky: ESXi and Proxmox ransomware ran on a monitoring server's root password and admin notes, no VM escape

    Defender takeaway

    Give each monitoring platform its own least-privilege identity instead of ESXi root, replace stored root passwords with vault retrieval or limited tokens, administer ESXi through vCenter with Lockdown Mode and SSH stopped by default, and keep logs off the hosts so a compromised administrator cannot erase them (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-06).

    Exposure · detection
    Exposure

    A virtualisation estate where a monitoring, backup or management server stores or can use hypervisor root credentials, where one root password is shared across hosts, where SSH to ESXi is reachable from general server networks, or where administrators keep Proxmox or ESXi credentials in plain-text notes; PRTG, which supports storing VMware credentials at device or group level, is the product Kaspersky names (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-06).

    Detection

    On ESXi, root SSH logins from addresses that do not normally administer the hosts, a file upload followed by a failed execution of a non-installed file, a change to the execInstalledOnly setting and cleared logs; on Proxmox, root@pam requests to the VNC web socket used to reach other hosts, as the log records Kaspersky shows (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-06). Kaspersky advises alerting when execInstalledOnly, Secure Boot enforcement, Lockdown Mode, SSH, local accounts or remote logging settings change (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-06).

    2026-11-06NOTABLEQilin's October build spreads through vCenter and ESXi, reboots into Safe Mode and prints notes on office printers

    Defender takeaway

    Treat HA or DRS being turned off in vCenter, followed by SSH sessions to ESXi hosts, as an incident, and alert on a Safe Mode boot configured by bcdedit outside change windows, which Seqrite names as a detection point (Seqrite Labs, 2026-11-03).

    Exposure · triage · detection
    Exposure

    Windows estates with a vCenter and ESXi hosts that a domain-joined host can reach over the network and SSH, since the spreading engine needs a vCenter login and SSH to the hosts; Seqrite names no initial-access route.

    Triage

    The Restart Manager and a DISM-installed RSAT component are normal in patching and administration, so each is weak alone; the combination with the print fan-out, the Safe Mode change and the vCenter actions is the signal.

    Detection

    In vCenter audit events, HA or DRS being disabled on a cluster followed by SSH sessions to ESXi hosts and esxcli activity; in process-creation and registry telemetry, bcdedit setting a Safe Mode boot and a Winlogon AutoAdminLogon change outside a change window, DISM installing the RSAT Active Directory component on a host that never had it, and rstrtmgr.dll loaded by a process that is not an installer; in network telemetry, one host sending to TCP 9100 on many hosts of a subnet; and an executable named resmon.exe outside System32 (Seqrite Labs, 2026-11-03).

    CVE-2026-123890, VMware vCenter Server: an unauthenticated authentication bypass in the Directory Service gives an administrator session; GTIG reports the suspected China-nexus actor ARMORY CASTLE exploited it from early September, Broadcom lists no workaround; first fixed in 8.0 U3m, 9.0.2.0200 and 9.1.1.0200, with 9.1.2 and 9.0.2.0300 now superseding the 9.x builds (CVSS 3.1 9.8)

    Broadcom's advisory VMSA-2026-0009 of 2026-11-19 fixes CVE-2026-123890, an authentication bypass in the VMware Directory Service of vCenter that needs only network access (CVSS 3.1 9.8), with no workaround; it does not state exploitation (Broadcom, 2026-11-19; Broadcom, 2026-11-19). Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant say ARMORY CASTLE, a suspected PRC-nexus actor, exploited it from early September 2026 with its BONESAW utility, in one intrusion (GTIG, 2026-11-19); BleepingComputer notes Broadcom has not confirmed it (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-19), and CISA listed the flaw as exploited on 2026-11-20 (CISA, 2026-11-20).

    The cause is a session-key disclosure over DCE/RPC in the rpc_srp_verifier service on TCP 2012: the SRP key getter checks neither that the caller authenticated nor that it owns the verifier, and the RPC context handles are predictable UUIDv1 values, so an attacker needs only network access to TCP 2012 and an existing SSO principal name (Rapid7, 2026-11-24). Rapid7 chained the administrator session to a root shell on vCenter 8.0.2.00400 and describes a Metasploit module for the whole chain that sits in an open public pull request (Rapid7, 2026-11-24; Metasploit pull request, 2026-11-20). In GTIG's intrusion the actor forged administrator SAML assertions after extracting the SAML signing key, switched SSH on through the appliance management API and reached ESXi hosts, where it planted an authorized_keys entry and extracted domain data from Windows guest disks (GTIG, 2026-11-19).

    Fixes for this flaw: vCenter 9.1.1.0200, 9.0.2.0200 and 8.0 U3m and VMware Cloud Foundation 5.2.5; vCenter 7.0 has no public fix (Broadcom, 2026-11-19). VMSA-2026-0011 of 2026-11-24 fixes a second Directory Service bypass, CVE-2026-123903, in 9.1.2 and 9.0.2.0300, so 9.1.1.0200 and 9.0.2.0200 leave it open while 8.0 U3m and 5.2.5 cover both (Broadcom, 2026-11-24).

    Detection
    In network telemetry, repeated calls to operation 1 of the SRP helper interface on TCP 2012 with no RPC authentication verifier and context UUIDs that differ mainly in their timestamp fields (Rapid7, 2026-11-24), and sweeps of ports 2012, 5480 and 9080 from a Linux appliance that is not a management host (GTIG, 2026-11-19); in appliance logs, a database-transfer grant in the vmdird log, the SSH-settings request and a new local administrator in the vAPI endpoint access log and the SSH enablement itself in the appliance management log, then an SSH login followed by sudo (Rapid7, 2026-11-24); on ESXi, vpxuser or root logins, a new authorized_keys entry and a hidden directory on a VMFS volume (GTIG, 2026-11-19). No virtualisation-layer vulnerability was exploited: credentials obtained from non-virtualisation systems inside the perimeter reached the appliances, and every action against ESXi ran through legitimate VMware management interfaces (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13). The activity is attributed to BlackNevas through the ransom notes (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13).

Payloads went to ESXi over SFTP, a self-test of the executable ran about three hours before the final detonation, and about the first 2 KB of the virtual disk files were overwritten (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13).     A vCenter and ESXi estate where accounts from systems outside the virtualisation layer can reach the vCenter appliances and where Host Profile remediation can change the SSH service state; the article names no version (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13).

    In vCenter events and tasks and in vpxa.log and vobd.log, Host Profile remediation that changes TSM or TSM-SSH from policy-disabled to running outside an approved change window, which Kaspersky rates critical when an SSH session from vCenter or another management appliance to the same host follows within 15 minutes; on the vCenter appliance, a new VMware-looking systemd unit absent from the organisation's list of trusted services; in ESXi shell logs, execution of a newly staged executable under /tmp followed by access to /vmfs; and preserved hostprofiletrace.log (Kaspersky GERT, 2026-11-13).
