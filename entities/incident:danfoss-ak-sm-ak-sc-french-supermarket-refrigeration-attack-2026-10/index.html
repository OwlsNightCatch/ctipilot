    Defender takeaway

    Take any Danfoss AK-SM or AK-SC, Copeland xweb or Dixell xweb management interface off the internet now and restore access only through a secure VPN or protected network, as CERT-FR advises (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13), and ask the contractor whether its own remote access was affected; for the AK-SM800 and AK-SC355, replacement is the lever, since Danfoss says it had long advised supermarkets to move off those models (AFP, 2026-10-12), no fix exists for them according to NCSC Switzerland (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13), and CERT-FR says a factory reset on an unpatched end-of-life unit removes the compromised instance's persistence but leaves the device vulnerable and almost certainly compromised again (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13).

    Exposure · triage · detection
    Exposure

    A Danfoss AK-SM or AK-SC refrigeration controller, or a Copeland or Dixell xweb unit, whose management interface is reachable from the internet, above all legacy units; Danfoss says the current AK-SM800A units that were affected ran outdated firmware (Cooling Post, 2026-10-12), and CERT-FR names no models, flaw or entry vector for the Copeland and Dixell units (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13). Copeland first declined to confirm CERT-FR's finding and has since acknowledged a vulnerability, which, Cybersecurity Dive reports, a Copeland spokesperson confirmed was found in discontinued end-of-life controllers, and says it is working with impacted customers to mitigate the risk; neither article names the flaw (SecurityWeek, 2026-10-15; Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-10-15). Infosecurity reports, without naming them, that supermarkets in Belgium and the Netherlands were also reportedly affected (Infosecurity Magazine, 2026-10-12); Blick reports that Migros confirms using, in a limited number of its Swiss stores, the same Danfoss refrigeration systems that were targeted and says nothing indicates an attack on them, while Aldi and Lidl say they are not concerned and Coop says it was not affected and declines to say whether it uses Danfoss (Blick, 2026-10-13); no source reports a Swiss victim, so ask whoever manages your refrigeration and building services whether a controller sits in your estate. For an AK-SM 800A, Censys maps the firmware to the published flaws: a build below 4.2 carries all three, 4.2.4 fixes only the date-based login and so still carries the two later ones, and 4.3.1 or later fixes all three, with 5.3.2 the current build it lists (Censys, 2026-10-14). For the Copeland and Dixell units, CERT-FR links a Dixell France guide (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16) that carries Copeland's own bulletin of 14 October, in which Copeland acknowledges the vulnerability on a generation of XWEB EVO controllers that is no longer marketed and has reached end of life, says to remove every XWEB EVO from the public internet immediately and says XWEB EVO no longer receives security patches, and that adds Dixell France's own recommendations, among them that XWEB PRO and EVO ship with the same factory Admin account (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). The bulletin names the affected generation but describes no flaw (Dixell France, 2026-10-14).

    Triage

    A defrost cycle is normal; BFMTV reports, citing sector professionals, that installations can be programmed for two hours of defrost per 24 hours, whereas the reported attack is said to be a continuous forced defrost at a 50-degree setpoint held for hours (translated from French) (BFMTV, 2026-10-12).

    Detection

    On the controller, defrost, setpoint and account changes outside a maintenance window; in firewall, provider VPN or ISP logs, inbound connections to the management interface from addresses other than the refrigeration contractor's; CERT-FR accepts filtering to the contractors' source addresses only as an emergency measure and wants access restored through a secure VPN or protected network (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13). For a controller that may have been taken over, CERT-FR's current text is to remove the internet exposure, restart the Danfoss unit or otherwise restore it with a factory reset, restore access only through a secure VPN or protected network, reconfigure it and apply the maker's practices, which for Danfoss means updating the firmware, setting the security level to Strict and confirming the default passwords were changed (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16); for a unit not known to be compromised it asks to check the whole configuration, notably the defrost ranges (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). Le Monde reports that besides raising the defrost setpoint to 50 degrees the attackers lengthened the defrost operation from one hour to eight so that alarms trigger late (translated from French), which makes the defrost duration a tamper signal beside the setpoint (Le Monde, 2026-10-12; SecurityWeek, 2026-10-15).

    Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC, Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb refrigeration controllers compromised, with forced defrost reported at hundreds of French supermarkets; CERT-FR and NCSC Switzerland say to remove internet exposure

    Coopérative U, Intermarché and E. Leclerc confirmed to AFP a cyberattack on their refrigeration systems overnight on 11 to 12 October 2026; AFP reports from the retailers' statements at least 300 Intermarché or Netto shops and more than 100 Coopérative U outlets, and the Paris prosecutor opened an investigation (AFP, 2026-10-12). BFMTV puts the Intermarché and Netto count at 300, which it says is twice the first estimates of 150 points of sale, and Coopérative U below 100 stores, while Infosecurity quotes 150 Intermarché and 30 Netto stores (BFMTV, 2026-10-12; Infosecurity Magazine, 2026-10-12). BFMTV, writing in the conditional, reports that the attackers are said to have imposed a forced, continuous defrost with the setpoint at 50 degrees, on software older than version 4, heating the equipment for hours (translated from French) (BFMTV, 2026-10-12). CERT-FR's alert, in its revision of 2026-10-13, says it knows of numerous compromises of the remote refrigeration controllers Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC series as well as Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb, and that exposing a management interface to the internet is contrary to good practice (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13). Danfoss told AFP that most affected units are legacy products that no longer receive software or security updates, naming the AK-SM800 and AK-SC355 regulators, and a person close to the Prime Minister said ANSSI had identified a vulnerability in software from a Danish company and that other countries may be affected (AFP, 2026-10-12). Intermarché told BleepingComputer that the attack reportedly affected a service provider that manages refrigeration systems in stores (BleepingComputer, 2026-10-12), and E. Leclerc told AFP that a cyberattack affected one of its refrigeration equipment suppliers (AFP, 2026-10-12). Infosecurity says the affected stores include those running vulnerable consoles with remote control and no adequately secured communication channel (Infosecurity Magazine, 2026-10-12). No source names an actor or the flaw or states how the attackers got in, and none confirms a tie to the 2025 AK-SM 8xxA vulnerabilities that Claroty found: BleepingComputer reports no tie (BleepingComputer, 2026-10-12) and NCSC Switzerland lists three of them only as alleged to be potentially related (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13); Censys names the same three as flaws that Danfoss patched more than a year earlier and says Danfoss has not confirmed the initial access vector (Censys, 2026-10-14).

    CERT-FR revised its alert on 2026-10-13: the list of compromised remote controllers now includes Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb besides Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC, campaigns against these devices are probably still ongoing, and an unpatched end-of-life unit kept on the internet is very likely to be compromised, so a factory reset alone is insufficient (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13). NCSC Switzerland published advisory 13054 on 2026-10-13 with the exploitation status Actively Exploited. It lists the Danfoss AK-SM 800 (end of life, no longer patched), the AK-SC 355 (phased out, no longer patched) and the current AK-SM 800A series when it runs outdated firmware, says no fixes exist for the two older models and that Danfoss urges replacing them, and says the attack could affect other sectors, such as healthcare for specialized cooling equipment, so organisations should check whether affected devices are in use (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13). Danfoss told Cooling Post that the AK-SM800 was declared end of life in 2021, that the AK-SC355 was phased out in 2016, and that the affected AK-SM800A systems ran outdated firmware (Cooling Post, 2026-10-12). Blick reports that Migros uses the same Danfoss refrigeration systems in a limited number of Swiss stores and sees no sign they were targeted, and that Aldi, Lidl and Coop say they are not affected (Blick, 2026-10-13); no source reports a Swiss victim. The advisory relays a six-step CERT-FR sequence (limit access to trusted addresses, factory reset, apply all fixes, rotate passwords and secrets, review accounts, reconfigure) and lists filtering to trusted addresses as a temporary measure (NCSC Switzerland, 2026-10-13), whereas the revised CERT-FR page puts removal of internet exposure first, accepts source-address filtering only as an emergency stop-gap and lists no secret rotation or account review (CERT-FR, 2026-10-13).

    Improvement15 Oct 2026 03:49 UTCrun 2026-10-15T0256Z-intelsourcesevidencesourcing_notebody

    Censys, which scans for Danfoss StoreView control panels, says Danfoss has not confirmed the initial access vector and points to three AK-SM 800A flaws that Danfoss patched more than a year earlier: CVE-2025-41450 (March 2025), which Censys describes as letting an unauthenticated actor log in as an administrator with a code computed from the current calendar date, and CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452 (August 2025), which it says let authenticated users run commands and disrupt the management interface (Censys, 2026-10-14). Censys maps the firmware by build: below 4.2 carries all three, 4.2.4 fixes only the date-based login and still carries the two later ones, and 4.3.1 or later fixes all three (Censys, 2026-10-14). Its scan counted 1,825 exposed StoreView panels in France on 2026-10-11 and 537 on 2026-10-14, a fall of 71 percent, with builds below 4.2, build 4.2.4 and builds from 4.3.1 leaving at about the same rate, which it reads as operators taking controllers off the internet rather than patching them (Censys, 2026-10-14). Outside France the count fell from 1,166 to 966, and in ten of the twelve countries with the most exposed hosts more than 60 percent of the hosts run a build below 4.3.1, led by Poland at 81 percent and Romania at 80 percent (Censys, 2026-10-14).

    Update18 Oct 2026 03:33 UTCrun 2026-10-18T0256Z-intelsummaryactionssourcesevidencesourcing_notebody

    CERT-FR's alert now has a third version, dated 2026-10-16 and listed on the page as an update of the recommendations; the page does not show which sentences changed (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). Its current text separates the two cases. For every unit it asks to remove the internet exposure of the affected units and of any unit on a public IP address, and to check the insurance contract conditions before cutting the data uplink to the refrigeration contractor (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). For a unit not known to be compromised it then asks to check the whole configuration, notably the defrost ranges; for a compromised Danfoss unit it says to restart the equipment or otherwise restore it with a factory reset, and then to reconfigure it for service (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). In both cases access returns only through a secure VPN or protected network, with source-address filtering for the contractors an emergency measure and not a lasting one, and the maker's practices apply (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). The alert still says campaigns are probably ongoing, still names no flaw or entry vector, and gives the risk as not specified by the vendor (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16).

    The alert's documentation section links an operator guide from Dixell France (Copeland) (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16). The file at that address was replaced on 2026-10-18; its current text, described in the correction of 2026-10-19T04:06:00Z, is Copeland's bulletin of 14 October 2026 followed by Dixell France's own complement (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). That complement says XWEB PRO and EVO ship with the same factory account, Admin with the password Admin, advises keeping the original Admin password sealed or in a password manager and not using it daily, gives each user a named account with an expiry date for an external contractor's account, and requires remote access through an encrypted VPN, with no port forward or DMZ and no remote-control software such as AnyDesk or TeamViewer on a site PC that stays on (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). CERT-FR does not say how the attackers got in, and SecurityWeek writes that neither Danfoss nor French authorities have tied the attack to the flaws Claroty disclosed (CERT-FR, 2026-10-16; SecurityWeek, 2026-10-15).

    Correction19 Oct 2026 04:06 UTCrun 2026-10-19T0256Z-intelsummarysourcesevidencesourcing_notebody

    The Dixell France guide that CERT-FR links was replaced on 2026-10-18, the file's date on the vendor's server, and now opens with Copeland's own security bulletin dated 14 October 2026 (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). The earlier update section's statements about XWEB PRO firmware 1.12.1, a CISA advisory and a March 2024 bulletin are no longer on that page and are removed from the summary and the body. The bulletin acknowledges the vulnerability on a generation of XWEB EVO controllers that is no longer marketed and has reached end of life, says to remove every XWEB EVO from the public internet immediately and then look for suspicious activity from the local network, and says XWEB EVO no longer receives security patches, while XWEB PRO is the supported platform and must not be exposed to the internet either (Dixell France, 2026-10-14). For an XWEB EVO that may have been accessed it gives a procedure: isolate the unit, restore the latest backup made before the access or otherwise check the users and profiles, set new passwords, check the alarm categories and recipients with a test alarm, and restore the device parameters from backup files or, without one, read each device's parameters and check setpoints, alarm thresholds and defrost parameters; it also says to check the product temperatures in the cabinets directly, since altered setpoints or alarm thresholds can compromise food safety (Dixell France, 2026-10-14).

    Le Monde reports that besides raising the defrost temperature to 50 degrees the attackers lengthened the defrost operation from one hour to eight so that alarms trigger late, which delayed interventions and amplified the damage (translated from French) (Le Monde, 2026-10-12). SecurityWeek relays that detail and adds that the Paris prosecutor's office said the attack caused fires as well as food losses, that the Finance Minister put the number of affected supermarkets at about 700 of roughly 10,000, and that investigators are reportedly puzzled by the lack of a claim of responsibility or a ransom demand, citing Reuters (SecurityWeek, 2026-10-15). Cybersecurity Dive had already reported that a Copeland spokesperson confirmed the vulnerability was found in discontinued end-of-life controllers (Cybersecurity Dive, 2026-10-15).
