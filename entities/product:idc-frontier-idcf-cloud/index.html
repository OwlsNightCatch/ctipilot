    ROUTINEupdatedNATOA1

    IDC Frontier: ransomware stops four zones of IDCF Cloud for 495 companies and local governments; the provider now says its backups are safe and retrievable for 194 customers and that it found no trace of data taken from customers' virtual servers

    IDC Frontier says a ransomware attack that began around 3:40 a.m. on 2026-10-07 stopped four zones of IDCF Cloud East Japan Region 1, affecting 495 companies and local governments, among them the websites of Ibaraki Prefecture and the city of Kodaira, and has suspended customer management consoles in every region; no source names the actor, the ransomware family or the intrusion route (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-07; IDC Frontier, 2026-10-08; BleepingComputer, 2026-10-08; Jiji Press via nippon.com, 2026-10-07). The lesson is supplier concentration: local governments sat behind one provider whose recovery first depended on copies the customers held, until the fifth report made the provider's own snapshot backups retrievable for 194 of the 495.

    Exposure
    Customers of IDCF Cloud; IDC Frontier lists IDCF Cloud TypeS and its private-cloud offering as outside the incident (translated from Japanese) (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-08). For any other body, the condition is a website, mail or form service whose hosting and recovery copies depend on one provider.
    Defender takeaway
    Check where the recovery copies of hosted websites, mail, forms and line-of-business systems live: IDC Frontier first expected restoration only from copies customers hold themselves (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-08), and its own snapshot backups became retrievable for 194 of the 495 customers only a week into the outage (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-14); the attacker's console message claims it wiped 554,153 snapshots, which the provider's report does not confirm (BleepingComputer, 2026-10-08). A copy in the provider's own environment is not one to count on for the recovery window.
    Update16 Oct 2026 04:12 UTCrun 2026-10-16T0256Z-inteltitleheadlinesummarysourcesevidencesourcing_notebody

    IDC Frontier's fifth report of 2026-10-14 says that, with an external security firm, it confirmed the backup data is safe, so the 194 of the 495 affected customers that used its snapshot function can retrieve it, and that it found no trace of customer data in the affected zones being queried or carried out, so it judges that none left those servers, while confirming improper queries of the registration data of 48 customers in its cloud portal with no trace of it being taken (translated from Japanese; IDC Frontier, 2026-10-14). This replaces the third report's expectation that data could be restored only from customers' own backups; the report does not state the intrusion route.
