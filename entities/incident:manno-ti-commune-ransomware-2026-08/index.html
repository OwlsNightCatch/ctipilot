    Swiss commune Manno (TI) confirms a cyberattack that encrypted part of its servers on 4 August 2026; SafePay has now published about 30 GB, which Risikomonitor reads as social-assistance decisions with AHV numbers, naturalisation files and a near-complete address book

    The Ticino commune of Manno stated in a signed notice dated 2026-09-16 that on 4 August 2026 part of its servers suffered an attack that encrypted data (Comune di Manno, 2026-09-16). The commune isolated the affected server immediately and, per the notice, restored data and operations from existing backups in the course of the afternoon (Comune di Manno, 2026-09-16). It reported the case to the competent authorities, filed a criminal complaint against persons unknown, and told residents to distrust unexpected letters or e-mails that demand urgent payments or personal or financial data and to verify through official channels (Comune di Manno, 2026-09-16). The notice names no actor, no intrusion vector, and does not say whether data left the network; Inside IT's article of 2026-09-29 calls it a ransomware attack and says a backup allowed a quick return to normal operation (Inside IT, 2026-09-29).

    On 2026-09-28, 55 days after the encryption, Ransomware.live recorded that the ransomware group SafePay had listed manno.ch on its leak site (Ransomware.live, 2026-09-28). Inside IT reported on 2026-10-27 that SafePay has put the stolen data into the darknet, about 30 GB in the case of Manno, and that the security firm Risikomonitor analysed it (Inside IT, 2026-10-27). Risikomonitor's reading, relayed by Inside IT and not verified independently: files with about 5,300 e-mail addresses and more than 2,000 identifiable persons; social-assistance decisions with name, address, monthly amount, AHV numbers and health insurer from 2014 to December 2025; debt-enforcement register extracts, naturalisation dossiers from 2018 and payment orders against tax debtors; employees' bank statements with IBANs; and mass letters, a youth-protection survey and a list of property owners with parcel and water-meter numbers that together give a near-complete address book of a commune of 1,381 inhabitants (Inside IT, 2026-10-27). Asked by Inside IT, the commune said it will make no statement on the publication (Inside IT, 2026-10-27).

    Defender takeaway
    A clean restore did not close the data-theft question here: the leak-site listing appeared 55 days after the encryption and the data has since been published, so a communal response to an encryption attack should keep its data-theft assessment open and plan for notification of affected persons, residents on social assistance first, and for the fraud that published AHV numbers, addresses and payment histories enable. The resident-fraud warning the commune issued belongs in the same response plan.
