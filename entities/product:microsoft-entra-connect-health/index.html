    NOTABLENATOB2

    Microsoft Entra Connect Health: an API flaw present from 2026-07-23 was exploited to read tenant metadata including administrator and technical-contact email addresses; Microsoft fixed it server-side on 2026-11-19 and warns of targeted phishing

    Microsoft told tenants through Azure Service Health, from 2026-11-20, that a vulnerability in the Microsoft Entra Connect Health API, introduced on 2026-07-23 and found on 2026-11-09, was exploited for unauthorized read-only access to tenant-level metadata (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-21). Connect Health is the cloud service that lets administrators monitor the health of their on-premises identity infrastructure through the Entra admin center (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-21). Microsoft deployed a server-side fix on 2026-11-19, concluded its investigation on 2026-11-20 and found no unauthorized configuration change, although the flaw could in principle also have changed Connect Health monitoring settings (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-21; Netzwoche, 2026-11-23).

    The exposed data are the email addresses of tenant technical contacts and administrator accounts, tenant IDs, display names, initial domain names, country and region codes, Entra licence tiers and directory-synchronisation status, Connect Health onboarding status, and an expired agent-registration certificate for an internal Microsoft service decommissioned in late 2024; Microsoft says passwords, password hashes, authentication tokens, valid cryptographic keys, on-premises server and Active Directory topology and Azure subscription data were not exposed (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-21). Microsoft has not said who exploited the flaw, how many tenants are affected or when the attacks began; more than a dozen administrators reported receiving the notice, and some managed service providers received notices for several customer tenants (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-21).

    Exposure
    Any tenant that received the Azure Service Health notification; Microsoft says the flaw needs no software update or other customer remediation (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-21).
    Detection
    The leaked data cannot be recalled, so the signal is the follow-on: mail-flow and helpdesk telemetry for phishing or social-engineering messages aimed at the exposed administrator and technical-contact addresses, and Entra sign-in and audit logs for privileged accounts and for changes to Connect Health settings; Microsoft advises reviewing the Connect Health configuration and the Entra audit and sign-in logs, and its PowerShell script shows the current values of the exposed metadata types, not what was accessed (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-21).
    Defender takeaway
    Treat the exposed administrator and technical-contact addresses as targets of tailored phishing and social engineering, tell those people, and enforce phishing-resistant MFA on privileged accounts; there is nothing to patch (BleepingComputer, 2026-11-21).
