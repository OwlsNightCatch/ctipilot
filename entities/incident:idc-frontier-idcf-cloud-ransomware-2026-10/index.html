    IDC Frontier IDCF Cloud ransomware attack (October 2026)

    incident · incident:idc-frontier-idcf-cloud-ransomware-2026-10 single-source-victim

    Ransomware attack from about 3:40 a.m. on 2026-10-07 that stopped four zones of the SoftBank Group subsidiary IDC Frontier's IDCF Cloud East Japan Region 1 for 495 companies and local governments, with the provider saying customer data in those zones can be restored only from customers' own backups and suspending customer consoles in every region; the intrusion route and the actor are not public (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-08; BleepingComputer, 2026-10-08; Jiji Press, 2026-10-07).

    Defender takeaway

    Check where the recovery copies of hosted websites, mail, forms and line-of-business systems live: IDC Frontier first expected restoration only from copies customers hold themselves (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-08), and its own snapshot backups became retrievable for 194 of the 495 customers only a week into the outage (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-14), and for 206 by 2026-10-20 (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-27); the attacker's console message claims it wiped 554,153 snapshots, which the provider's report does not confirm (BleepingComputer, 2026-10-08). A copy in the provider's own environment is not one to count on for the recovery window. Ask a hosting or cloud supplier in writing how its customer portal authenticates and whether the open-source components behind that login are inventoried and patched, because here the path to the virtualisation platform ran from the portal's login system through the management console; the supplier's own remediation, a rebuilt portal, an external identity provider, access limited to customer IP addresses with mandatory multi-factor authentication and a company-wide software inventory matched automatically against vulnerability data, is the checklist (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-27).

    Entries about IDC Frontier IDCF Cloud ransomware attack (October 2026) (1)

    ROUTINEupdatedNATOA2

    IDC Frontier: ransomware stops four zones of IDCF Cloud for 495 companies and local governments; the provider's seventh report traces it to an open-source flaw in the customer portal's authentication system and says no server data left

    IDC Frontier says a ransomware attack that began around 3:40 a.m. on 2026-10-07 stopped four zones of IDCF Cloud East Japan Region 1, affecting 495 companies and local governments, and has suspended customer management consoles in every region; no source names the actor or the ransomware family (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-07; IDC Frontier, 2026-10-08; BleepingComputer, 2026-10-08). The provider's seventh report of 2026-10-27 gives the route: a flaw in open-source software used in the customer portal's authentication system was exploited, and the attacker then obtained administrator rights on the virtualisation platform through the management console, stopped customers' virtual servers and encrypted them; the software is not named (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-27). The lesson is supplier concentration: local governments sat behind one provider whose recovery first depended on copies the customers held, until the fifth report made the provider's own snapshot backups retrievable for 194 of the 495 (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-14) and for 206 by 2026-10-20 (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-27).

    Exposure
    Customers of IDCF Cloud; IDC Frontier lists IDCF Cloud TypeS and its private-cloud offering as outside the incident (translated from Japanese) (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-08). For any other body, the condition is a website, mail or form service whose hosting and recovery copies depend on one provider.
    Defender takeaway
    Check where the recovery copies of hosted websites, mail, forms and line-of-business systems live: IDC Frontier first expected restoration only from copies customers hold themselves (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-08), and its own snapshot backups became retrievable for 194 of the 495 customers only a week into the outage (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-14), and for 206 by 2026-10-20 (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-27); the attacker's console message claims it wiped 554,153 snapshots, which the provider's report does not confirm (BleepingComputer, 2026-10-08). A copy in the provider's own environment is not one to count on for the recovery window. Ask a hosting or cloud supplier in writing how its customer portal authenticates and whether the open-source components behind that login are inventoried and patched, because here the path to the virtualisation platform ran from the portal's login system through the management console; the supplier's own remediation, a rebuilt portal, an external identity provider, access limited to customer IP addresses with mandatory multi-factor authentication and a company-wide software inventory matched automatically against vulnerability data, is the checklist (IDC Frontier, 2026-10-27).
    Update16 Oct 2026 04:12 UTCrun 2026-10-16T0256Z-inteltitleheadlinesummarysourcesevidencesourcing_notebody

    IDC Frontier's fifth report of 2026-10-14 says that, with an external security firm, it confirmed the backup data is safe, so the 194 of the 495 affected customers that used its snapshot function can retrieve it, and that it found no trace of customer data in the affected zones being queried or carried out, so it judges that none left those servers, while confirming improper queries of the registration data of 48 customers in its cloud portal with no trace of it being taken (translated from Japanese; IDC Frontier, 2026-10-14). This replaces the third report's expectation that data could be restored only from customers' own backups; the report does not state the intrusion route.

    Update28 Oct 2026 03:44 UTCrun 2026-10-28T0256Z-inteltitleheadlinesummarytechniquessourcesevidencesourcing_notebodyclassificationverification

    IDC Frontier's seventh report of 2026-10-27 says its investigation with an external security firm is complete and names the cause: a third party exploited a vulnerability in open-source software used in the authentication system of the IDCF Cloud Portal, the portal customers use to start virtual servers and check billing, and so entered that system (translated from Japanese; IDC Frontier, 2026-10-27). The attacker then obtained administrator rights on the virtualisation platform through the management console, stopped customers' virtual servers and encrypted them; the software is not named. The provider finds no trace of customer data leaving the virtual servers, says the improper queries of portal registration data reached up to 71 customers, up from 48, that up to four customer login passwords were improperly changed and have been reset, and that backups are retrievable for 206 customers. It is rebuilding the portal in a new environment, replacing the affected authentication system with an external identity provider and limiting portal access to customer IP addresses with mandatory multi-factor authentication.
