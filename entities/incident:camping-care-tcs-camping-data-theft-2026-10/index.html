    For a booking, ticketing or citizen-portal application that a supplier runs on PHP, ask the supplier in writing whether its runtime was updated to 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35 (CISA, 2026-10-26) and whether customer data was accessed before that, because SRF, Blick and a TCS spokesperson tie this theft to the PHP flaws (SRF, 2026-10-26; Blick, 2026-10-26; watson.ch, 2026-10-26), although a customer's notice says the supplier is still investigating the cause (Campingplatz Hüttenberg, 2026-10-23) and no source says how the intrusion happened; and tell the people whose data the supplier holds that a message quoting their real booking details is no proof that it comes from you.

    NOTABLEupdatedNATOB2

    Camping.Care booking system (TCS Camping): customer and booking data stolen in connection with the PHP flaws, with real reservation numbers already used in WhatsApp fraud and 15,000 guests warned as a precaution

    TCS Camping is warning about 15,000 guests as a precaution because the provider of its booking system, Camping.Care, suffered a data theft; TCS confirmed to the SRF consumer programme Espresso on 2026-10-26 that fraudsters could steal customer and booking information, and SRF reports the case in connection with the worldwide PHP security hole (SRF, 2026-10-26). Blick describes Camping.Care as a Dutch company whose booking system handles reservations for all campsites of the Touring Club Schweiz, writes, citing SRF, that the hackers used the critical PHP flaw to steal customer and booking data and that Camping.Care is not the only software supplier hit (Blick, 2026-10-26). No source names the CVE identifiers, the entry point, the date of the intrusion or an actor, and TCS says the hole has since been closed (SRF, 2026-10-26). The stolen data already in criminal use includes, among other data, names, telephone numbers and reservation numbers: guests received fraudulent WhatsApp messages that refer to existing reservations (SRF, 2026-10-26). TCS says account details and IBANs were not affected and that credit card data is not stored in the booking system (SRF, 2026-10-26). A second customer, Campingplatz Hüttenberg in canton Thurgau, told its guests in a notice (page date 2026-10-23) that Camping.care had informed it of WhatsApp messages to guests of several campsites that contain real booking information and a link, that Camping.care is investigating the cause and that booking data must be assumed to have been used without authorisation; the notice does not mention PHP, and Netzwoche points out that it does not say whether the attackers used one of the PHP flaws (Campingplatz Hüttenberg, 2026-10-23; Netzwoche, 2026-10-27).

    Exposure
    The guests who booked through the TCS campsite booking system, about 15,000 of whom TCS has warned, and any other customer of the Camping.Care platform, one of which, Campingplatz Hüttenberg, has warned its own guests; Blick says Camping.Care is not the only software supplier hit (Blick, 2026-10-26; Netzwoche, 2026-10-27).
    Detection
    The only signal the sources describe is on the recipient side, unsolicited WhatsApp messages that quote a real reservation with the correct name, telephone number and reservation number; no source gives intrusion telemetry from the supplier's systems (SRF, 2026-10-26).

    Triage: TCS Camping sends no automated booking details, payment requests or reservation links over WhatsApp, so a WhatsApp message of that kind that quotes a genuine reservation is the fraud, however accurate its details (SRF, 2026-10-26).

    Defender takeaway
    For a booking, ticketing or citizen-portal application that a supplier runs on PHP, ask the supplier in writing whether its runtime was updated to 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35 (CISA, 2026-10-26) and whether customer data was accessed before that, because SRF, Blick and a TCS spokesperson tie this theft to the PHP flaws (SRF, 2026-10-26; Blick, 2026-10-26; watson.ch, 2026-10-26), although a customer's notice says the supplier is still investigating the cause (Campingplatz Hüttenberg, 2026-10-23) and no source says how the intrusion happened; and tell the people whose data the supplier holds that a message quoting their real booking details is no proof that it comes from you.
    Update28 Oct 2026 03:43 UTCrun 2026-10-28T0256Z-intelheadlinesummarysourcesevidencesourcing_notebody

    Netzwoche reported on 2026-10-27 that a second Swiss Camping.Care customer, Campingplatz Hüttenberg in canton Thurgau, has warned its guests of WhatsApp messages about existing bookings that contain real booking information and a link; the campsite's notice carries the page date 2026-10-23 (Netzwoche, 2026-10-27; Campingplatz Hüttenberg, 2026-10-23). The notice says Camping.care informed the campsite of the messages, that Camping.care is investigating the cause and that booking data must be assumed to have been used without authorisation; it does not mention PHP, and Netzwoche points out that it does not say whether the attackers used one of the PHP flaws. The link to the PHP flaws therefore rests on SRF, Blick and a TCS spokesperson's statement to Keystone-SDA (watson.ch, 2026-10-26), not on the supplier.
