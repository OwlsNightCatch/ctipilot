    B1ack's Stash carding marketplace releases about 4.6 million stolen payment card records for free

    SOCRadar reported on 2026-05-18 that B1ack's Stash, a dark-web carding marketplace operating since at least 2023, suspended about 8 million stolen CVV2 records from its inventory because sellers had resold its cards in competing shops, and released about 4.6 million of them as a free download (SOCRadar, 2026-05-18). Each record holds the full card number, expiry date, CVV2, cardholder name, billing address, e-mail, phone number and IP address; SOCRadar assesses that the data likely comes from e-commerce skimming or phishing operations, and about 70% of the records belong to US cardholders, followed by Canada, the United Kingdom, France and Malaysia (SOCRadar, 2026-05-18). After SOCRadar filtered out expired and duplicate records, roughly 4.3 million appear fresh and usable, and the marketplace has used free releases as marketing before, one million cards in April 2024 and over four million records in February 2025 (Security Affairs, 2026-05-20).

    Exposure
    Card issuers and merchants whose cardholders appear in the dump, overwhelmingly in the US; Europe appears mainly through UK and French cards (SOCRadar, 2026-05-18).
    Detection
    Payment fraud telemetry: SOCRadar expects card-not-present fraud to accelerate in the weeks after the release, and complete billing addresses make address verification easy to pass (SOCRadar, 2026-05-18).
    Defender takeaway
    For card-fraud teams the decision is SOCRadar's recommendation to review card-not-present fraud thresholds for transactions whose billing details match the dataset's patterns (SOCRadar, 2026-05-18); organizations without a card portfolio or merchant estate have no decision to make.
